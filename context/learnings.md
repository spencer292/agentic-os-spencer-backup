### What works well
- 2026-08-11: **When two systems disagree on a number, reconcile the gap to zero before proposing a fix — the residue is where the real bug is.** Ninety showed 687 TMCP jobs against a live audit's 674 and a tag count of 653. Decomposing rather than picking a favourite: 687 − 26 archived jobs (a genuine bug) = 661, + 4 jobs starting after the week end (correct behaviour for a weekly snapshot) + ~9 created after the data pull (cache staleness) = 674. Only one of those three terms was a defect; stopping at "Ninety is wrong by 13" would have produced a fix for the wrong thing, and stopping at "they measure different things" would have missed the archived-job bug entirely. Cheap tell that the residue is structural not noisy: the discrepancy was **exactly 26 in every week pushed**, which also proved the week-over-week trend had never been wrong — only the level. **A constant offset across periods means a classification bug; a drifting one means a timing difference.**
- 2026-08-05: **Date-scope any conformance audit to when the standard took effect, or the number is fiction.** Measuring 138 jobs against territory grid v5 gave "tech matches 47%" and read like a broken process. Grid v5 only went live 2026-08-01; splitting at that date gave **80% after** vs 35% before — the low number was almost entirely jobs built correctly under the *previous* three-truck structure. Same session, same shape twice more: a "0/138 on the right day" result was a date-parsing bug of mine, and a TMCP cadence that looked like it contradicted the product promise turned out to be correct once the actual visit list was read instead of the recurrence rule. **Before reporting a process as broken, ask three questions: when did the rule start, is my parser right, and am I reading the summary field or the real records?** Two of the three headline findings this session would have been wrong without it — and a wrong finding sends someone to "fix" work that was already right.
- 2026-07-31: **Before interviewing someone about a gap, tabulate their stated asks against what the existing asset literally says — quote the word count.** Spencer listed six things his training needed ("how to park", "how to dress", …). Rather than open with questions, the audit put each ask beside its current coverage: parking = 6 words, dress = one line, "how to work" = absent entirely. That table did the persuading and made the interview immediately productive — he answered in concrete operating rules instead of re-explaining the problem. Related pattern: when a client has two training assets and one works, find out *why* structurally. Here the phone pack (role-play + scenarios + answer key + progress log) is a **program**; the field guide is a **reference document**. Same subject-matter quality, completely different outcomes — the fix was to stop deepening the document and build the program layer on top of it.
- 2026-07-28: **When a new tool feeds an existing pipeline, import that pipeline's parser and gate output on it — don't re-describe the format.** Building voice→Jobber notes, the tempting shape was "prompt an LLM to write notes in our style". Instead the Worker imports the *same* `parse-note.mjs` the report sync and scheduling engine already use, runs every generated note back through it, and shows the technician a per-field check before they can send. That makes drift structurally impossible (one definition of the grammar) and turns eval into a real test rather than a vibe check — 18/18 text cases and 6/6 audio cases assert against the live parser, so they'd fail the moment the app and the automation disagreed. Reusable rule: **the acceptance test for generated content should be the downstream consumer, not a human reading it.**
- 2026-07-28: **Verify a provider's key actually has quota before designing around it, and mine the real corpus before writing the prompt.** Two cheap checks that changed the build: (1) `OPENAI_API_KEY` is present in `.env` but 429s `insufficient_quota` on every call — presence ≠ working, so probe each key before it becomes an architectural assumption (Gemini took over both transcription and formatting). (2) Pulling 4,341 real notes first surfaced things no amount of reasoning would have: `1 mile`/`no mile` as live typos for "mole" (which speech-to-text makes *worse*), 41 distinct next-action phrasings, and four compass placements the parser silently drops. The prompt was written from that evidence, not from the documented grammar.
- 2026-07-26: **Model-routing config: read the resolution order from live docs, don't reason from the model name.** Auditing model tiering across this install, the load-bearing fact was invisible from the config files themselves — subagent `model:` frontmatter **defaults to `inherit`**, so a definition with a blank field silently bills at the session model (found `l2s-content-packager` inheriting). Full order: `CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL` env var > per-invocation param > frontmatter > session model. Consequence worth remembering: a blanket env-var floor *overrides* frontmatter, so it flattens per-agent tiering — explicit pins beat a global cap when you want design work high and grunt work low. Also: saved model policies go stale fast (the stored one named Opus 4.8 and didn't know Fable 5 existed) — re-verify model IDs/pricing against the `claude-api` skill before acting on a remembered routing rule.
- 2026-07-26: **Check the cron runtime before promising a scheduled change will take effect.** Shipped seven correct `model:` changes to cron jobs and told Spencer they'd apply on next fire — then found at wrap-up that the dispatcher had been `runtime: stopped / leaderState: absent` since ~7/21. Editing a cron file is not the same as the cron running. `bash scripts/status-crons.sh` costs one call; do it in the same turn as any cron edit, not at wrap-up.
- 2026-07-21: Email-requested infra change (Roy's agent asked Spencer to delete a DMARC record): verified every claim against live authoritative DNS (nslookup vs ns09/ns10.domaincontrol.com) before endorsing, and re-verified after Spencer's delete. Caught that the fix "he did" hadn't actually landed, confirmed the advice was sound (dup DMARC = both ignored), and validated keeping p=none because Jobber/CallRail send as got-moles.com. Pattern: any emailed request to change DNS/infra gets independently verified against live state first — phishing-shaped even when legit.
- 2026-07-20: Transcript-mining pipeline landed end-to-end in one session (Spencer: "Looks good"): CallRail API pull -> 4 parallel analysis agents over 203 transcripts -> ranked FAQ training doc -> role-play skill -> Voice Assist reconfig -> Jobber sync cron. - 2026-07-31: **A rule captured in `CLAUDE.local.md` reaches Claude and nobody else.** Spencer set the activity-code follow-up cadence on 2026-07-26 (M/A → this week, L/A → 1–2 weeks, N/A → ~2 weeks) and it went into the agent's config. - 2026-07-31: **Named a root cause before measuring attribution, and had to walk it back to Spencer.** Found the website form creating Jobber clients unconditionally, saw duplicates, and built a whole plan around the form being the primary duplicate source. Spencer then described what he actually sees ("CallRail creates a client, then another when I call back") and the data agreed with him: 80 of 97 duplicate groups are call-driven, only 17 are the form. Cheap check I skipped — cluster the duplicates by origin and by time-gap FIRST (the gap equalling call duration was the whole answer, and was one script away). Also: my first "the sync log clears our script" conclusion came from grepping 7 names — a real cross-reference of all 97 groups was the honest version of the same claim.
- 2026-07-31: **Silent failures are the actual disease on this install.** Three found in one session, all the same shape: the website form logged leads nobody was told about, the daily Visit Notes n8n workflow failed 7 nights running with no alert, and form spam (4 submissions from one bot since May) sat undetected in the client list. Anything unattended needs a failure path that reaches a human — a job that only reports on success is indistinguishable from a job that is dead.
- 2026-07-23: Email triage (skill gap - candidate ops-email-triage): Gmail-connector cloud routine processes a bounded chunk per run, so multi-hundred-thread backlogs need repeated RemoteTrigger run calls; Gmail search `label:Label_N` by internal ID can return empty even when labelIds prove the label - filter with has:userlabels / has:nouserlabels instead. ### tool-optimoroute

- 2026-07-26 (undocumented driver API): `update_drivers_parameters` (PLURAL) takes `{updates:[{driver:{serial:"<name>"}, date:"YYYY-MM-DD", enabled:bool}]}` — per-DATE, addressed by **serial** (externalId is blank on this account). It accepts `enabled` ONLY. `workTimeFrom`/`workTimeTo` exist solely on the SINGULAR `update_driver_parameters`, which requires `externalId` — so **the 10-hour cap is NOT settable by API until drivers have externalIds**, and every over-10h day has to be hand-corrected. ## General
- 2026-08-01: When the user says a task "should be simple" and keeps correcting the same class of
  error, stop solving the instance and build the measurement that implements their rule. Spencer's
  rule — "overflow goes to the closest tech, only the closest jobs" — was computable from data
  already in hand. Twenty round-trips of hand-moving zips preceded writing the twenty-line function
  that did it properly.
- 2026-08-04: **Two call-routing engines in series silently swallow calls.** CallRail's flow hands
  off to the softphone's flow, and ANY action the softphone takes — answering to run its own ring
  logic, an auto-greeting, voicemail — reads to CallRail as "call answered." CallRail then exits its
  flow, so its timeout and Voice Assist fallback never fire, and nothing errors: the call log shows
  `answered=true` and looks healthy. Debug rule: **whichever endpoint answers first wins**, so every
  downstream voicemail or auto-greeting must be slower than the upstream timeout, or deleted
  outright. Corollary: never leave two timers matched — the original bug was CallRail 30s vs Quo
  30s, a dead heat Quo won.
- 2026-08-04: **Test live routing changes on an inert number with its OWN flow.** All 8 Got Moles
  trackers shared a single call-flow object, so "just testing" an edit would have re-routed every
  live lead across the website pool, three GBPs, Bing, Facebook and the referral line. Cost of
  isolation was one tracker with zero calls in 90 days. Re-verify production is untouched via the
  API after every change, not just at the end.
- 2026-08-04: **Verify the vendor's own diagnosis before accepting a dead end.** Concluded "Quo is
  the wrong tool, switch to JustCall (~$696/yr)" on the strength of broken DTMF — then Quo support
  identified it as an inbound-media problem, and a cellular-vs-wifi test proved it was the agent's
  home router. JustCall uses the same UDP media path and would have failed identically. When a
  symptom is "outbound works, inbound doesn't," suspect the network before the product.
- 2026-08-12: **CORRECTS the 2026-08-04 entry above — Quo's broken inbound DTMF is the PRODUCT,
  not the network.** Keypresses were tested by Muhammad (Pakistan) and Spencer (US), on separate
  networks and separate devices, and the call drops for both. The router diagnosis was wrong and
  it cost weeks: it talked the 08-04 session out of a correct conclusion ("Quo is the wrong tool")
  and the same bind resurfaced today. Two users on different continents failing identically is a
  platform verdict — one user's network is an anecdote, two is a spec.
- 2026-08-12: **A softphone that runs its own ring logic ANSWERS THE CALL SESSION BEFORE A HUMAN
  PICKS UP, and upstream call tracking reads that as "call answered."** CallRail then exits its
  flow, so its Dial timeout never fires and Voice Assist never runs — the caller just hears the
  softphone's ringback until it gives up, then silence. This extends the 08-04 rule ("whichever
  endpoint answers first wins") down to the SIGNALLING layer: the destination does not have to
  play audio to steal the call, it only has to accept the session. **Test for it:** let a tracker
  ring out with screening OFF — ~30s of ringback then silence means premature answer. **Confirm
  it:** turn ON "Prevent voicemails and automated systems from answering a call"; if the AI
  fallback suddenly works, the destination was false-answering. **Structural fix:** a plain SIP
  softphone (Zoiper/Bria on a bare SIP line) does not answer until a human taps answer, and
  passes RFC 2833 DTMF — CallRail's Dial step takes a SIP address natively (`user@sip.provider`).
- 2026-08-12: **Call-tracking recordings only capture the ANSWERED leg — never infer "nothing
  answered" from one.** Hours went into transcribing recordings that showed 36s of ringing and
  silence, concluding Voice Assist was dead, when the recording simply ended before the answered
  portion. **The tell is a recording shorter than the call duration** (46s call / 40s recording).
  Treat that gap as "there is audio you cannot see", not as evidence of silence.
- 2026-08-12: **ASK THE USER WHAT THEY HEARD BEFORE INFERRING FROM TELEMETRY.** This session
  produced four wrong diagnoses in a row — busy-line collapse, matched timers, flow corruption,
  an account-level CallRail bug — each built by reasoning over API fields and audio while Spencer
  had the answer in his ear the whole time. Every correction came from him, not from the data.
  On a live system a human is on, one question ("what did you hear?") outranks an hour of
  forensics, and the forensics should be used to CONFIRM what they report, not to replace it.
- 2026-08-12: **A vendor UI can hide the calls you are looking for behind a default filter.**

  explained everything only appeared after switching it to "Any". A vendor log showing "no record
  of that call" is not proof the call never arrived — clear every filter first. The rejected

- 2026-08-12: **In a ring-all group, one busy device kills the call for everyone unless the
