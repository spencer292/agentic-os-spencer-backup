A diamond spends most of its life in darkness. Buried, overlooked, under pressure, with no fanfare to announce it and no eyes to applaud its making — and yet, in time, it learns to bend light itself.

The entrepreneurs I sit with are not so different. Their brilliance rarely introduces itself. It gets cut and carved in the quiet years nobody sees, the years that look from the outside like nothing is happening at all. I know this because I lived in that dark for a long time. In the spring of 2021 my body finally sent me the invoice for fourteen-hour days, and the man who had built three companies could not climb his own stairs without stopping halfway to rest.

That was the moment everything I thought I knew about success turned over in my hands. The thing is, the breakdown was not the end of the story. It was the pressure that made the stone.

If you are reading this in the dark right now, working hours that are quietly costing you everything you started the business to protect, I am not writing to pull you out. I am writing to hold up a mirror, so you can see what I was finally able to see.
