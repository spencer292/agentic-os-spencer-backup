# Hiring Playbook — Got Moles

Hiring is the growth bottleneck for this business, and it is the HR function where an outsourced
consultancy adds the least value. Nobody at an HR firm knows what a good mole technician looks like.
This playbook encodes what Got Moles has actually learned about the role.

The sequence is fixed. Each step produces a written artifact, because the point of structure is that
two candidates get compared on the same evidence rather than on who interviewed more recently.

---

## Step 1 — Define the role before advertising it

Do not skip to the ad. Half of bad hires are a role nobody had defined.

Produce `templates/job-description.md`, answering:

- **What does this person own, and what result are they judged on?** Not tasks — an outcome. Tie it
  to the Ninety scorecard seat if one exists. If no seat exists, that is a signal worth raising: a
  role without a seat tends to become whatever the last emergency was.
- **Who do they report to, and who reports to them?**
- **What is the pay band** — floor, target, ceiling — and what moves someone through it?
- **What must be true on day one** (licence, driving record, physical requirements, availability)
  versus **what can be trained** (everything mole-specific, per the technician-training-program).
- **What are the first 90 days?** Straight from the training program's competency gate for a tech.

Physical and licence requirements must be real and job-related, and stated. "Able to walk 3–6 miles
a day across uneven ground in all weather, lift 40 lb, and hold a valid licence with an insurable
driving record" is honest and it screens accurately. Vague postings attract vague applicants.

---

## Step 2 — Pay band before the ad, not after the offer

Set the band from what the route is worth, not from what the last applicant asked for — remember the
salary history ban means you cannot ask what they earn now anyway (`wa-compliance.md` §6).

Sanity checks before the band is set:

- Total cost is not the wage. Add employer payroll taxes, L&I premium at the correct class code, the
  vehicle, fuel, phone, equipment, and the training weeks before the person is productive.
- Check the band against the exempt salary threshold if the role is salaried — **$80,168.40 in
  2026**. Below it, the role is hourly and pays overtime, and the ad and offer must say so.
- Check the floor against local minimum wages in the territory the person will work.

---

## Step 3 — Write the ad

Different document from the job description. The description is internal and complete; the ad sells.

- **Post the pay range** even though under 15 employees Got Moles is not required to. It is where
  the market is, it raises applicant quality, and it kills the compliance cliff at hire 15.
- Lead with what is genuinely good about the job: outdoor work, a truck, a route you own, real
  training, veteran-owned, a company that gives you a system rather than leaving you to guess.
- Be specific about the hard parts. Weather, dogs, driving, solo days. Filtering out the wrong
  candidate at the ad stage is the cheapest filter available.
- No salary-history questions in the application form, and none in any screening tool.
- Include the equal opportunity line and the physical requirements.

---

## Step 4 — Screen

A short structured phone screen, identical questions for everyone, scored as you go:

1. What are you doing now, and why are you looking? *(listen for what they are moving away from)*
2. Describe a day where you worked alone, unsupervised, and had to decide something yourself.
3. What is your comfort with dogs on a property, and with going into back yards?
4. Driving record — anything that would show up? *(their account, before the MVR is run)*
5. Availability, start date, and what they are looking for in pay.

**No criminal history questions at this stage** — fair chance rules put that after the qualification
determination (`wa-compliance.md` §6).

---

## Step 5 — Structured interview

`templates/interview-guide.md`. Same questions, same order, scored 1–5 against written anchors,
before comparing candidates to each other.

The four things that predict a good tech here:

| Trait | Why it matters | Ask about |
|---|---|---|
| **Works alone without drifting** | Nobody watches a route. Pace and standard have to be internal. | A time they were unsupervised for a long stretch — what kept the standard up |
| **Talks to homeowners without being told what to say** | Every visit is a customer interaction on someone's property | A time they handled an unhappy customer face to face |
| **Notices things** | The whole job is reading a yard for sign — this is an observation job disguised as a labour job | Walk me through how you'd figure out where an animal is moving on a property you have never seen |
| **Writes it down** | The Jobber-note audit found four conventions in use and most visits with no note at all | How they documented work in their last job, and what happened when they did not |

Do not ask about family, children, childcare, age, health, disability, religion, national origin,
citizenship (beyond "authorised to work in the US, yes/no"), arrests, or salary history. If an
interviewer wanders into one, the answer is not considered and it is not written down.

---

## Step 6 — Reference checks

Two, both former supervisors. Most references say nothing useful because the questions are bad.
These are the ones that work:

1. What were they responsible for, and how did they do against it?
2. On a day nobody was watching, what did the work look like?
3. What did they need from a manager to do their best work?
4. Would you rehire them? *(the pause before the answer is the answer)*

Record the answers in the candidate file. Reference notes are a Tier 1 document.

---

## Step 7 — Offer

`templates/offer-letter.md`. Verbally first, in writing the same day.

The offer letter must state: role, reporting line, start date, **hourly rate or salary with the
exempt/non-exempt status named explicitly**, pay frequency, benefits (or plainly that there are
none yet), that employment is at-will, what must be completed before day one (I-9 documents, MVR
consent, background consent), and an acceptance deadline.

It must **not** promise job security, a bonus that is actually discretionary, an annual salary
figure for an hourly employee (that reads as a guaranteed year), or PTO payout on separation unless
that policy genuinely exists.

---

## Step 8 — Hand off to onboarding

The moment an offer is accepted, run `onboard` mode. The I-9 and new-hire-reporting clocks start at
the start date and they are hard deadlines, not paperwork.

---

## What "good" looks like at each gate

| Gate | Kill criteria |
|---|---|
| Application | No licence, or unavailable for the schedule |
| Phone screen | Cannot give one concrete example of unsupervised work; hostile to customer contact |
| Interview | Scores below 3 on "works alone" or "talks to homeowners"; blames every past employer |
| Reference | A former supervisor who will not say they would rehire |
| MVR / background | Fails the stated driving standard, applied identically to every candidate |

A vacancy costs less than a bad hire on a solo route with keys to a truck and access to the customer
list. Hold the gate.
