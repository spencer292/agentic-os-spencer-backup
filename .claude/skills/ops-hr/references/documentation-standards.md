# Documentation Standards — performance, discipline, separation

This is the highest-risk part of the HR function and the part an outsourced consultancy is slowest
at, because it happens on the day it happens.

The governing idea: **a document is worth exactly what a stranger can understand from it.** Assume
the reader is an ESD adjudicator, an L&I investigator, an attorney, or the employee themselves —
who in Washington has a right to request their file. Write for that reader every time.

---

## The four rules

**1. Behaviour, not character.**
Not "Robert has a bad attitude." Instead: "On 2026-09-14 Robert raised his voice to a customer at
the Barbee Mill site after she questioned the trap placement, and left before completing the visit
note. The customer called the office the same afternoon."

**2. Standard, then gap, then date.**
Every entry names the expectation that was missed, states the specific miss, and dates it. A gap
against no stated standard is an opinion, and it will be read as one.

**3. Written the day it happens.**
A warning dated three weeks after the incident invites the question of what changed in between. If
a conversation happened and was not documented, document it now and say plainly when the
conversation occurred and when the note was written. Never backdate anything.

**4. What was said, and what happens next.**
Record the employee's response in their words, including disagreement. A document that records only
the employer's side reads as one-sided because it is. Then state the specific consequence of a
repeat, and the date it will be reviewed.

## Performance reviews

Tie to the Ninety scorecard number the seat owns where one exists. Where it does not, the review
should say so — a role with no measurable is a management problem, not an employee problem.

Structure:
1. The number they own, and how it moved
2. Two or three specific things done well, with examples and dates
3. One or two specific gaps, with examples and dates
4. What changes before the next review, stated as an outcome, not an attitude
5. Their response, in their words
6. Both signatures and the date

Cadence: 90-day for a new hire (aligned to the technician-training-program competency gate), then
annually, plus a short quarterly check where the number is moving the wrong way.

**Do not use a review to deliver a first warning.** If something is serious enough to warrant a
warning, it warrants one at the time. Storing it up for review season is how an employee gets fired
for a problem they were told about once, six months earlier, at their annual.

## Coaching note (informal)

The lightest record, and the most valuable. A dated note that a conversation happened, what was
discussed, and what was agreed. Not discipline, not in a formal file structure — but the reason a
later warning is credible rather than sudden.

```
2026-09-14 — coaching, {name}, verbal
Discussed: visit notes missing on 6 of 11 stops last week.
Standard: every visit gets a note with activity code, catch count, and next action.
Agreed: {name} will note at the truck before leaving each property.
Review: next Friday.
```

Three lines. Thirty seconds. It is the single highest-leverage HR habit in a small company.

## Progressive discipline

Got Moles does not have to run a fixed progressive-discipline ladder — Washington is an at-will
state, and writing a rigid ladder into a handbook can create a contractual expectation that removes
flexibility. **The handbook should reserve the right to skip steps.**

In practice the sequence that holds up is:

1. **Coaching conversation** — documented as above
2. **Verbal warning, documented** — the employee is told this is a warning
3. **Written warning** — `templates/written-warning.md`, signed
4. **Final written warning or PIP** — with a defined review date
5. **Termination**

Skip straight to the end for: theft, violence or threats, a safety violation that endangered
someone, driving under the influence in a company vehicle, falsifying records or customer counts,
or harassment. Note in the file why the ladder was skipped.

---

## Written warning — what makes one hold up

`templates/written-warning.md`. It must contain:

- The standard, quoted from the handbook or a documented instruction
- The specific incidents, each with a date
- Prior conversations on this subject, with dates
- What must change, stated observably ("a note on 100% of visits"), and by when
- The consequence if it does not
- The employee's response, in their words
- Signatures. **If the employee refuses to sign**, note "employee declined to sign; copy provided
  {date}" and have a witness initial. Refusing to sign does not invalidate the warning.

## PIP (performance improvement plan)

Use a PIP when the person can plausibly succeed and you want them to. Do not use one as a paper
trail toward a decision already made — an employee can usually tell, it wastes 30 days, and it
damages the rest of the crew's trust more than a clean decision would.

30 or 60 days, with: the specific measurable target, weekly check-ins that actually happen, what
support the company provides, and the stated outcome if the target is not met.

---

## Termination

**Before a termination happens, this must already exist in the file:**

- [ ] The standard was documented and communicated before the failure
- [ ] Prior warnings are in the file, dated, unless the conduct justified skipping the ladder
- [ ] The reason is consistent with how other employees in the same situation were treated
- [ ] No protected activity in the recent past — a safety report, a leave request, a complaint, a
      disclosed medical condition, a workers' comp claim. **If any of these exist, stop and get an
      attorney** (SKILL.md escalation list). Timing is what retaliation claims are built on.
- [ ] Final pay is calculated: hours through the last minute worked, plus any accrued PTO the policy
      actually promises. Due by the end of the next established pay period.
- [ ] Equipment recovery list: truck, keys, phone, trap inventory, uniform, fuel card
- [ ] Access list: Jobber, OptimoRoute, email, phone system, any shared password
- [ ] Route handover: which customers, to whom, and who tells the customers

**In the meeting:** short, two people present, the decision stated as a decision rather than a
debate, the effective date, the final pay timing, and what happens to their equipment. Do not
negotiate, do not list every past failing, do not apologise for the decision.

**After:** a termination memo in the file the same day, stating the reason given. Whatever is written
here is what Got Moles is committed to if ESD asks — so it must be accurate, and it must match what
was said in the room.

**Unemployment:** most separations result in benefits being paid, and contesting a claim you will
lose costs more in time than the experience-rating effect. Contest only where there is documented
misconduct — meaning a willful violation of a known standard, not poor performance. Poor
performance is generally not misconduct for ESD purposes, and arguing otherwise without documents
is how a small employer loses credibility on the claim that actually matters later.

---

## Layoff or position elimination

Different from a termination for cause and must be documented differently — the business reason, why
this position, and what selection criteria were used. Do not use "layoff" as a softer label for a
performance termination: it changes the unemployment outcome, and it contradicts the personnel file
if anyone ever reads both.
