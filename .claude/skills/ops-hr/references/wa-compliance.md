# Washington Employment Compliance — Got Moles

Scope: a Washington-only employer with **fewer than 10 W-2 employees**. Headcount matters enormously
in employment law — most federal obligations switch on at 15, 20 or 50 employees. Several of the
worst compliance burdens simply do not apply at this size. Some of the worst traps still do.

**Every number below carries a source and a verify-by date.** WA reindexes minimum wage, the exempt
salary threshold, PFML rates and the non-compete threshold every January. A stale number here is
worse than no number. If a verify-by date has passed, look it up live and update this file before
answering.

Last full verification: **2026-08-26**.

---

## 1. Thresholds — what applies to Got Moles and what does not

| Law | Switches on at | Applies to Got Moles? |
|---|---|---|
| WA Minimum Wage Act, paid sick leave, breaks, overtime | 1 employee | **YES** |
| WA Paid Family & Medical Leave (PFML) | 1 employee | **YES** (employee share only — see §5) |
| WA Cares Fund (long-term care) | 1 employee | **YES** — employee-funded |
| L&I workers' compensation | 1 employee | **YES** — WA is a monopolistic state fund, no private carrier |
| WA Law Against Discrimination (WLAD) | **8 employees** | **AT THE LINE — count carefully** |
| WA Equal Pay & Opportunities Act — pay range in job postings | 15 employees | Not yet. Post ranges anyway (see §6) |
| Federal Title VII, ADA | 15 employees | No |
| Federal ADEA (age) | 20 employees | No |
| Federal FMLA | 50 employees | No |
| ACA employer mandate | 50 FTE | No |
| PFML **employer** premium share | 50 employees | No — small-employer relief applies |

> **The 8-employee line is the one to watch.** The Washington Law Against Discrimination applies to
> employers with eight or more employees, and it is broader than federal law — more protected
> classes, and no damages cap. Crossing from 7 to 8 employees changes the legal exposure of every
> hiring, discipline and termination decision made afterwards. Recount at every hire.
> Source: RCW 49.60.040. Verify by: 2027-01-31.

## 2. Pay

**Minimum wage 2026: $17.13/hour** statewide — but **the state rate is not the operative number for
Got Moles.** Local minimums override where higher, and a tech who performs work inside one of those
cities must be paid that city's rate for those hours.

| City in Got Moles territory | 2026 | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Seattle** | **$21.30** | **All employers, every size — this is the binding floor for Territory 2** |
| Tukwila | $21.65 | Size-tiered ordinance; confirm whether a sub-15-employee employer is covered |
| Renton | $20.57 mid / $21.57 large | Size-tiered; mid rate steps to $21.57 on 2026-07-01 |
| Burien | $20.63 mid / $21.63 large | Size-tiered |
| SeaTac | $20.74 | **Hospitality and transportation only — does not apply to mole control** |

Practical test: if the lowest field wage clears **$21.65**, every local ordinance question closes at
once and the size-tier details never need resolving. "Above minimum wage" meaning above $17.13 is
not the same statement and does not close it.

Source: WA L&I; 2026 Washington minimum wage updates (Ogletree, National Law Review).
Verify by: 2026-12-15 — and note Renton reindexes mid-year.

**Overtime:** 1.5x after 40 hours in a workweek. WA has no daily overtime.

**Salaried does not mean exempt.** To be exempt from overtime in Washington an employee must meet
BOTH a duties test AND a salary threshold far above the federal one.

> **2026 exempt salary threshold: 2.25 x minimum wage = $1,541.70/week = $80,168.40/year**, for
> employers of every size. It steps up to 2.5x by 2028.
> Source: L&I, "Changes made to Washington's overtime rules". Verify by: 2026-12-15.

Any salaried person paid below that is **non-exempt and owed overtime**, whatever their job title.
Manager, Operator, Director — the title is irrelevant, and so is the fact that they agreed to a
salary. See `projects/briefs/hr-in-house/open-findings.md`.

**Final paycheck:** due by the end of the next established pay period after separation, for quits
and terminations alike. WA does not require payout on the last day. Accrued PTO is only owed if
company policy or past practice promises it — which is a reason to write that policy deliberately
instead of letting a practice form by accident.

**Deductions from final pay** for unreturned equipment, damage or shortages are tightly restricted
in WA and generally require a written agreement made in advance. Do not withhold pay to force the
return of a trap kit, phone or truck key — recover the property separately.

## 3. Hours, breaks and drive time

- **Rest break:** 10 minutes, paid, for each 4 hours worked. Must be *allowed* — it cannot be waived
  away by paying extra.
- **Meal break:** 30 minutes, unpaid, beginning no later than the end of the 5th hour. If the
  employee is required to remain on duty, it must be paid.
- Breaks must be recorded, or a written policy must make them auditable. On a solo route with no
  supervisor present, a missed-break claim is decided on whatever documentation exists.

**Drive time.** Ordinary home-to-first-stop and last-stop-to-home commuting is generally unpaid.
Travel *between* job sites during the workday is hours worked and must be paid. The operating note —
"HOURS = job 1 to last job; commute unpaid" — matches that rule, and holds so long as the tech is
genuinely free of duty while commuting.

**The one exception that exists here: fuel.** Fueling a company vehicle the tech is required to
drive is generally **integral and indispensable** to the work rather than a preliminary errand.
Once a compensable activity begins, the **continuous workday** has started and travel after it is on
the clock. Washington is more employee-favorable than the federal baseline — L&I treats
employer-required travel outside the ordinary commute as hours worked. So on a day where a tech
fuels before the first job:

- Home → gas station: ordinary commute, unpaid
- **Fueling: paid**
- **Gas station → first stop: paid** — the day has already begun

**Standing rule for Got Moles: techs fuel between stops during the workday, never before the first
job.** That keeps the activity inside time already being paid, costs nothing, and removes the
question entirely. If fueling first is unavoidable on a given day, the clock starts at the fuel stop
that day. Confirmed with Spencer 2026-08-26; see `open-findings.md` F4.

Anything else that becomes required before the first stop — loading the truck, taking dispatch
calls, carrying company property under instruction — starts the same clock. A wrong assumption here
compounds across every employee and every week, which is why wage-and-hour sits on the attorney
escalation list in `SKILL.md`.

## 4. Paid Sick Leave (WA PSL)

- Accrues at **1 hour per 40 hours worked**, from the first day of employment. No accrual cap.
- Usable from the **90th calendar day** of employment.
- **Carryover:** unused leave carries over. From 2026 employers must permit a minimum carryover of
  24 hours even where policy caps lower; the long-standing 40-hour carryover remains the safe
  operating floor.
- Paid at the greater of minimum wage or the employee's normal hourly rate.
- Usable for the employee's own illness or a family member's, closure of the workplace or a child's
  school for public-health reasons, and domestic-violence-related absences. The definition of
  "family member" was broadened effective 2025.
- **A doctor's note may only be required after 3 consecutive scheduled workdays**, and only if a
  written policy said so in advance.
- **Notice and records:** employees must be told their accrued, used and available balance at least
  monthly, and records kept for **3 years**.

Source: WA L&I paid sick leave rules; 2026 updates. Verify by: 2027-01-31.

Frontloading is permitted and is usually simpler than accrual tracking for a small crew — but a
written policy is a condition of doing it, not an optional extra.

## 5. Payroll-tied programs

These are administered by the payroll provider and audited here.

| Program | 2026 | Got Moles obligation |
|---|---|---|
| **PFML** | Total premium **1.13%** of wages, to the SS wage cap ($184,500) | Under 50 employees: **exempt from the employer share**, but must still withhold and remit the employee portion and report quarterly |
| **WA Cares** | 0.58% of wages, employee-funded | Withhold and remit unless the employee holds an approved exemption |
| **L&I workers' comp** | Rate set by risk classification | Quarterly reports. The correct class code matters — misclassification is assessed retroactively, with penalties |
| **ESD unemployment** | Experience-rated | Quarterly wage reports |

Source: ESD news release on the 2026 PFML rate; L&I. Verify by: 2026-12-15.

**Small-employer PFML relief is from the premium, not from the program.** Employees are fully
covered and can take PFML leave. Job-restoration obligations under PFML attach at 50+ employees —
so at this headcount an employee can take paid state leave that Got Moles is not legally required to
hold the job open for. Decide that deliberately and in writing, before it happens, not during it.

## 6. Hiring and job postings

- **Pay range disclosure** in job postings is required at **15+ employees** (Equal Pay & Opportunities
  Act, amended 2025). Got Moles is below it. **Post the range anyway** — it lifts applicant quality,
  it is where the market has already moved, and it removes a compliance cliff at the 15th hire.
  Source: RCW 49.58.110. Verify by: 2027-01-31.
- **The salary history ban applies at every size.** Do not ask an applicant what they currently earn,
  and do not let an interviewer ask. Asking what they are looking for is fine.
- **Fair chance:** WA restricts asking about criminal history until the applicant has been determined
  otherwise qualified. Because techs drive company vehicles and enter private property, a background
  and driving check is defensible — but it comes *after* the initial qualification screen, and must
  be applied identically to every candidate for that role.
- **MVR / driving records:** required for anyone driving a company vehicle, for the insurer as much
  as for the law. Record the *decision* in the personnel file ("meets driving standard: yes/no,
  checked {date}"); the underlying report goes to `hr-private/`.
- **I-9:** Section 1 by the employee's **first day of work**; Section 2 by the employer within
  **3 business days of the start date**. Hard federal deadline, per-form penalties. Store I-9s
  **separately from personnel files** — that separation is the entire point of the rule.
- **WA new-hire reporting:** report every new hire to the state within **20 days**. Most payroll
  providers do this automatically — get that in writing from yours and file the confirmation.
  Assuming it happens is how it stops happening.

## 7. Non-competes and protecting the route book

- A non-compete is **void** in WA unless the worker earns above an indexed threshold —
  **$126,858.83 for employees in 2026** ($317,147.09 for independent contractors). No Got Moles
  technician will be anywhere near it. Any non-compete a tech has signed is unenforceable today.
- **All non-competes in Washington become void on 2027-06-30**, regardless of earnings, under the
  2026 amendment. Source: RCW 49.62 as amended. Verify by: 2027-01-31.
- What still works, and is what actually protects this business: a **confidentiality agreement**
  covering the customer list, route book and pricing, plus a **narrowly scoped non-solicitation**
  of customers. Scope those with a lawyer, not with this skill.
- The customer list and route data are the asset. Treat them as trade secret and restrict access
  accordingly — that protects far more than a signature a court will ignore.

## 8. Required postings and written notices

Small WA employers must display current state and federal posters where employees can see them — at
the shop, and realistically delivered digitally too, for a crew that is rarely in an office.
Minimum wage, paid sick leave, PFML, WA Cares, workers' comp ("Job Safety and Health"), unemployment,
EEO, and "Your Rights as a Worker". Posters are free from L&I and ESD — **never pay a
compliance-poster vendor.** Reissue every January when rates change.

Employees must also receive, in writing: the paid sick leave policy and monthly balance, the PFML
notice, the safety program (§9), and a signed handbook acknowledgement.

## 9. Safety — DOSH / WISHA

Washington runs its own OSHA equivalent (DOSH, inside L&I). A field crew that handles traps, works
in gardens and around dogs, drives all day and lifts has genuine exposure.

- A written **Accident Prevention Program (APP)** is required of **every** employer in Washington,
  regardless of size. Not optional, and among the first things DOSH asks to see.
- Employers with **more than 10 employees** keep OSHA 300 logs. Below that, still record serious
  incidents.
- **A fatality or hospitalisation must be reported to L&I within 8 hours.**
- Documented safety orientation at hire, and a first aid kit in every vehicle.

If Cascade HR never produced an APP, that is a finding — see `open-findings.md`.

## 10. Record retention

| Record | Keep for |
|---|---|
| Payroll, hours, wage records | 3 years |
| Paid sick leave accrual and use | 3 years |
| I-9 | 3 years after hire OR 1 year after separation, whichever is later — **stored separately** |
| Personnel file (offers, reviews, discipline) | 3 years after separation minimum; 6 is safer |
| Medical / ADA / workers' comp | Separate confidential file, never inside the personnel file |
| Applicant records for a filled role | 1 year minimum |

**Washington employees may request a copy of their personnel file at least annually, and former
employees may request it for up to 3 years after separation — generally to be produced within 21
calendar days.** Write every document in this system as though the employee will read it, because
they are entitled to.
