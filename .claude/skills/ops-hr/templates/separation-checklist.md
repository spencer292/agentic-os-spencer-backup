## Before the meeting — involuntary separations only
- [ ] The standard was documented and communicated before the failure
- [ ] Warnings are in the file, dated — or the conduct justified skipping the ladder and the file
      says why
- [ ] Treatment is consistent with how others in the same situation were handled
- [ ] **No protected activity in the recent past** — a safety report, a leave request, a complaint,
      a disclosed medical condition, a workers compensation claim. **If any of these exist: stop and
      get an attorney.** Timing is what a retaliation claim is built on.
- [ ] Final pay calculated — hours through the last minute worked, plus accrued PTO only if policy
      actually promises it. **Due by the end of the next established pay period.**
- [ ] A second person arranged to be in the room

## The meeting
Short. State the decision as a decision, not as an argument. Effective date, final pay timing, what
happens to the equipment. Do not negotiate, do not re-litigate the history, do not apologise for the
decision.

## Unemployment
Expect a claim. Contest only where there is **documented willful misconduct** — a deliberate
violation of a known standard. Poor performance is generally not misconduct for ESD purposes.
Contesting a claim you will lose costs more in time than the experience-rating effect, and it spends
credibility that is needed for the claim that actually matters later.
