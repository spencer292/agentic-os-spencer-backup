## 1. Operating philosophy (why the settings look the way they do)

Fit: APPLIES.

- "Andromeda era": creative is the targeting. Meta's ranking model matches each ad's content to individuals; audience inputs are suggestions, not walls. Broad targeting "outperforms detailed targeting in 95% of cases." The leverage is creative and offer, not audience stacking [04/04].
- Meta's defaults are tuned for the "lowest common denominator" advertiser and quietly alter the signal you send. The author claims ROAS gains several times just from switching defaults off. Re-check at every launch, and again after publish, because Meta re-enables some [04/01].
- Consolidate. More than 3-4 ad sets in one prospecting campaign means over-segmenting and competing with yourself. A healthy account is about 2-4 campaigns [04/04, 04/02, 04/13].
- Bid strategy cannot fix weak creative, offer or page. It only sets how aggressively Meta competes [04/03].
- One meaningful change at a time; keep a change log. Do not change the landing page and launch new ads in the same week, or you cannot attribute the result [04/06, 04/07].

## 3. Campaign structure: ABO test, CBO scale, optional retargeting

### 3.1 Campaign map

Fit: ADAPT (local, modest budget: start with ONE test campaign only).

- Campaign 1 "testing vault": ABO. 10-20% of total spend once the account has winners. In a brand-new account the entire account is testing at the start [04/02].
- Campaign 2 scaling: CBO, broad, only proven ads. 70-80% of spend [04/05]. The graduating lesson says "about 80%" [04/13].
- Campaign 3 retargeting: separate campaign, 10-15% of spend, only when there is enough warm volume (section 10) [04/05, 04/12].
- Typical healthy account: 2-3 campaigns, up to 4. For one market, one offer and one sales team, "all you may need is one testing campaign and one scaling campaign" [yt-local, 04/07].
- Do NOT make a campaign per town or per radius. Split markets only when they need different offer, sales team, capacity, economics or budget owner [04/07, yt-local]. He says one combined campaign is right for about 98% of local businesses; multi-location (his example: five gyms) may justify splitting.

### 3.2 CBO vs ABO rules

Fit: APPLIES.

- CBO (campaign budget) lets Meta shift money between ad sets/ads. Use it to scale proven winners [04/02].
- ABO (ad set budget) forces spend per ad set. Use it for testing, because CBO dumps most budget (he says ~95%) on the first ad that gets a click or conversion and starves the rest, giving false negatives [04/02].
- Never test new creative inside CBO. Never scale with ABO [04/02].
- Fragmenting a scaling budget over many campaigns, or running everything through capped ad sets, starves the algorithm [04/05].
- Click path: campaign level has a "Campaign budget" (CBO) toggle. Off = budget is set per ad set (ABO) [04/02].

### 3.3 Ad set shape inside each campaign

- Testing (ABO), two test types. (a) Creative test: 3-4 fresh creatives for a known angle in one ad set, to beat the current winner. (b) Angle test: each ad set is one angle (pain-based, social proof, direct offer) with 3-4 creatives inside, fixed daily budget per ad set so every angle gets spend [04/13].
- Scaling (CBO): small budget = ONE broad ad set holding all winners (he says people reach seven figures this way). With more budget: several broad ad sets, each an angle bucket or awareness level (problem-aware, testimonial/social proof, direct offer) [04/13].
- Keep ad sets to 3-4 or fewer in any prospecting campaign [04/04].

## 4. Objective and optimization

Fit: APPLIES (lead path). The sales path is ECOM-ONLY.

- "99% of campaigns are either a Lead or a Sales objective." Local service lead gen = **Leads** [04/08].
- Special Ad Categories (credit, housing, employment, social issues): not applicable to mole control. Confirm none is selected [04/08].
- Conversion location: **Website** (his default; "lead forms usually don't bring in the best quality of leads") [04/08, yt-local]. Alternatives: Instant Form, Messenger/IG/WhatsApp (section 8).
- Performance goal: **Maximize number of conversions**. Do NOT optimize for clicks or link clicks (Meta then finds clickers) [04/08]. Later experiment: maximize value of conversions once there is data. Landing page view optimization exists, but "a loaded page is not a lead" [04/06].
- Pick the correct **pixel** and **conversion event** (standard events, or a custom conversion if built). His example event: Submit Application. Verify every time [04/08].
- Attribution: Standard (default). Default window is 7-day click; shorten if the decision is fast [04/08].
- Signal quality: optimize on an event that means a real lead. A Lead event that fires on landing on a form page "is not a lead" [04/12]. Where there is a qualification step, send back only qualified events so Meta learns the right people [04/06, 04/10].
- Pixel plus Conversions API (CAPI) is a one-time setup worth a one-off developer. Later, feed qualified and later-stage outcomes back via CRM + CAPI; an improvement later, not a substitute for fixing the basic process [04/12, yt-local, 04/07].

## 5. Bid strategy

Fit: APPLIES. The answer is simple.

- Field: campaign level, **Campaign bid strategy**. Options at recording: Highest volume, Cost per result goal, ROAS goal (only if value optimization is eligible), Bid cap [04/03].
- Default and right choice for new accounts, new campaigns, creative tests, low volume and most scaling: **Highest volume**. Stay there until you have a baseline [04/03].
- Cost per result goal (modern "cost cap"): only when the campaign is proven and stable, you know the real target CPA, volume is healthy (Meta suggests roughly 50-100 conversions per week for goal strategies), and you accept less scale. It sets an AVERAGE, not a ceiling. Start near the current average or slightly above (Meta suggests 10-20% headroom). Too tight and delivery slows to nearly nothing [04/03].
- ROAS goal: e-commerce with varying prices; rarely the first tool for lead gen without downstream revenue data. ECOM-ONLY in practice [04/03].
- Bid cap: advanced, high volume only. Not for new tests or a rising CPA. Common mistake is setting it too low so spend stalls [04/03].
- If spend stalls under a control: confirm campaign active, compare target to recent baseline, check conversion volume, review over 1-4 weeks (not today), relax the target or return to Highest volume [04/03].
- Before choosing anything else write down: recent average CPA, break-even CPA, weekly conversion volume for the optimized event. If unknown, stay on Highest volume [04/03].

## 6. Audience, location and Advantage+ settings (local core)

### 6.1 Principles

Fit: APPLIES.

- Start broad inside the real service area. For standard local businesses do not add interests: "the local boundary, the offer and the creative is enough filtering." City plus interests can shrink the audience until it is not worth running [yt-local, 04/07].
- Three filters, not one: (1) location setting, (2) local message (service, relevance, proof, next step), (3) qualification path (page/form/conversation) [04/07].
- Location targeting is "not a perfect fence," so the ad and page must finish the filtering [04/07].
- Do not fragment a modest budget across five nearby towns, several radius sizes, or interests by default. "If you can't explain why a separate ad set or campaign helps you decide, don't make it" [yt-local, 04/07].
- Segment with creative, not audiences: different hooks for different awareness levels, and let the system route them [04/04].
- Do not stack interests, do not build 15 exclusion layers, do not spend test budget on testing audiences instead of creatives [04/04].
- Lookalikes: optional guiding signal for new accounts (e.g., 1% of best customers). Most mature accounts find broad beats 1% lookalike. Custom audiences (visitors, video viewers, lists, purchasers) are warm retargeting layers, not restrictions on cold [04/04].

### 6.2 Field by field (ad set > Audience)

Fit: APPLIES.

1. **Locations.** Ad set > Audience > Locations. Pick exact cities, regions, counties or ZIP codes; use an address/pin with radius only if it reflects real operations. Exclude areas only if the business cannot serve them [04/07, yt-local]. Check every detail. Author story: he once selected the COUNTRY Georgia instead of the US state, spent a lot and refunded the client. Triple-check before publish [yt-local].
2. **Location-type setting.** Speaker: "make sure this is always unchecked ... if you leave this checked it will show to people outside of areas you cover." The transcript never names the control. It is most likely the "recently in this location" / travelers inclusion; pick "People living in this location" if it is a dropdown. Field name is my inference; confirm on screen [yt-local].
3. **Age.** Set a minimum only for a real reason (legal, product eligibility, business constraint) [04/10]. For home services he notes 18-year-olds usually do not own homes [yt-local]; his high-ticket default is 25+ [04/10]. Do not restrict to an imagined ideal-client band like 25-54 [04/10].
4. **Gender.** Leave open unless the offer is gender-specific. His example: Advantage+ left on for a men-only offer wasted about a quarter of spend on women; fixing it halved cost per lead [04/10].
5. **Advantage+ audience.** Turn OFF for local. In the UI at recording, click "Further limit the reach of your ads," which switches off Advantage+ audience so age, gender and location bind. "If you leave it on, it can and will likely show your ad to people outside of your service area." Even then Meta delivers to whoever it predicts will convert; location and minimum age are hard controls, the rest are suggestions [yt-local, 04/10, 04/08].
6. **Detailed targeting.** Empty for cold local. Turn off "Use as a suggestion" / detailed targeting expansion [04/01, yt-local].
7. **Custom audience exclusions.** Only when they prevent genuine wasted spend. Where existing customers cannot buy again, exclude them [04/10]. See section 10 for Got Moles. Keep exclusions minimal and intentional [04/04].
8. **Languages / devices.** Default. Restrict device only if the offer requires [04/08].
9. Do not flip Advantage+ on and off after a bad day or two; give changes a few days [04/10].

### 6.3 Placements

Fit: APPLIES.

- Default (all placements: Facebook Feed, Stories, Reels, Instagram, etc.) usually does best. **Exclude Audience Network only.** Everything else stays on [04/08, 04/01].
- Creative sizes: 1:1 or 4:5 for feed (4:5 preferred, more real estate); 9:16 for Stories/Reels. Leave the right-column/desktop slot square even if the editor asks for another ratio; it has displayed squares "for months" [04/08].
- Preview every placement before publishing [04/08, 04/09].
- Inference: removing Audience Network may require switching from Advantage+ placements to Manual placements. Verify.

### 6.4 When Advantage+ audience is fine (not for Got Moles)

ADAPT/skip. He uses it as the default for broad-offer prospecting once the account has conversions, but names a defined service area as a reason to turn it off [04/10]. For a brand-new account/offer he restricts first and opens up "8.5 times out of 10" after data [04/10]. For Got Moles, location is a hard constraint, so keep it off.

## 7. Budget allocation

Fit: ADAPT (framework applies; numbers scale down).

- Buckets: testing 10-20%, scaling 70-80%, retargeting 10-15%. He says the ratios hold from $1,000/month to $1M/month [04/05].
- At $1,000/month: testing $150-200, scaling $700-750, retargeting $100-150. (His $5,000 example mis-states the scaling figure; use the percentages, not his sums.) [04/05]
- Testing buys certainty and is expected to lose money. "You do not scale to find winners, you scale winners you've found" [04/05].
- Test unit: $10/day per ABO ad set for about 5 days = about $50 per test; if the ad hits KPI, move it to scaling. Contrast: $100 behind an untested ad for a week ended up as $700 spent with no learning [04/05].
- Shift toward testing when: brand-new account, scaling ads fatiguing, new market or offer, no consistent winner in 60 days. Toward scaling when: 3+ proven ads consistently hit KPI and the test pipeline yields regular winners. Toward retargeting when: long sales cycle, engaged-but-not-converting cold traffic, a launch/promo [04/05].
- Raise budget from a plan (max daily spend, acceptable downside), not fear of missing out. Be careful with automated rules ("my most expensive mistakes have been automations") [04/14].
- No lead-gen numeric KPI thresholds are given anywhere in the module. The KPI must come from the business's own break-even.

## 8. Conversion path: website vs instant form vs messages

Fit: APPLIES (important for a phone-first business).

- He prefers a **website landing page** over an instant form because instant forms "tend to bring in lower quality leads." If lead quality is low or leads don't answer calls, "it could very likely be because you're using instant form" [yt-local, 04/08].
- Website: when the person needs proof, detail, an application or a booking flow. Instant form: when the offer is very easy to understand and the business can follow up extremely fast; ask only questions that change the next action. Message path: when a real conversation helps qualify and someone can reply promptly [04/07, yt-local].
- Do NOT run website, instant form and messenger at once and crown the cheapest. Test one path at a time and compare the same downstream outcomes [04/07, yt-local].
- Exception honored: if instant-form quality is fine, use it [yt-local].
- Qualification gate (his high-ticket trick): a qualifying application before the calendar, and only the "qualified" event goes back to Meta [04/06, 04/10]. Local adapt: ask service-area / timeline style questions that improve quality, but keep the form short. One unnecessary question can cut conversion about 30% [04/06].

## 9. Lead quality loop and measurement

Fit: APPLIES.

- A cheap lead is a platform metric. Track the chain: Lead > Contacted > Qualified > Booked > Showed > Sold > Collected revenue. "Somebody needs to record what happens after the lead comes in." No perfect CRM needed on day one [yt-local, 04/07].
- If lead quality is weak, diagnose in order: (1) was the location correct, (2) did the message state service, offer and next action, (3) did the page/form confirm what matters, (4) was follow-up prompt and tracked [04/07]. "Leads from a different state" almost always means default Advantage+/location settings [yt-local].
- Google Local Services Ads: eligible home services should consider LSA alongside Meta. Meta introduces the offer and proof before the search; LSA captures active search. Compare channels on the same outcome path, not a cheap Meta form fill against an LSA call. Same discipline for both: accurate service area, correct services, reviews/proof, fast response, lead quality process [yt-local, 04/07]. Fit: ADAPT. Got Moles already runs Google; apply the same-outcome comparison.
- Landing page diagnosis patterns [04/06]:
  1. Outbound clicks healthy but landing page views weak = page handoff problem (speed, broken URL, redirects, tracking, in-app browser, cookie banner, pop-ups).
  2. Landing page views healthy but next action weak = message match, clarity, proof, price, commitment framing. Don't fiddle with ads.
  3. Leads fine but downstream quality weak = form too easy, attracting curiosity, qualification unclear, or follow-up failing.
  4. Page converts but business result weak = economics, product quality, sales process, shallow event; the page may not be the constraint.
- Retargeting often shows the best ROAS/CPA but may only be taking credit for people who would have converted. Judge by whether total qualified leads rise [04/12].

## 10. Existing customers and retargeting

### 10.1 Customer lifecycle strategy (existing-customer control)

Fit: ADAPT. [yt-existing]

- A new setting at the TOP of the ad set level "when you build a new sales campaign." Default is "get conversions from all audiences." Switch to "acquire new customers" to shift delivery toward people who haven't bought. The video says sales campaign, so it may not appear for a Leads objective. Check on screen.
- Before enabling: Meta guesses which custom audiences are existing customers, and "you can't trust that guess." Review the list. Upload a customer list or connect the CRM, build a purchasers audience from the pixel, then mark these as existing customers in advertising settings.
- Leave OFF the option to "exclude engaged audiences." Website visitors and content engagers are hot prospects; excluding them tanks results.
- Use new-customer-only when a segment breakdown shows a big share of spend reaching existing customers, the offer is new-customer-only, or the product is one-time. If customers repurchase, leave on all audiences. Not on by default.
- Manual exclusions in targeting "choke delivery"; the lifecycle setting is built into optimization.
- Got Moles read: Total Mole Control is a recurring service for the same households, so spend reaching current clients is plausible. Needs a Jobber customer-list upload (privacy: Meta hashes uploads; confirm with Spencer first).

### 10.2 Retargeting

Fit: ADAPT (mostly later).

- Retargeting still works, but the old mandatory cold/warm/hot three-campaign funnel is outdated. Some accounts keep scaling broad and use creative (testimonials, comparison, objection handling) for product-aware and fully aware viewers. Standalone retargeting stopped working after the model launch for a while, then "made a comeback" about six months before recording [04/12, 04/04].
- Two valid structures. A) Creative-led: one broad scaling campaign containing cold, problem-aware and bottom-funnel creative; exclude recent purchasers/converted leads if the goal is net-new. B) Compact standalone retargeting campaign for a specific high-intent behavior with enough volume [04/12].
- Hold off on standalone retargeting when warm traffic is low, event tracking is unreliable, or you have no distinct warm message. Small accounts: no six tiny ad sets; at most one campaign with one high-intent warm ad set [04/12].
- Verify events first. For lead gen: application started, submitted, call booked, qualified [04/12].
- Build click path: Ads Manager > Audiences > Create audience > Custom audience > Website > pick pixel + event + retention window > name it (e.g., "Visitors Who Did Not Purchase 30 Days"). Window should match the buying cycle (e-commerce about 30 days, high-ticket longer). New audiences take time to populate [04/12].
- Warm message = answer the objection that blocked the first conversion: Why care? (unique process). Can I trust you? (proof: testimonials, case studies, founder video). Will it work for me? (specific story). Why now? (real deadline, price increase, low-friction next step) [04/12].
- If running standalone: audience = custom audience only; Advantage+ audience OFF and "use as suggestion" OFF or it shows to everyone; exclude those people from cold and exclude visitors from cold so they do not overlap; send people to the step they left, not the beginning [04/12].
- Pre-call engagement campaign (high ticket): engagement objective to people who booked a call, 4-10 objection-handling videos, about $25/day, from booking through about 14 days; he claims show-up rates doubled. He also does not exclude people who booked [04/12, 04/04]. Skip for $450-$600 residential jobs; maybe test later for scheduled in-person commercial bids (unproven here).
- Dynamic catalog / cart-abandon retargeting: ECOM-ONLY.

## 11. Landing page rules

Fit: APPLIES. (The "Post-Click Page Audit" Google Sheet linked from the lesson is not in the corpus.)

The ad starts the conversation; the page finishes it. One page, one job: help the right person take the next meaningful action [04/06].

1. **Message match.** First screen confirms the exact promise that earned the click: same promise, buyer, visual context and next action (not word for word). Example: if the ad promises a storm damage inspection, don't open with company history and a stock truck photo. Great CTR with poor conversion is usually a page that doesn't continue the conversation [04/06].
2. **Answer in order:** Am I in the right place? Why should I care? Is it believable (proof)? What am I asked to do? What will it cost me (money, time, privacy, sales-trap fear)? Why trust this business (guarantees, FAQs, process)? [04/06]
3. **Above the fold** shows the action. Lead-gen headlines state directly what they get; no curiosity headlines [04/06].
4. **Proof where the doubt appears,** not a testimonial carousel at the bottom. Specific beats vague ("great service" is weak; a concrete customer story is good) [04/06].
5. **Friction:** waste (long irrelevant forms, unclear offer) vs useful qualification (service area, project timeline). Five checks: cognitive (buyer language, not brand language), credibility, practical (price, service area, timeline, what happens next), technical (mobile and in-app browser), process (the business delivers on the expectation after submit) [04/06].
6. **Mobile first.** 80-90% of visitors may be on phones. Click the real ad link on a real phone; check redirects, cookie banners and pop-ups; run Google PageSpeed Insights (not a perfect score, just quick) [04/06].
7. **Form length.** One unnecessary question (his example: address) can cut conversion about 30% [04/06].
8. **No homepage** as destination except rare cases; send to a page matching the ad. Several ads that share buyer, promise, offer and next action can share one page; build a variant when the ad sets a different expectation [04/06].
9. **Thank-you page** states what happens next [04/06].
10. **Change control:** one hypothesis, one change, logged; no ad changes the same week [04/06].
11. **Local signal on page:** confirm service area and location details so the lead can self-select [04/07].
12. No universal "good" conversion rate; benchmark against yourself [04/06].

## 12. Ad copy and creative fields

Fit: APPLIES. Click path: Ad level > Ad setup > Create ad (manual, not catalog) > Creative > Media, Primary text, Headline, Description, Call to action, Destination [04/08, 04/09].

- **Identity:** pick the Facebook Page and Instagram account; leave WhatsApp off [04/08].
- **Media:** upload 1:1 (or 4:5) and 9:16; keep the right-column/horizontal slot square [04/08].
- **Primary text:** the first line is the hook, since feed cuts off at "See more"; don't open with a generic brand intro. Up to 5 variations; he uses 2 short + 3 long so both reader types are covered (high-ticket leans long, low-ticket leans short). All variations must work with the creative, headline and page because Meta may pair any of them [04/09]. He tested one vs five variations and recommends filling all five.
- **Headline:** short; sits below the creative in feed; may not show in Stories/Reels; long ones cut off. Up to 5; each must fit every primary text [04/09].
- **Description:** optional small credibility line; often not displayed; never put price or deadline only here [04/09].
- **Website URL:** must match the ad's promise (a booking ad goes to a booking page). **Display link:** cosmetic only [04/09].
- **CTA button:** match the destination and commitment. Learn more (educate first), Sign up (free/lead magnet), Book now (booking flow), Apply now (application), **Get quote** (quote request), Send message (conversation). He sees little difference between buttons but the CTA and page must tell the same story [04/09].
- **Tracking:** add UTM parameters (Website URL section > build a URL parameter) so Google Analytics can attribute [04/08].
- **Naming:** a convention that survives 100 ads. Campaign = brand_objective_name_date; ad set = audience description + date; ad = creative description, hook/angle, format, date [04/08].
- **AI copy prompt shape (his):** give offer, creative angle, visual hook, audience; ask for 5 primary texts (2 short, 3 long), 5 headlines, 1 optional description; first line of each must stand alone; headline short and complementary; no repeated sentences; no invented claims; state the CTA. Always do a human edit (strip emojis, AI tone) [04/09, 04/08].
- **Local signal:** every local ad needs at least one real local signal: city/area in copy, a real nearby job or recognizable setting in creative, authentic local proof, or the service area on the destination. Including the city is said to lift conversion (his "100%" figure is unsupported). Don't force city names where unnatural. Keep the usual pain-point and proof structure. A relevant local problem beats "[City] residents get a free quote." Pattern: "[City] [service] appointment. See whether your project qualifies for a free on-site estimate" (place, service, next step) [yt-local, 04/07].
- Got Moles: all copy passes the mkt-cmo claims gate (no invented numbers, no I-713, Posture A wording, applies to imagery too).

## 13. Graduating a winner (testing to scaling)

Fit: APPLIES (later). [04/13]

1. Prove it: final outcome repeats at a viable CPA (a high CTR or one cheap result is not enough); downstream quality checked; angle known.
2. Choose ONE existing post (Facebook or Instagram original, whichever holds the social proof). One post ID per ad; creative and compatible copy stay identical.
3. Method A (his preference): on the winning ad, Share > "Facebook post with comments" preview link; copy the post ID after "post"; in the scaling campaign ad set, Create ad > **Use existing post** > paste ID > publish. Preserves likes, shares and comments; the same ad in several ad sets shares proof; **copy cannot be edited**.
4. Method B: Duplicate to the scaling campaign/ad set (Meta has a "show existing reactions, comments and shares" option, not guaranteed). Fine when spend was small or copy will change.
5. Before publish check identity, destination, conversion event, creative.
6. After: confirm live; **pause the winner in the testing campaign** so new tests get a fair read (can overlap a couple of days). Results often look worse for a few days in scaling and recover; scaled ads also assist each other. Reusing a post preserves proof, not delivery history.
7. Graduation log: post/platform, why it earned the move, date moved, next review date.

## 14. Local expansion, Q4, and e-commerce-only items

- Expansion (local): add a market only if (a) the current market yields qualified opportunities, not just cheap leads, (b) crews/sales/appointment capacity exists, (c) there is a specific hypothesis for the next market, (d) proof, offer, pricing and fulfillment support it. Keep offer and path stable; change only what the new market needs. One market at a time [04/07, yt-local].
- Q4 for a local service: do not copy e-commerce Black Friday discounts. "A local service may prioritize response capacity and booking availability." Build a seasonal baseline from your own prior periods (CPM, CTR, landing page view rate, conversion rate, cancellations, capacity), not a quiet week. Test offer/creative before the peak. Urgency must be honest. Define what gets paused if the path degrades [04/14]. Black Friday offer stacks, inventory, shipping cutoffs and Meta holiday guidance: ECOM-ONLY.
- Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns, Advantage+ Catalog, ROAS goal, purchase-value optimization: ECOM-ONLY [04/11, 04/03].

## 15. Campaign build spec for Got Moles first launch
