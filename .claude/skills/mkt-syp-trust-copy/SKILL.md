# SYPerformance Trust Building

SYP sells billet drivetrain parts to people who have already broken something. They are
skeptical by default, they have been burned by rebadged catalog parts, and they are
spending real money on a car they care about. **Trust is the entire conversion mechanism.**

This skill exists because the first draft of the SYP site failed on trust in a specific,
fixable way: **it argued instead of proving.**

## The brand in one line

**Performance, precision, quality. We don't cut corners.**

Everything below is how that line gets expressed on a page without ever being stated as a
slogan. If copy has to say "we don't cut corners," the page has already failed. **Show the
corner that wasn't cut.**

---

## Rule 1: Positive frame, always

The failed draft defined SYP entirely by negatives — what we don't do, what doesn't ship,
what someone else gets wrong. Defensive copy makes a reader ask who accused you.

Every claim gets rewritten as the affirmative version of itself.

| Defensive (kill) | Affirmative (ship) |
|---|---|
| "We don't rebadge someone else's." | "Our drawings. Our tolerances. Our name." |
| "Nothing ships that does not meet spec." | "Every part is inspected, then boxed." |
| "Rather than discovered after checkout." | "Lead time is on the product page before you pay." |
| "Designed against the failure." | "Designed from what broke." |

**The test:** if a sentence contains *don't, doesn't, not, never, rather than, unlike*,
rewrite it. Comparison to competitors is allowed only when the comparison is a spec, not a
jab.

## Rule 2: Proof over adjectives

Adjectives are free. Anyone can type "precision." Trust comes from a number, an image, a
name, or a process step that a liar would not bother to fabricate.

Every trust claim needs one of these attached:

- **A number with a unit.** Tolerance in thousandths. Material spec (4340, 300M, 6061-T6).
  Power held. Torque figure. Hours of machine time.
- **A photo of the real thing.** The mill. The fixture. A part mid-cut with chips on it.
  Not a stock photo, not a render.
- **A named car.** Platform, power, owner or shop, how long the part has been on it.
- **A process step.** What specifically gets checked, on what equipment, by whom.

**The rule:** no trust section ships with zero proof assets. If proof doesn't exist yet,
the section doesn't go live yet — write a smaller, true claim instead.

**The liar test:** would a company selling rebadged catalog parts be able to write this
exact sentence? If yes, it proves nothing. *"Built to our spec"* passes the liar test —
anyone can say it. *"Held to ±0.0005 on the spline OD"* does not.

## Rule 3: The reader is the builder, not us

The failed draft was 90% we/our/us. The customer never appeared. Nobody buys because a
company is proud of itself.

Aim for roughly **30% SYP-visible, 70% builder-visible** by feel. The builder should
recognize their own car and their own problem in the copy.

- Weak: "SYPerformance manufactures its own parts."
- Strong: "You snapped an axle at 600. The replacement snapped too. That's the part we
  started with."

Name the failure the reader has actually had. Broken axle on a launch. Carrier flex. Third
gear synchro. Cracked collector. Specificity here does more for trust than any badge.

## Rule 4: Headlines must parse on the first read

The original hero — *"We make the drivetrain parts that break first"* — reads as **our
parts break first**. A headline that requires a second pass is a dead headline.

**Headline checklist:**

1. Read it once, fast, out of context. What does it mean?
2. Can any noun be misread as the subject of the wrong verb?
3. Does it name a platform, a part, or a number? Concrete beats clever.
4. Under 10 words.

Working directions:

- "Halfshafts that hold what you're making."
- "Your build has a weak link. We machine it."
- "[Number] whp. Same axles." — only if provably true.

## Rule 5: Athletic sentences

Precision as a brand value has to show up in the writing itself. Loose sentences undercut a
precision claim.

- Cut: *that, very, really, just, in order to, the fact that, it should be noted*.
- Cut most *-ly* adverbs; find the stronger verb.
- Break compound sentences joined by *and/but/so*.
- Short declaratives carry authority. Fragments are fine. Machine-shop cadence, not
  marketing cadence.
- Read every paragraph aloud. Any sentence needing two breaths gets split.

## Rule 6: Never overclaim

The fastest way to destroy trust with this audience is one specification that turns out to
be false. **This crowd measures things.**

- **Power figures are claims, not guarantees** (Spencer, 2026-08-26 — this amends the
  original rule, which read "no power figure without a dyno sheet or a named car behind it").
  A power number may appear in a description as what the part is **designed for**. It may never
  be presented as a rating the part is warranted to meet. Say *built for*, never *rated to*, and
  say on the page that it is a design figure rather than a guarantee. A dyno sheet or a named
  car is still what turns a claim into proof — it is now what makes the number *strong*, not
  what makes it *permitted*.
- No tolerance number that engineering hasn't confirmed.
- No "in stock" language on built-to-order parts.
- No lead time that isn't currently being met.
- **Warranty terms stay per-product** (Spencer, 2026-08-26). Several products state "1 year to
  the original purchaser with receipt" in their own copy. That is those products' term. Do
  **not** promote it to a site-wide policy, do not put it on `page.warranty` as a blanket
  statement, and do not add it to a product that does not already carry it.

If a claim is unverified, write the smaller true version and move on.

**Underclaiming and over-delivering is the brand.** Overclaiming once costs more than a
hundred good pages earn.

## Page-level proof requirements

**Homepage** — minimum three proof assets above the fold or immediately below it: one real
shop/machine photo, one hard spec number, one named build with a power figure. Platforms
listed explicitly (Honda B/D/H, K, Evo 7/8/9, Evo X, B58). No section that is pure claim.

**Product page** — material spec, key tolerance, fitment list, what's in the box, lead time
stated before checkout, and at least one photo of the actual part (not a render). Failure
mode the part solves, in one sentence.

**About** — the origin failure. What broke, on what car, and what got machined because of
it. This page carries the most trust weight on the whole site and should read like a shop
story, not a company profile.

**FAQ / shipping / lead times** — plain, unhedged, specific. Trust is won in the boring
pages. A published lead time that is met beats every trust badge on the internet.

---

## Ship checklist

Run before any SYP page goes live:

- [ ] No sentence defined by what we don't do
- [ ] Every trust claim has a number, photo, name, or process step attached
- [ ] Headline parses correctly on one fast read
- [ ] Builder appears in the copy, not just SYP
- [ ] A specific failure mode is named somewhere on the page
- [ ] No claim that engineering can't verify today
- [ ] Lead times and stock status accurate as written
- [ ] Twinkie words cut; sentences read aloud clean
- [ ] Nothing on the page could have been written by a rebadger

**If any box fails, the page isn't done.**

---

## How to run it

Two modes. Say which one is running.

**WRITE** — new or replacement copy. Draft, then run the ship checklist against the draft
before showing it. Report which proof assets the page still needs; never invent them.

**AUDIT** — existing copy. Pull the copy **from the rendered page**, never from the theme
source or the admin: saved section settings override schema defaults, and a grep over the
source misses them. Report per page: the violation quoted verbatim, the rule it breaks, and
the affirmative rewrite. Rank by how much money the page sits in front of.

Both modes end the same way: **what shipped, what is blocked on a proof asset, and who owns
each blocker.**

## Standing SYP facts this skill must not violate

Settled, and they outrank a good sentence. Detail in
`projects/briefs/syperformance-redesign/docs/checklist.md`.

- **SYPerformance IS the manufacturer.** It owns the designs, drawings and specs, contracts
  production to them, inspects and warranties. Use *we manufacture, we make, our parts, our
  spec, our drawings*. **Never name a physical shop floor** — no *machined in our own shop*,
  *cut here*, *on our machines*, *our facility*. The first claim is unshakeable. The second
  is checkable, and it is not ours.
- **Rule 2 collides with that, and the collision has one answer.** "A photo of the mill" and
  "hours of machine time" are proof types this brand cannot use, because both assert a
  floor. Reach for the proof that survives: material spec, tolerance, inspection step, named
  car, power held, what is in the box. When a section can only be proved with a shop-floor
  asset, write the smaller true claim.
- **Synchro Solutionz and Comp 1 Clutch are independent brands SYP distributes.** Never *our
  own*, *our line*, *manufactured by us*, *we build*. Never fold synchros or clutches into a
  list of what SYP makes.
- **The confirmed in-house count is 75**, not 110. 52 products are unclassified and resolve
  as resold until Spencer marks them. Publish no count other than 75.
- **Numbers are SY's to supply.** Materials, tolerances, weights, HP ratings, heat treat,
  test data, warranty terms and lead times ship as a flagged gap, never as a plausible
  guess. A 400-word page where the reasoning is real and the numbers are pending beats a
  200-word page with invented numbers.
- **US English throughout.**
