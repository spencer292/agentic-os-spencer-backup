## 2. Neighbor marketing around active jobs

Why it fits: moles migrate and cross lot lines, so the 10-15 homes nearest an active job carry the
same risk. This is the best-qualified prospect list the business has, and it is free to find.

### 2.1 Door hangers (do first)

**Trigger (not every visit):**
1. First visit of each new Quick Fix or TMCP start.
2. First visit on a property where a mole is caught.
Recurring visits do not trigger. Fallback: a weekly Monday batch from Jobber of last week's new
starts and first catches, for any start the tech missed.

**Radius:** nearest 10-15 homes on the same street and adjacent streets, single-family only. Door
handle or door frame; **never in a mailbox** (federal postal rule). `KNOWN` rule, not a preference.

**Runbook (tech, per trigger):**
1. Before leaving the truck, pick 10-15 doors around the job. Walk it; 8-10 minutes (`ESTIMATE`).
2. Write the tech's first name and the expiry date by hand in the marked spaces (Sharpie). One
   print run serves any period because the date is handwritten.
3. Hang. No door-knocking, no conversation required. If someone asks, answer and give the card.
4. Log in Jobber on the job: "Door hanger drop, N hung" (note field) and the street names.
5. Never say or imply that a specific neighbor's house has moles (privacy). Never point at the
   job house.

**Copy rules (all copy passes `claims-gate.md`, then `tool-humanizer`):**
- Headline: "Chemical-free mole control, now serving your street." Subhead: "Safe for pets and
  children." Line: "We're working in your neighborhood this week."
- Include: Got Moles brand, the dedicated tracking number (2.4), QR to the quote page, "283+
  five-star Google reviews" (`KNOWN` 2026-08-21; **re-measure with `gbp-scrape-reviews.mjs` before
  each print run**), "Founded 2017" if age is mentioned. Never bare "15 years".
- Offer with a hand-written expiry. Offer content is Spencer's decision, kept inside published
  tiers: for example waived $150 setup fee on a first Quick Fix or a first-month credit on TMCP.
  Never mention commercial or over-5-acre pricing.
- Back: blank space for the tech's note. Suggested line: "This is [first name] with Got Moles. I
  was just working a lawn down the road and wanted to introduce myself."
- Banned: any trap, kill, lethal, body-gripping, bait, poison, spike or harpoon wording; I-713;
  "#1"/"best"; a client count; "hauled away".
- US English. Courtesy framing is fine ("sorry for the noise" is not needed; visits are quiet).

**Print spec:** 3.5x8.5 in. door hanger with die-cut, 2 sided, 1,000 minimum. Keep the die-cut hole
area clear. Design in Canva (about $9-15/mo, `ESTIMATE`) or Fiverr ($10-55, `ESTIMATE`; require the
editable/vector file). Vendor shown in the course: UZ Marketing, 1,000 for $210 (`ESTIMATE`, 2024-25
course price, re-quote). Print lead time 1-2 weeks (`ESTIMATE`).

**Cost (`ESTIMATE`):** print $0.21 each; labor about $3-5 per start. At an assumed 40 qualifying
starts a month (`UNKNOWN`, pull the real count from Jobber), about 600 hangers: print about $130,
labor about $150, total about $280 a month.

**Expected return (`ESTIMATE`):** the course claims 2-3%; plan on 0.5-1% calls (3-6 per 600), 1-3
booked jobs a month, plus late calls over 1-3 months. Judge only after 90 days or about 2,000
hangers. Kill criterion, set before launch: if CPBJ exceeds the Quick Fix first-month margin
ceiling that `acc-cfo` sets, stop.

**Seasonality:** run now through mid-Nov (fall peak), pause or lean on the TMCP offer Dec-Feb,
resume Mar-May (spring peak).

### 2.2 Customer-lawn yard signs (permission-based pilot)

Small pilot only. Permission-based, so no right-of-way or ordinance exposure.

**Spec:** 18x24 in. corrugated plastic, 2-3 color, 24-in. stakes. Copy: "Chemical-free mole
control / Got Moles / phone + QR" (tracking number and QR path from 2.4). Posture A applies. No
claims beyond the pillars.

**Cost (`ESTIMATE`):** 50 signs at $4.50-6 each = $225-300, plus design. Vendor option from the
course: Dope Marketing (HSA page); get a fresh quote.

**Runbook:**
1. Tech asks at a TMCP or Quick Fix visit: "Could we leave this sign in your front lawn between
   visits? It helps neighbors find us." Only where the lawn is visible from the street and is
   already being treated.
2. Tech places it at the property line, away from the sidewalk and right-of-way. HOA lots: ask the
   customer to confirm HOA allows it; if unsure, skip.
3. Retrieval at the next scheduled visit (recurring schedule makes this free).
4. Log in Jobber: "Yard sign placed" / "retrieved".
5. Optional small discount for the loan, as in the course (steps $10-15 off). **Keep it a separate
   offer from any review ask**; if used, Spencer approves and it must not appear near a review
   request.

**Expected return (`ESTIMATE`):** a few calls per 50 signs; mostly brand support. Pilot 50 for 60
days, read calls on the sign's own tracker. Scale only if CPBJ beats door hangers.

### 2.3 A-frame sidewalk signs: only as adapted

The course puts an A-frame in the roadway ahead of the truck, styled as a "men at work" sign. **Do
not do that**: it is misleading, a traffic and liability risk, and mole visits last 15-30 minutes so
there is no multi-hour event to exploit.

Allowed adaptation only: an A-frame inside the customer's own lawn or driveway (with permission),
on private property, out of the roadway, same copy as the yard sign. Treat it as a yard sign
variant, not a separate program. No purchase until the yard sign pilot reports.

### 2.4 Tracking: set this up before any print order

Without this, neighbor marketing cannot be judged.

1. **CallRail trackers (pool numbers or static), one per source** (about $5/mo each, `ESTIMATE`
   from the course; confirm CallRail plan pricing): Door Hanger, Yard Sign, Gift Card/Referral,
   Gift Drop. Forwarding numbers into other systems drops the source tag (course finding); keep
   source as a CallRail tag and copy it to Jobber at intake.
2. **QR path:** one QR per source pointing at the quote page with a distinct UTM
   (`utm_source=hanger`, `yardsign`, `giftcard`). The landing page is the website, so **brief Roy**
   for the UTM capture and a "how did you hear" field; track the date asked in `open-findings.md`.
   Generate QR codes once and reuse the file.
3. **Promo code fallback:** a printed phrase ("Mention NEIGHBOR") captured by whoever answers the
   phone, for callers who dial the main line.
4. **Jobber lead source values (add as picklist options):** Door hanger, Yard sign, Referral, Gift
   drop, Review/GBP, Missed-call text, Winback text. Phone staff select one at every intake; a
   blank is a defect. This also moves F-01.
5. **Drop log:** one row per hanger drop (date, job #, street, count, tech) in a gitignored sheet
   or Jobber job notes, so a call can be tied to a street. Aggregates only in tracked files.
6. Report monthly: hangers hung, calls by tracker, booked jobs, CPBJ, TMCP vs Quick Fix mix.

### 2.5 Wealthiest-area targeting: partial

The course sorts USPS EDDM routes by household income. Mole damage tracks lawn size, irrigation and
soil, not income. Use EDDM Table view only to find residential routes with high single-family counts
and larger lots in the four territories, cross-checked against Jobber customer density and the

EDDM mass mail itself: skip for now. The course's own junk-removal test lost money on 25,000

Dope Marketing) is the only mailer worth pricing; cost `UNKNOWN`. Door hangers do the same job
cheaper.

Door-knocking: skip (solicitation complaints attach to a 5.0 rating).

## 3. Referral / gift card program

**Rule:** the reward is paid only on a **booked job** from someone else. It is never tied to, or
mentioned alongside, a review. Referral asks and review asks are separate messages on separate days.

**Mechanic (adapted from the course):**
1. Each new TMCP enrollee, and each Quick Fix customer at the visit with the first catch, gets 2
   cards (not 3): "A gift from [giver's first name]: [dollar-off] your first mole control service."
   Tech or office writes the giver's name by hand ("gifted by ___") and an expiry date.
2. Usable once, residential only, by someone other than the named giver. Never states a commercial
   or over-5-acre price.
3. On redemption at booking, the office records the giver's name; Jobber lead source = Referral,
   plus the giver's name in the client note.
4. Reward to the giver once a week, after the referred job is booked: **$25-50** (`ESTIMATE`; local
   coffee/restaurant card or TMCP bill credit; Spencer sets it, `acc-cfo` sanity-checks against
   LTV). Replacement cards only after a first redemption (scarcity; never hand out ten).
5. Reward terms printed on the FAQ sheet ("What is this? What's in it for me? How is it tracked?
   Can I have more?") so the incentive is disclosed.

**Print (`ESTIMATE`):** 250 cards at about $0.40 = $100; thick glossy stock; template in Canva.
Reward cost is incurred only on a booked job, so it is a pure CAC line.

**Expected return (`ESTIMATE`):** 10-20% of givers produce one booked neighbor within 90 days. At
about $25-50 reward against a TMCP of $1,200-1,800 a year, payback is inside one billing month.
`UNKNOWN`: real redemption rate; read it at 90 days.

**Timing:** hand over after the first catch (customer happiest), at TMCP enrollment, and in the
Jan-Mar winback call (section 5).

**Referral outreach rule:** never auto-text a referred person who has not opted in. The referred
person calls in with the card, or the giver shares a link.

## 4. Speed-to-lead and follow-up

The course tool (SnipeyLead / HighLevel) is rejected. The methods transfer; build them on CallRail,
Jobber and the office.

### 4.1 Targets

| Lead type | Target | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Missed phone call | Text within 1 minute | `ESTIMATE` target |
| Web form / Meta instant form (business hours) | Human call back within 5 minutes | `ESTIMATE` target |
| After-hours form | Acknowledgment text immediately; call by 9 AM next business day | `ESTIMATE` target |
| First reply to any customer message | Same business day | `ESTIMATE` target |

Course claims ("78% buy from first responder", "8x in first 5 minutes", "391%") are `UNKNOWN`
provenance; do not quote them. Measure our own: minutes from lead timestamp to first human contact,
weekly, from CallRail and Jobber request timestamps. Rule from the course worth keeping: **call
back, do not text back**, once a form lead is live.

### 4.2 Missed-call text-back

CallRail text features or the call flow's no-answer branch (confirm what the current plan
includes; `UNKNOWN`). Wording:
"Hey, this is Got Moles. Sorry we missed your call. Is this for a home or a business, and about how
many acres?" This serves the standing intake rule (property type and acreage). It is a reply to the
person's own inbound call, so it is service-initiated; keep it to one message, no offer, no price.
Raise ring timeouts in the call flow so calls do not drop to voicemail in 10-20 seconds.

### 4.3 Estimate and quote follow-up cadence

Jobber quote follow-ups (verify plan tier; `UNKNOWN`), at most 3-4 touches total:
- Day 2 after quote sent: email or text, "any questions?".
- Day 5: one more.
- Stop on any reply, answered call or booking. Remove from the sequence at stage change.
- Quiet hours: 8 AM-8 PM local only. Never send an automated message that quotes a price.
- Commercial and over-5-acre: no automated follow-up; Cory owns the bid follow-up.
- Tag "ghosted" = request or quote older than 14 days with no response (Jobber saved filter).

### 4.4 Day-30 last-chance text

For unconverted estimates and requests, only **where marketing text consent exists** (4.6):
"Hi [first name], it's Got Moles. You asked about mole control about a month ago. Is this still
something you want to handle, or should I stop bothering you? Reply STOP to opt out." Course yield
(`ESTIMATE`, self-reported): about 10 replies per 50, 3 genuinely interested. Plan on 3-10 replies
per 100. One send per person, ever.

### 4.5 "Tech in your area Tuesday" winback text

Course source: an open-appointment text (mobile dog groomer idea): real date, real window, implied
scarcity. Got Moles has what the course members lack: each zip has a real service day.

**Rules:**
- Day comes from **actual Jobber visit history by zip** (the service-day lookup built from
  reality), never from `territories.json` or the territory grid (memory: Jobber history 90% vs the
  grid 73%). Verify the day with Spencer and the route owner (Cory/office) before use. No day
  moves are promised; the text says the tech is "in your area", with a window the office confirms
  at booking.
- Only offer a slot that exists. Office confirms capacity in OptimoRoute/Jobber before send.
  Never create false scarcity.
- Segment by zip/service day so responders cluster on one route day.
- Residential only. Property type and acreage are captured at reply.

**Template (draft; Spencer approves exact copy and segment):**
"Hi [first name], it's Got Moles. Our tech will be in your area [Tuesday, Nov 11]. If moles are
back on your lawn, we can fit you in that day. [Offer, e.g. waived setup fee] if you book by
[date]. Reply YES and we'll confirm a time. Reply STOP to opt out."

**Expected reply (`ESTIMATE`):** 3-10 per 100 sent. If the slot fills, offer the next route day;
the goal is reopening the conversation.

### 4.6 Consent and registration: gate before any marketing text

- **Express written consent** to receive marketing texts, with a clear disclosure at capture:
  who is texting, what kind, approximate frequency, "message and data rates may apply", STOP/HELP.
  Web-form submission alone is not consent for marketing texts (course treats it as permission; we
  do not). Capture the consent state per contact in Jobber (tag: `sms-marketing-ok`) with date and
  source.
- **A2P 10DLC brand and campaign registration** under the legal entity (Rainier Power Wash LLC +
  EIN, not the DBA) must be approved before any marketing text. Register honestly; the course's
  thin "A2P funnel" shortcut can get numbers blocked.
- Transactional texts (arrival windows, appointment reminders) are a separate lane with separate
  consent (`projects/briefs/jobber-text-automation/`). **Never send a marketing offer through the
  transactional channel.**
- Voicemail drops and prerecorded calls: banned (section 9).
- Washington RCW 19.190 covers commercial email and texts; CAN-SPAM, TCPA verify-by dates sit in
  `email-playbook.md` (2027-01). `UNKNOWN`: current consent coverage of the Jobber/CallRail lists.
  Audit before the first send; have counsel confirm form wording.
- Opt-outs honored the same day, recorded outside any single tool, exported before any vendor
  change.
- Batch throttle for first sends: about 50 per 30 minutes (course; `ESTIMATE`) to protect sender
  reputation.

## 6. Reviews and GBP

Current: 3 GBP listings, 289 total / 283 five-star, growing about 7 a week (`KNOWN`, measured
2026-08-21; re-measure before publishing). Review acquisition is a field and office behavior; this
seat designs the ask and measures velocity.

**Policy floor (P0, Google review policy):**
- Ask **every** completed customer. No screening by satisfaction ("rate us first" flows are review
  gating and banned), no routing low scores away from Google.
- **No incentive of any kind** for a review: no discount, gift card, coffee or cash. Referral
  rewards (section 3) stay in a separate message and never mention reviews.
- **No quotas** and **no tech-naming credit** (no per-tech bonus for a review naming them).
- A private "tell us how we did" option may appear **alongside** the Google link for everyone, not
  instead of it.

**Ask timing and channel:**
1. In person at the end of a first visit or after a catch: tech shows a **tap card** (NFC) or QR
   linking to that location's direct review link. Course claim: tap cards in person beat text
   automation about 4-5x (`UNKNOWN`, self-reported); test on one tech for 60 days.
2. Follow-up text/email 1-3 days after the visit, one reminder, stop. Service-triggered review
   requests are a gray area under consent rules; send by email or to contacts with consent.
3. Wording: "Thanks for choosing Got Moles. If you have a minute, a Google review helps neighbors
   find us: [link]." Nothing else.
4. **Photo reviews:** a photo of the work or the property (no customer faces without consent) is
   welcome but is only requested, never required or rewarded. Course: about 1 in 5 reviews carries
   a photo.

**GBP hygiene (Roy owns site changes; GBP is this seat):**
- Reply to every review within 2 business days (`ESTIMATE` target): thank, no scripted boilerplate,
  no claims, no mechanism wording; negative reviews get an apology and a phone number, never an
  argument.
- Services/products list filled with specific terms ("mole control", "mole prevention", "TMCP"),
  Posture A wording.
- Hours only when the phone is answered. Q&A seeded with real questions. Post photos regularly.
- Link socials from the listing. Claim and complete Yelp (a tier-1 local asset); do not advertise
  there and do not solicit.
- Fallback plan if a listing is suspended (course: a competitor-reported ban cost 30% of a
  member's revenue): document the recovery contacts and keep the others healthy.

**Map-rank grid:** SEMrush Local Map Rank Tracker or equivalent. 11x11 grid, 10-15 mile radius
around each of the 3 locations, monthly, keywords such as "mole control near me", "mole removal
[city]", "get rid of moles". About $10/mo per campaign, about $30/mo for three (`ESTIMATE`).
Report rank by grid area; fix gaps by adding the missing term to the services list and, naturally,
the replies. **Do not edit reviews or ask customers to include keywords.**

**Expected result (`ESTIMATE`):** slow and compounding. The course's "145 calls in two months"
came from a seasonal business with a $3,800 ticket and does not transfer.

## 7. Commercial gift drops

Supports the 1.5 commercial accounts per month target. Cory owns every bid; this seat supplies the
warm-up.

**Course method (`ESTIMATE`, self-reported):** 5-10 gift drops a week (donuts/cookies, about $8
each) to named dream accounts; weeks 1-2 little traction, then conversations, then inbound calls
citing a drop 2-3 weeks earlier. At 1 per week it is not worth doing.

**Got Moles version:**
1. Targets come from `mkt-whale-hunt` (the 10 whales) and the `mkt-linkedin-nurture` pipeline
   (property managers, HOA, golf, schools, municipal, hospitality). Never drop on a name not in
   either list.
2. Drop = a small gift plus a one-page card (who we are, chemical-free, the insurance certificate
   available on request, Cory's contact). Handwritten note. **No pricing, no mechanism wording.**
3. Cadence (`ESTIMATE`): up to 5 drops a week, spread across accounts in the pipeline; one drop
   per account per quarter unless a conversation is open.
4. Expected lag: 2-3 weeks to the first response, 2-3 months to a bid (`ESTIMATE`).
5. Log each drop in `pipeline.json` or the whale tracker, with date and tracker number (CallRail
   "Gift Drop"). The next LinkedIn touch references the drop only if it fits the voice; never
   "I sent you donuts" as a hook.
6. Gifts to municipal or public-sector staff may be restricted by ethics rules. **Check before
   dropping at any public entity** (`UNKNOWN`); default to no gift for those accounts.
7. Proof loop: ask commercial customers (not as a condition of service) to mention the property in
   a review; share published reviews with prospects in the same sector.
8. Keep the insurance certificate and vendor packet ready (course; commercial buyers screen on
   it). A printed leave-behind folder for Cory's bids is cheap (`ESTIMATE` under $5 each).

**Feeds:** `mkt-linkedin-nurture` runs the supervised outreach sessions and `mkt-whale-hunt` the
weekly touches. This seat sets the targets and the CPBJ view; those skills execute; Spencer approves
every touch and every spend. Cost per commercial account: drops plus Cory's time; treat as
`UNKNOWN` until three accounts have closed.
