# Editor's Pass — *Backing Yourself* (Chapters 1–3)

**Scope:** Continuity, repetition, internal consistency, and structure across the first three chapters. This is a developmental/line-edit read, not a copyedit.

**Headline verdict:** The writing itself is strong — clean, confident, plain-spoken, with good rhythm and well-earned closing lines. But the three chapters do **not** yet hang together as a sequence. There is one structural problem (a duplicated chapter) and one factual contradiction (a tenfold money discrepancy) that a reader will catch immediately, plus a motif that hasn't settled on a single phrase. All are fixable, and fixing them mostly means *cutting and reconciling*, not writing more.

---

## 1. Critical issues (fix before anything else)

### 1.1 Chapter 1 and Chapter 3 are the same story told twice
This is the biggest problem. **Chapter 1 ("The Twenty Minutes") and Chapter 3 ("Down to the Wire") narrate the identical event:** the BBC / White City interview for a spreadsheet role, walking past the training shop, the £70 Excel course, the twenty-minute deal, getting the job, and going back to pay. Nothing material in Chapter 3 is new — it is a re-telling, not a different beat or a deeper layer.

A reader will experience this as the author having forgotten they already told it, or as two drafts of the same chapter left in by mistake. Right now it reads like the second one.

**Recommendation:** Keep **one** version. Chapter 1 is the stronger draft — it has the concrete stakes (£75 down to a fiver, "stomach in knots," "one of the most daunting buildings in London") that Chapter 3 flattens out. Cut Chapter 3 as a standalone chapter and **harvest its one unique asset** — the closing line *"When you back yourself, the door tends to open"* — into Chapter 1's ending, because that line is on-title and lands well (see §4). Then promote the current Chapter 2 forward and find a genuinely *different* second story for the slot Chapter 3 vacates.

If you have a strong reason to keep both (e.g. Chapter 3 is meant to reopen the book from a different angle much later), then it cannot stay at position 3, and it must be reframed so it is visibly a callback ("I've told you about the twenty minutes…") rather than a fresh telling. As written, that framing isn't there.

### 1.2 The money figure contradicts itself — £75 vs £750
- **Chapter 1:** *"seventy-five pounds left to my name"* → £75 − £70 course = *"Five pounds for food until payday."*
- **Chapter 3:** *"seven hundred and fifty pounds to my name."*

That is a **10× discrepancy** on the single most important number in the origin story. In a memoir this is exactly the kind of slip that makes a reader stop trusting the rest.

It's not just a typo to reconcile — **£75 is the correct figure on the evidence of the writing itself**, for two reasons:
1. Chapter 1's emotional engine ("five pounds for food until payday," living on a fiver) only works at £75. At £750, the maths and the drama both collapse.
2. Chapter 3 *also* undercuts its own figure: it opens on *"the cold that comes with an empty bank account"* and is literally titled *"Down to the Wire"* — yet £750 minus a £70 course leaves £680, which is neither empty nor down to the wire. So £750 contradicts Chapter 3 internally as well as contradicting Chapter 1.

**Recommendation:** Standardise on **£75** everywhere. Delete £750.

## 3. Are you repeating yourself? Yes — in two ways

1. **Whole-anecdote repetition** — the BBC story (§1.1). This is the serious one.
2. **Structural/rhetorical repetition** — every chapter ends on a short aphoristic maxim:
   - Ch 1: *"…that is exactly where the biggest steps happen."*
   - Ch 2: *"…the moment the business started becoming a business."* / *"You have to believe."*
   - Ch 3: *"When you back yourself, the door tends to open."*

   The maxim-button is a good device and a real strength of your voice. But if **every** chapter lands the same way, by chapter four or five it becomes a formula the reader can feel coming. Keep it, but vary the *shape* of your endings deliberately — let one or two chapters end on an image or an unresolved beat instead of a lesson.

---

## 4. The "You have to believe" / "back yourself" motif needs to settle

You have a recurring thesis phrase, which is good — repetition of a *signature line* is a feature, not a bug. But it is currently **two different phrases**, and one of them isn't the book's title:

- Ch 1 & Ch 2 use **"You have to believe."**
- Ch 3 (and the title) uses **"back yourself."**

The book is called ***Backing Yourself***. The signature refrain should almost certainly be **"back yourself"**, with "you have to believe" demoted to ordinary supporting texture (or cut). Right now the load-bearing repeated line and the title are pulling in slightly different directions.

**One smaller attribution wrinkle:** Chapter 2's callback says *"I had said it to myself in a shop doorway years before, and here it was again, the same sentence doing the same work."* But in Chapter 1, "You have to believe" is the narrator's *retrospective* reflection after the scene — it is not shown as a line he said to himself in the doorway in the moment. So Chapter 2 retro-fits a quote that Chapter 1 didn't actually plant. If you want the Chapter 2 callback to work (and it's a lovely beat — "the same sentence doing the same work in a different room"), then **plant the line as an in-scene thought inside Chapter 1's doorway moment** so Chapter 2 has something real to call back to.

## 5. Structure — does the sequence hang together?

Chronology as currently ordered:
- **Ch 1:** the origin (BBC, before any companies)
- **Ch 2:** *"Two years in"* — first hire, David (inside the business)
- **Ch 3:** *"right at the start, before any of the companies existed"* — back to the BBC origin again

So the timeline goes **start → +2 years → back to start**, and the jump back lands on the same event we opened with. That's a double whiplash: a backward jump *and* a repeat. Even setting aside the duplication, Chapter 3's "before any of the companies existed" framing fights Chapter 2's forward momentum.

**Recommendation:** Once Chapter 3 is cut/merged (§1.1), the spine becomes clean: **Ch 1 origin → Ch 2 first hire**, both moving forward, both carrying the "back yourself" refrain. Whatever fills the new Chapter 3 should continue *forward* in time (or be a clearly-signposted flashback), not re-tell the opening.

## 6. Line-level notes (minor — address after the structural fixes)

- **Ch 1, opening:** *"Before the IT companies, before any of it, I had…"* — strong opening. The later *"before any of the companies existed"* in Ch 3 echoes it almost verbatim; another reason the two reuse the same furniture.
- **Ch 1:** *"with my stomach in knots and just enough to get through the door"* — nice. This concreteness is what Ch 3 lacks; preserve it as the canonical version.
- **Ch 2:** *"you cannot scale fear. You can only scale trust"* — the best line in the three chapters. No conflict; keep it prominent.
- **Ch 2:** *"a fortnight to prove me right. He proved me right in a week."* — clean and effective.
- **Ch 3:** *"There is a particular kind of cold that comes with an empty bank account. I have felt it more than once."* — good line, and *"more than once"* hints you have other broke-moment stories. That is the kind of *genuinely different* episode that should fill the Chapter 3 slot, rather than re-telling the BBC one.

---

## 7. Prioritised fix list

1. **Cut the duplication.** Remove Chapter 3 as a re-telling of the BBC story; keep Chapter 1 as the canonical version. *(Critical — §1.1)*
2. **Fix the money.** Standardise on **£75** everywhere; delete £750. *(Critical — §1.2)*
3. **Rescue the good line.** Move *"When you back yourself, the door tends to open"* into Chapter 1's ending. *(Quick win — §1.1 / §4)*
4. **Settle the refrain on "back yourself"** to match the title; demote or cut "You have to believe." *(High — §4)*
5. **Plant the doorway line in Chapter 1** so Chapter 2's callback has a real antecedent. *(Medium — §4)*
6. **Find a different episode** for the vacated Chapter 3 slot (the "more than once" broke moment is a ready candidate), keeping the timeline moving forward. *(Medium — §5)*
7. **Vary chapter endings** so the maxim-button doesn't become a formula. *(Low, ongoing — §3)*

**Bottom line:** the prose is ready; the *architecture* isn't yet. The single highest-leverage move is deleting the duplicate origin story and reconciling £75 — do those two and the three chapters immediately read as one coherent, trustworthy opening.
