### Ch4 — Burnout

**M1 — Through-line / coherence — 2/10.** I cannot state the book's claim in one sentence, and that is the finding, not a softness on my part. Ch1 is self-improvement, Ch2 is adventure, Ch3 is a technical tutorial, Ch4 is recovery memoir. Even under the most generous inferred spine (the recovered-operator arc), **Ch3 does no job on any version of it** — it could be deleted with zero loss to the argument, which is the textbook M1 down-marker. Three chapters could each plausibly belong to a real book; together they're a collection, not a book.

**M2 — Macro arc / transformation — 2/10.** Nothing escalates and the chapters are interchangeable in order. Worse, the natural arc is actively inverted: the crisis that should set the stakes (Burnout, Ch4) arrives *after* the solution it produced (Morning Routine, Ch1). The reader meets the cure before they've felt the disease. Measured against `icp.md`, the draft doesn't move Mark — and its current opening (5am routine, "discipline is only hard at the start") hits a named ICP turn-off, so it can lose him before the arc even starts.

**M3 — Continuity — 9/10.** I built the fact inventory and checked it across chapters: no contradicted figures, dates, names, or self-claims. 2,500+ skydives, 2021, the 90-hour weeks all appear once and consistently. The register is genuinely clean. The only caveat is honest thinness — the chapters share so little world that there's little surface for contradiction; this is a high score earned by absence of error, not by tightly interlocking facts.

**M4 — Repetition & redundancy — 7/10.** No story is told twice — each anecdote appears once. The single cross-chapter overlap is the recovery practices: **breathwork** appears in Ch1 (routine) and Ch4 (recovery), and **cold** appears as "cold shower" (Ch1) and "cold exposure" (Ch4). This is an unwitting overlap, not a deliberate callback — neither mention knows the other exists. It's low-severity and the fix is to *connect* (make Ch1's practice explicitly the thing that came out of Ch4's recovery), which converts a weak repeat into a deliberate callback rather than cutting anything.

**M5 — Pacing & peak distribution — 4/10.** See the Peak Map. The failure here isn't the classic front-loaded clustering — it's that **half the book has no pulse at all**: Ch1 and Ch3 carry no emotional or device peak, and Ch3 in particular is dead air (a how-to with no narrative). As ordered (flat / high / flat / high) the energy stutters rather than builds, and the strongest beat (Burnout) lands last without anything having built to make it a climax.

**M6 — Promise & payoff — 3/10.** There is no opening promise, so by definition nothing is paid off — and a book that promises nothing can't deliver. The most fertile thread in the whole draft — Ch4's "a hard look at *why* I was working that way in the first place" — is opened in the final chapter and immediately dropped. That question is arguably the real book; right now it's an orphaned setup at the very end.

**M7 — Voice consistency — 4/10.** Ch2 and Ch4 read like one author — Roy's flowing baseline with athletic landings placed at the peaks. Ch1 reads like a different, more generic hand (prescriptive, staccato throughout, spirit-thin, lesson-as-instruction). Ch3 reads like a third hand entirely (a software manual). The jump from Ch3's tutorial register straight into Ch4's memoir is tone whiplash. (These are flagged as *cross-chapter* outliers; the internal craft fix for Ch1 and Ch3 is a `writ-review` job, not this pass's — see The Moves.)

## The Structural Prescription

**What's working (protect):**
- **Ch4 (Burnout)** is the spine candidate and the best-voiced chapter — consequence-led, athletic landing placed correctly. Protect it; build the book around it.
- **Ch2 (Skydiving)** is a genuine, well-told peak with real specifics. Keep it — it just needs a job on the spine.
- **Continuity is clean.** Don't introduce contradictions when you reorder and connect.
- **The breathwork/cold thread** is an asset in disguise — two mentions of the same practice are a callback waiting to be wired up, not a problem to cut.

**What's not:**
- No spine, so the book "goes round the houses" — exactly the feeling reported. (M1, blocking.)
- The arc is inverted: the cure precedes the crisis. (M2.)
- Ch3 belongs to a different book. (M1/M7.)
- The opening loses the target reader on a named turn-off. (M2 vs `icp.md`.)
- The book's most promising question ("why was I working that way?") is buried at the end and dropped. (M6.)

**The moves (highest leverage first, each located):**

1. **Name the spine and write a one-page intro that states it.** Commit to one promise — the strongest the material supports is the recovered-operator arc: *the cost of overwork, and the way of living and operating that came out of recovering from it.* Without this the rest is rearranging deck chairs. (Fixes M1 — the blocking defect — and gives M6 something to pay off.)

2. **Reorder so crisis precedes cure: Burnout → recovery → the rebuilt self.** Move **Ch4 (Burnout) to the front** as the stakes/origin. Follow with **Ch1 (Morning Routine)**, reframed as *what he rebuilt afterward* rather than a standalone productivity list. Then **Ch2 (Skydiving)** as the rebuilt self making clear decisions under pressure. This is the chapter-order analogue of emotional sequencing — start where the weight is. (Fixes M2; lets the Ch4 peak become an earned origin instead of a stranded climax.)

3. **Cut or radically repurpose Ch3 (Spreadsheets).** It fits no version of the spine. Either drop it, or shrink it to a brief illustration *inside* a business-systems chapter that serves the recovered-operator argument (e.g. "the systems that let the business run without me"). As a standalone how-to it's off-spine and off-voice. (Fixes the largest M1 and M7 drag in one move.)

4. **Wire the breathwork/cold thread into a callback.** In the reordered book, Ch4 (now early) introduces breathwork and cold as recovery; the Morning Routine chapter then *returns* to them as the established daily practice — one line referencing the origin rather than re-introducing them cold. (Converts Repetition Register #1–#2 from unwitting repeats into a deliberate callback; lifts M4 and M2.)

5. **Promote and pay off the buried question.** Pull Ch4's "*why* I was working that way in the first place" up into the intro as the book's opening promise, and resolve it across the arc. (Fixes the orphaned setup in M6.)

6. **(Route, not a structural move) Send Ch1 and Ch3 to `writ-review` for internal craft.** Their voice/spirit problems (Ch1 generic-prescriptive; Ch3 manual-toned) are per-piece craft issues. I'm flagging them as cross-chapter outliers here; the line-level fix belongs to `writ-review`, not this manuscript pass.
