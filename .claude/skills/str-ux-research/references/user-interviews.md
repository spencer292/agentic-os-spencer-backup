# User interviews and jobs-to-be-done synthesis

Self-contained reference for `str-ux-research` Mode C. The switch interview finds why someone
changed from one solution to another, in their words, and it is the only mode in this skill that
produces real user data.

## Who to interview

Six to eight people is enough to see patterns. Prioritise in this order.

1. Recent buyers, ideally within the last three months while the memory is intact
2. People who enquired and did not buy
3. People who left the client for a competitor
4. People who are in the middle of deciding right now

Avoid the client's favourite customers. They tell you the story the client already believes.

## The switch interview

The interview is a timeline reconstruction, not an opinion survey. You are rebuilding what
happened, in order, from the first moment of dissatisfaction to the purchase. People are
unreliable about why they did things and reliable about what happened.

### Opening

"I want to understand the story of how you ended up with [solution]. I am going to ask you to
walk me back through it in order. There are no wrong answers and I am not selling you anything."

### The timeline anchor

- Think back to when you first started looking for something like this. What was going on at the
  time that made you start looking?
- When was the first moment you thought the way you were doing it was not working?
- What happened between that thought and you actually doing something about it?

### Push, what drove them away from the old way

- What was getting most frustrating about what you were using before?
- Was there a specific event that made it worse? A deadline, a complaint, a cost, a failure?
- What did you try first to fix it without changing anything?

### Pull, what attracted them to the new way

- When you started looking, what were you hoping would be different?
- What did you see or read that made you think this one might work?
- What did you picture yourself doing once you had it?

### Anxiety, what nearly stopped them

- What worried you about switching?
- What almost made you not do it?
- What did you need to see or be told before you were comfortable?

### Habit, what kept them where they were

- What was good enough about the old way?
- Who or what would have been disrupted by changing?

### The decision itself

- Who else was involved in the decision?
- What else did you look at, and why did you rule them out?
- What was the moment you decided? What tipped it?
- What did you do immediately before you got in touch?

### Language

- If you were telling a colleague about this problem, how would you describe it?
- What would you have typed into a search box back then?

That last pair is the highest-value question in the script. It feeds both the language bank and
`str-keyword-strategy`.

## Per-interview synthesis

Capture eight fields per interview, immediately after, before the next one.

| Field | What goes in it |
|-------|-----------------|
| Timeline | First dissatisfaction, first search, evaluation, switch, present day |
| Struggling moment | The specific situation that created the need |
| Push | The two or three things driving them away from the old solution |
| Pull | The two or three things attracting them to the new one |
| Anxieties | What almost stopped them |
| Habits | What almost kept them where they were |
| The job | The progress they were trying to make, phrased without naming any product |
| Language | Exact words and phrases, quoted |

The four forces are push, pull, anxiety and habit. People change when push and pull together
outweigh anxiety and habit. A site that only does pull, which is most sites, is fighting with one
hand.

## Cross-interview roll-up

After all interviews, produce four outputs for the brief.

**Top tasks.** What people came to do, ranked by how often it appeared and how much it mattered.
Keep the list to seven or fewer. Each recommended page in the brief names one of these.

**Objection list.** Every anxiety that appeared more than once, with the evidence a visitor would
need to see to clear it, and where on the site that evidence belongs.

**Language bank.** Verbatim phrases grouped by problem, outcome and category name. Never
paraphrase into marketing register. This is what `mkt-copywriting` and `str-keyword-strategy`
read.

**Jobs statement.** One or two sentences per segment in the form: when [situation], I want to
[motivation], so I can [expected outcome]. Written from the interviews, never invented.

## The hypothesis rule

If Mode C did not run, none of the four outputs above exists as evidence. Write the interim
versions from the discovery interview and the analytics, prefix every line with `Hypothesis:`,
and state in the brief header that user research is outstanding. Never fabricate a quote, a
percentage, or a persona detail. An unattributed quote in a discovery brief is a fabrication that
gets repeated in copy and then repeated back to a real customer.
