# Target Profiles — Got Moles Corporate LinkedIn

Seven buyer profiles, drawn from `brand_context/icp.md` Segment 2 and validated against the
first real prospect pull on 2026-07-06 (`projects/tool-browser/2026-07-06_linkedin-corporate-prospects.md`).

Two things are true of every profile here and worth holding onto while you search. First, the
buyer is never solving an emotional problem — mole damage is a professional embarrassment and a
budget line, not a ruined weekend. Second, the person is rarely the account: one community
manager carries a portfolio, so a single relationship can be worth a dozen sites. That's why
portfolio signals (certifications, "portfolio manager" in a title, a management company rather
than a single property) outrank everything else when ranking a list.

## 1. HOA / Community Association Managers

**Tier 1 — the highest-fit profile in the campaign.** The July pull found ten of these, almost
all 2nd-degree with a warm path in.

**Who they are:** Community Association Manager (CAM), Senior CAM, Portfolio Community Manager,
Executive CAM, or Director of Community Management at a management company.

**Why they buy:** They manage a portfolio of communities, not one property. Mole mounds on an
entry lawn generate board emails, and board emails land on them. They need it handled before it
becomes their problem, on a contract, with documentation they can forward to a board.

**Qualifying signals:**
- Certifications: **CMCA, AMS, PCAM** — these are the strongest portfolio-scale signal available. PCAM is the senior tier.
- Title contains "portfolio"
- Employer is a management company (Associa, The Management Trust, Trestle, Kappes Miller, Agynbyte, VIS Group, Yates Wood & MacDonald) rather than a single HOA
- CAI (Community Associations Institute) membership or WA chapter activity

**The real account:** the management company behind them. Landing one CAM opens the firm.

**Search strings:**
- `community association manager Washington`
- `CMCA AMS PCAM Seattle`
- `portfolio community manager Seattle OR Tacoma`
- `HOA manager Washington`
- `Associa Washington` / `The Management Trust Washington` (company-first searches find the whole team)

**Their language:** communities, boards, common areas, reserves, vendor management, RFPs, walkthroughs.

**Message angle:** one contract covering every community they run; handled before spring mound
season instead of after a board complains; a written report per visit they can forward straight
to a board; chemical-free so it's safe in common areas where kids and dogs are.

## 2. Commercial Property Management

**Tier 1.** Larger portfolios, longer procurement, bigger contracts.

**Who they are:** Property Manager, Senior Property Manager, Facilities Manager, Director of
Facilities, Grounds Manager, Operations Manager — at a CRE firm or on a corporate campus.

**Why they buy:** Grounds are part of the asset's presentation. Mole activity creates trip
hazards on public-facing land, which is a liability conversation, not a cosmetic one.

**Qualifying signals:**
- Employer is a CRE firm (CBRE, JLL, Kidder Mathews, Colliers) or a large corporate campus
- Title includes facilities, grounds, or operations at a company with real acreage
- Mentions of vendor management, RFPs, or service contracts in their About

**Watch for:** procurement gates. This buyer often can't sign alone. Ask early who else is
involved rather than pitching for six weeks to someone with no authority.

**Search strings:**
- `facilities manager Seattle` / `facilities director Bellevue`
- `commercial property manager Washington`
- `grounds manager Washington`
- `campus operations manager Seattle`

**Message angle:** liability and presentation; a specialist rather than a generalist pest
contractor; scope of work, service agreement, reporting; reliability — no no-shows.

## 3. Golf Course Superintendents

**Tier 1 by fit, cold by network.** The July pull put these mostly at 3rd degree — high value,
no warm path, so the message has to carry more weight on credibility.

**Who they are:** Golf Course Superintendent, Director of Agronomy, Grounds Superintendent,
Regional Superintendent. Often carry **CGCS** (Certified Golf Course Superintendent).

**Why they buy:** Moles wreck greens and fairways, chemical-free is non-negotiable near play, and
private clubs sign annual contracts. Turf pride is real and personal for this buyer.

**Qualifying signals:**
- CGCS certification
- GCSAA membership (national association) or the WA chapter
- Private or semi-private club rather than a municipal course (budget and contract appetite)

**Their language:** turf, greens, fairways, agronomy, playability, IPM (integrated pest
management), member experience.

**Search strings:**
- `golf course superintendent Washington`
- `CGCS Washington` / `director of agronomy Seattle`
- `golf course superintendent Tacoma OR Olympia`

**Message angle:** speak turf, not pest control. Chemical-free matters near play and near
members. Damage during member season is the pain. Credibility-forward, because there's no
mutual connection doing the work for you.

## 4. Schools, Campuses & Athletic Fields

**Tier 2.** Slower, more procedural, but chemical-free is close to mandatory here — which is the
whole Got Moles position.

**Who they are:** Grounds Supervisor, Facilities Director, Athletic Facilities Manager,
Director of Operations at a school district, private school, or college.

**Why they buy:** Child safety makes chemical methods a non-starter. Athletic fields with mole
damage are an injury risk, which turns this into a liability decision with a deadline attached
to the sports calendar.

**Qualifying signals:** district-level rather than single-school titles; athletic field
responsibility in the headline; a private school or campus with visible grounds.

**Watch for:** public districts run formal procurement and bid cycles. Expect a long runway and
a paper process. Worth it for the contract size, but don't mistake slow for uninterested.

**Search strings:**
- `grounds supervisor school district Washington`
- `facilities director school Seattle OR Tacoma`
- `athletic facilities manager Washington`

**Message angle:** chemical-free and safe around children first, always. Then field safety, then
the calendar — get it handled before the season, not during it.

## 5. Municipal Parks & Public Grounds

**Tier 2.** Chemical-free is often policy, not preference. Procurement is the barrier.

**Who they are:** Parks Superintendent, Parks & Recreation Director, Public Works Grounds
Manager, Cemetery Superintendent.

**Why they buy:** Public land, public scrutiny, and frequently a written policy against chemical
treatment. Trip hazards on public ground are a liability exposure.

**Qualifying signals:** city or county employer; parks, recreation, or public works in the title.

**Watch for:** this is the slowest profile in the campaign. Formal bids, budget cycles, sometimes
council approval. Treat it as relationship-building for a future cycle rather than a near-term
close, and set expectations with Spencer accordingly.

**Search strings:**
- `parks superintendent Washington`
- `parks and recreation director Washington`
- `public works grounds Washington`

**Message angle:** chemical-free as policy fit; documentation and accountability for public
records; experience across 60+ Western WA cities.

## 6. Hotels, Resorts & Hospitality Grounds

**Tier 2.** Small in count, high in standards.

**Who they are:** Director of Engineering, Property Operations Manager, General Manager, Grounds
Manager — at a resort, golf resort, hotel with grounds, or a wedding/event venue.

**Why they buy:** Grounds are part of what the guest is paying for. A mound-covered lawn in a
wedding photo is a direct revenue problem, and event dates are immovable.

**Qualifying signals:** resort or venue rather than a city-center hotel with no land; event and
wedding business visible on the company page.

**Search strings:**
- `director of engineering resort Washington`
- `property operations manager hotel Washington`
- `event venue grounds manager Washington`

**Message angle:** guest experience and photography; reliability against fixed event dates;
discreet, scheduled service that doesn't disrupt guests.

## 7. Landscaping & Lawn Care

**Referral partners, not buyers — and this distinction changes the entire message.**

**Who they are:** Owners and operations managers at landscape maintenance, lawn care, and
grounds-care companies across Western WA.

**Why they matter:** They are on the properties every week and get asked about moles constantly.
They don't want to do mole work — it's specialist, time-consuming, and it makes their own results
look bad when they fail at it. Subcontracting to a specialist protects the result they're
being paid for.

**The critical distinction:** pitching a landscaper as though they're the buyer reads as a
competitor prospecting them, and it burns the relationship. The approach is partnership: you
handle moles so their turf work isn't undermined, they keep the client relationship, nobody
poaches anybody. That framing has to be explicit and early in the message.

**Qualifying signals:** company does maintenance rather than design-build only; operates in the
Got Moles service area; owner-operator or ops manager (someone who can decide).

**Search strings:**
- `landscape maintenance owner Washington`
- `lawn care company Seattle OR Tacoma OR Puyallup`
- `grounds maintenance manager Washington`

**Message angle:** you protect their result; they keep their client; a clear referral or
subcontract arrangement. Never anything that sounds like competing for the same work.

## Warm-route hubs

connection on prospect after prospect across the HOA and landscaping searches:

- **Patrick LaCroix** — the shared connection to roughly eight of the HOA managers found
- **Nick Granberg**
- **Brandon Sechrist**
- **Mark Pyrah**

Two ways to use this. Tactically, naming a genuine shared connection in a connection note lifts
acceptance materially and is true, which satisfies the no-fabrication rule. Strategically, one
warm introduction from a hub into a management company beats a dozen cold requests — so when a
session surfaces a cluster of prospects behind the same hub, raise the introduction route with
Spencer before defaulting to cold outreach.

Recurring mutuals in future searches are new hubs. Note them in the session log when they appear.

## Geography

Western Washington — King, Pierce, and Thurston counties primarily. Priority cities by ICP
density: Sammamish, Bellevue, Kirkland, Seattle, Tacoma, Puyallup, Auburn, Federal Way, Renton,
Kent, Issaquah, Enumclaw. The service area covers 60+ cities across Western WA.

A prospect outside the service area is a disqualification regardless of how good the fit looks
on paper.

## Universal disqualifiers

Drop before research, not after:

- Outside the Western WA service area
- Already a Got Moles customer (the Jobber check in Step 3 catches this)
- Already in `pipeline.json` as contacted, rejected, or dormant
- Recruiters, MLM, or coaching-adjacent profiles regardless of stated title
- A pest control company — that's a competitor, not a prospect
- Residential homeowner with no commercial role — wrong channel entirely; those leads come
  through CallRail and Jobber
- Job title suggests no grounds responsibility at all (pure finance, HR, IT)

## Ranking a raw list

When a search returns 25 people and the session has room for 12, rank on these in order:

1. **Portfolio scale** — how many sites does one relationship reach?
2. **Warm route** — is there a genuine mutual connection, ideally one of the four hubs?
3. **Connection degree** — 2nd beats 3rd; 2nd degree with a hub mutual is the best row on the page
4. **Recent activity** — someone who posts gives you a real hook and a way to warm them without messaging
5. **Geography** — priority cities first
6. **Decision authority** — owner, director, or senior over coordinator or assistant

A 2nd-degree PCAM at a management company who posted last week is worth more than five cold
3rd-degree names, and the session should be spent accordingly.
