# Example Messages — the quality bar

Read these before drafting. They're from the calibration batch written 2026-07-06
(`projects/tool-browser/2026-07-06_linkedin-outreach-drafts.md`) — never sent, so treat them as
a standard for construction rather than proven performance. When a message Spencer actually
approves outperforms one of these, replace it here.

What to notice across all four: every one opens with something true and specific to that person,
none of them compliment the recipient, and no two share a sentence.

---

## 1. Warm route, named mutual — HOA portfolio manager

**Mark Johnson — CAM, Kappes Miller Management (Seattle) · warm: Patrick LaCroix**

**Connection note (283 chars):**
> Hi Mark — Patrick LaCroix is a shared connection, which is how I found you. I run Got Moles, the mole-only specialists in Western WA. Kappes Miller manages a lot of turf that moles love right now. Would value a quick chat about keeping your communities mound-free. — Spencer

**Follow-up (after accept):**
> Appreciate the connect, Mark. Quick reason I reached out: we're the only crew in Western Washington that does moles and nothing else — going on 7 years, close to 5,000 properties, 283+ five-star reviews. For a firm like Kappes Miller the pitch is simple: one managed contract covers every community you run, we handle the spring mound season before boards start emailing you, and you get a written report after every visit. Chemical-free, so it's safe around kids, pets and common areas. Worth 15 minutes to see if a couple of your problem communities are a fit?

**Why it works:** the mutual is named and real, so the "how I found you" is honest. The firm is
named. "Before boards start emailing you" is the pain in his language, not ours. The ask is 15
minutes, not a meeting.

## 2. Certification as the qualifying signal

**Samantha Oldham, PCAM — Sr CAM, Trestle Community Management · warm: Nick Granberg**

**Connection note:**
> Hi Samantha — Nick Granberg is a mutual connection. I lead Got Moles, mole-only specialists across Western WA. With a PCAM-level portfolio at Trestle, you're the one who hears it when moles tear up a community's common areas each spring. Would love to compare notes. — Spencer

**Follow-up:**
> Thanks for connecting, Samantha. You manage enough communities that mole damage is a when-not-if — usually right before someone on the board spots the mounds on the entry lawn. We're mole-exclusive (that's all we do, going on 7 years and nearly 5,000 properties), we work on an annual managed contract so it's handled year-round instead of call-by-call, and every visit ends with a report you can forward straight to a board. Chemical-free too. If Trestle has two or three communities that flare up every year, I'd love to take those off your plate.

**Why it works:** PCAM isn't decoration — it's used as evidence of portfolio scale, which shows
the message was written by someone who understands her industry. "When-not-if" and "forward
straight to a board" are her world. The close names a realistic first step: two or three
communities, not the whole portfolio.

## 3. Reputation angle, no compliment

**Amy Gannon — Sr CAM, Yates, Wood & MacDonald · warm: Patrick LaCroix**

**Connection note:**
> Hi Amy — Patrick LaCroix is a shared connection. I run Got Moles, mole-only specialists in Western WA. Yates, Wood & MacDonald has been trusted with Seattle properties for generations — we're the specialist you call so mole damage never lands on your desk. Would value a chat. — Spencer

**Follow-up:**
> Thanks for connecting, Amy. A firm with Yates, Wood & MacDonald's reputation can't afford a contractor who no-shows or leaves a job half-done — that reflects on you, not them. We only do moles (going on 7 years, close to 5,000 properties, 283+ five-star reviews), we show up on a set schedule, and we leave a written report every visit so you and the board always know what happened. Chemical-free, safe around residents and pets. If a few of your communities fight moles every spring, I'd like to be the name you stop worrying about.

**Why it works:** it compliments the *firm's* standing as a factual observation and turns it
into her risk — "that reflects on you, not them" — which is the actual psychology of this buyer.
"The name you stop worrying about" is a close about reliability, which is what she buys on.

## 4. Cold, no mutual — credibility has to carry it

**Golf course superintendent, 3rd degree, private club**

Pattern rather than verbatim, since the anchor changes per club:

**Connection note:** open on the specific club and its turf situation → mole-only specialist in
Western WA → chemical-free matters near play and near members → a conversation about keeping the
course clean through member season.

**Follow-up:** speak turf, not pest control. Greens and fairways, not "your property". Damage
during member season is the cost. The proof stack does the work a mutual connection would
otherwise do, so lead with the specialism and the numbers earlier than you would in a warm
message.

**Why it's separated out:** with no mutual connection, the first line has nowhere to hide.
It has to demonstrate that you know what a superintendent's season looks like, or the message
reads as generic regardless of how the rest is written.

## The disqualifying sentence

If any draft contains a sentence that would survive being pasted into another prospect's
message unchanged, that draft isn't finished. That's the test.
