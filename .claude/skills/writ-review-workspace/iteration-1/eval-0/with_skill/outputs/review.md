## 4. Interaction & contrast flags

- **⚑ Athletic ↔ author voice — TOTAL override (marquee flag).** The baseline voice is not merely diluted; it is *absent*. The device has replaced the author entirely. §7.2 names this as "the single most common way the method is applied wrongly" — and this passage is the textbook specimen.
- **⚑ The contrast law — no valleys, so no peaks.** This is the direct answer to "does it land?" The collapse, the sleepless night, and the triumph are all delivered at *identical* intensity and identical rhythm. When everything is a peak, nothing is. The three moments that should spike read at the same flat-line volume — which is exactly why the gut-punch doesn't connect. **Missing contrast is a defect even when each line is individually clean.**
- Statement-question saturation — N/A (none present).
- Lexicon over-coining — N/A (none present).
- **Note the shape of the failure:** it is *not* "too few devices." Adding more devices would repeat the original error in a new key. The failure is *one* device at maximum dose crowding out voice and contrast.

## 5. The prescription

### ✅ What's working (keep it)
1. **The opening instinct is right.** "The deal collapsed." leads with consequence, not journalism (P1) — and as a three-word fire opener it *earns* its staccato. This is the one place the device is correctly placed.
2. **It's lean.** No fatty filler, no "that"-padding (§2.6). The toxin-removal is genuinely clean — that discipline is worth keeping.
3. **The bones of an arc exist.** Collapse → night → rebuild is a real three-beat skeleton. The structure is salvageable; only the *texture* and *sequencing* are broken.

### ✗ What's not working
1. **Wall-to-wall athletic writing (D7).** Every sentence is a three-word peak → no peaks survive (contrast law). This is *why it doesn't land*.
2. **No baseline voice / no valleys.** The author's natural texture is erased; there is nothing flat for the spikes to stand against.
3. **Chronological, not emotional, sequencing (D4).** Straight birth-to-triumph line kills anticipation.
4. **CTA close, not a call to mirror (D8 / P2).** "Never quit. Keep going." is a platitude that keeps the camera on the author.
5. **Synthetic spirit (P4).** No lived specifics; reads model-generated by *cadence* alone.
6. **Missing structural devices** — no focus story, statement question, lexicon, or 90/10 (D2/D3/D5/D6). Opportunities, addressed *after* the texture is fixed, never to "compensate" for it.

### 🔧 How to make it work (located edits)
1. **De-saturate the athletic writing — restore flow to ~80%, reserve three-word lines for ONE peak.** Keep "The deal collapsed." as the fire opener, then let the very next sentences *breathe* into longer, flowing lines that carry the **consequence**: what those three years actually were, what the deal meant, what walked out the door with it. The contrast is the whole mechanism — flowing prose is the valley that makes the three-word line a peak.
2. **Pick the single peak for the staccato.** Strongest candidate: the sleepless-night beat — let "The ceiling stared back." be the *one* place the prose goes to three words, with flowing sentences on either side. Don't let the collapse, the night, *and* the triumph all punch — choose one.
3. **Supply lived specifics only you can (fixes P4 spirit).** Replace generic beats with the real ones: the actual sum, the name of the deal/company, who Mike was *to* this, the one object you stared at, the thing you told no one. A model can write "the shame was heavy"; it cannot write the detail you carry. This is the spirit, and it cannot be invented (§5 house rule).
4. **Reorder for suspense (D4).** Don't run collapse→morning→triumph in a straight line. Open *inside* the sleepless night and flash back to the call; or withhold the *cause* of the collapse and plant a statement question — e.g. "I'd find out later what Mike already knew." (no question mark, §2.1) — so the reader carries a gap.
5. **Convert the CTA close into a call to mirror (D8 → P2).** Cut "Never quit. Never stop. Keep going. Always." Replace it with a line that hands the reflection to the reader, so they close the page thinking about *their* collapse, not your comeback. The mirror outperforms the command (§3.2).
6. **Optional, and only ONE:** a single focus story up top *or* one coined pairing for the lesson — not both, and not as a patch for the texture problem. The fix is *subtraction of the overdose first*, then at most one deliberate addition.

## 6. Verdict + gate

**REVISE.** (Book-chapter gate 9/10, no posture below 9 — not close: postures land 3–6, the marquee device D7 is a 2 by overdose, and the contrast law is failed outright.)

**Does it land? No — and precisely because of the thing that looks like craft.** The relentless three-word punch reads as "powerful writing" for two sentences and then flattens everything that follows into the same beat, so the moments that should hit don't. The piece is a seasoning served as the entire meal.

**Top 3 fixes that move the needle most:**
1. **De-saturate the athletic writing** — restore a flowing baseline, reserve three-word lines for one peak (fixes D7 + the contrast law in one move).
2. **Supply lived specifics** — the sum, the names, the one object — to restore spirit (P4).
3. **Replace the CTA close with a call to mirror** — turn the spotlight from your comeback to the reader's collapse (fixes D8 + P2).

Fix 1 alone takes this from a flat-line to a passage with peaks. After the texture pass, run the result through `tool-humanizer` (deep mode if a voice-profile exists) to catch any residual synthetic cadence.
