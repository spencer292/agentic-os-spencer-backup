## 4. Interaction & contrast flags (§7.2)

- **Athletic ↔ author voice:** *No conflict* — there is no athletic writing, and the inferred baseline is itself flat. Flag this check as **lower confidence** (no author profile; the on-disk profile is a different author). The real texture problem is the inverse of overdose — see below.
- **Statement-question saturation:** N/A — zero present. No saturation.
- **Lexicon over-coining:** N/A — zero present. No over-coining.
- **The contrast law (peaks need valleys) — FAILED, in the rare inverse direction.** The piece is **all valley, no peak.** Every sentence sits in the same low, even register, so the genuinely high-stakes moment (burnout) reads at the *same emotional volume* as "I went to school there." Nothing is given relief, so nothing can lift. This is the headline contrast defect.

> **Reviewer's honesty check (the skill's whole thesis):** this piece's failures are almost entirely **absence, under-dose, and mis-ordering** — *not* overdose. So the correct prescription is **not** "add all eight devices." That would trade journalism for device-soup — a different anti-pattern. The needle-movers are the postures and the sequencing; layer in *one* fire/focus move and *one* athletic peak, no more.

## 5. The prescription

### What's working (keep it)
- **The raw arc is strong and inherently mirror-able.** The 12–14-hour / 6-day grind, the burnout, and especially the counterintuitive payoff (hours roughly halved, revenue held) are exactly what the target reader lives. This is the hardest thing to manufacture and it's already here — it's just in the wrong **order** and the wrong **register**. Don't lose these facts.
- **Restraint is a usable baseline.** The author isn't over-writing; the voice is calm and uncluttered. Once the hedge-toxins are trimmed and one or two peaks are allowed to lift, that calm becomes an asset — restraint is what makes a single three-word peak hit. Do **not** overcorrect into purple prose.
- **The promise is clear** (AI → time back). Clarity of thesis is an asset; it just shouldn't be the *opening* move.

### What's not (located)
1. **Para 1 (whole)** — journalism: birth, schooling, emigration, company count. Leads with the least consequential facts. *(P1, D1, D4)*
2. **"In 2021 I experienced a period of burnout."** — the emotional core, sanitised to a clinical clause and buried mid-para 2. *(P3, P2)*
3. **"Revenue stayed roughly the same while my hours came down considerably."** — the single most surprising, sellable fact, thrown away in hedged language. *(D6, P4)*
4. **Strict chronological order across the whole piece** — actively kills anticipation. *(D4)*
5. **The close is a blurb/CTA**, not a call to mirror. *(D8, P2)*
6. **Hedge toxins throughout:** "typically," "around forty," "roughly," "considerably," "a period of." *(D7 execution, P4)*
7. **No focusing or fire mechanism anywhere up top** — the reader is given no reason to lean in by line two. *(D1, D2, D3)*

### How to make it work (specific edits, prioritised — five surgical moves, not eight devices)
1. **Re-sequence: open at the peak, not the birth.** Cut "I was born in Bulawayo in 1972" as the opener. Start at (or just before) the **burnout**, or at the **counterintuitive turn**. Reveal the three businesses / forty people later as *what was at stake*, not as a CV. All of para 1's biography moves to a subordinate position — background goes *somewhere*, never the lead (§1.9). **Biggest single needle-mover.**
2. **Lead with consequence in the burnout line.** Run the bridge exercise: one sentence of the event, then *"and because of that…"* that says the costly, un-sanitised thing — what the burnout actually took, the moment he knew. Fixes **P1 + P3 + P4** at once. The lived specific (the real moment, not "a period") is the **spirit the model can't supply — it must come from the author.**
3. **Turn the payoff into the 90/10 surprise, not a hedge.** Replace "revenue stayed roughly the same while my hours came down considerably" with the concrete, un-hedged version (real numbers if true: "I cut my week from ~80 hours to [X]. Revenue didn't move."). 90% familiar (everyone believes hours = output) + 10% twist (he proved it false) = the door into the book. Give it its own line. *(D6)*
4. **Reserve exactly ONE athletic peak.** At the turn, allow a single three-word / very short sentence as the emotional high. Keep the calm declarative baseline everywhere else — the peak only works *because* everything around it is even. **This is the opposite of "make it all punchy."** *(D7, contrast law)*
5. **Convert the close to a call to mirror.** End by turning the question on the reader — invite them to look at their own week and its cost — so they close para 3 thinking about *their* life, not the author's CV. *(D8, P2)*
6. **Trim the hedges (execution, not ornament).** Delete "typically," "roughly," "considerably," "a period of," "around." Cleaner declaratives, *not* more adjectives. *(D7 toxins)*
7. **(Optional — at most one)** add a single focusing move up top: *either* a fire opener (a declaration/statement question about working yourself into the ground) *or* one short focus-story analogy — **not both**, and not stacked on top of the above.

## 6. Verdict + gate

**Answering the author's question directly: no, this is not a strong opening yet — but it sits on strong foundations.** The failure is craft, not material: it opens as **journalism** (who/what/where/when), runs strict **chronology**, sanitises its emotional core to a clinical clause, and closes on a **blurb instead of a mirror**. The lived arc underneath — grind → burnout → the counterintuitive recovery — is genuinely mirror-able, which is the hardest ingredient to fake, and it's already here.

| **Postures** | 2 / 2 / 2 / 3 — all below floor |
| **Devices** | mostly 1–3 |
| **Result** | **REVISE** (well below gate) |

**Top 3 moves that move the needle most:**
1. **Re-sequence** — open at the burnout/turn, not at birth; demote the biography.
2. **Rewrite the burnout + payoff lines for consequence and un-hedged specifics** — the spirit and the lived detail the author alone can supply.
3. **Close on a mirror, not a blurb** — leave the reader thinking about *their* week.

Do those three and the *same facts* become an opening that earns the next page. Hold the line on restraint: this needs re-ordering, consequence, and one peak — not eight devices crammed into three paragraphs.
