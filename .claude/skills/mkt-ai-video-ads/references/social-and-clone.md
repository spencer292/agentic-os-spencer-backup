## 1. Social and virality

### The course's core claim
Visuals and ads are worthless if nobody sees them. Virality is described as engineered through hooks, retention structure, riding trends, engagement and understanding the algorithm. The author says the tricks "work on every platform", but every concrete rule is written for TikTok.

### Choosing a platform (07-02)
- **TikTok:** discovery-based, so an account with zero followers can reach millions; fast growth, but short shelf life (a video spikes, then stops getting views about a week later). Start here for the first few weeks.
- **Instagram Reels:** slower to break through, rewards consistency and look, builds trust via Stories, carousels and DMs. Once you have followers, Reels reliably reach them (5k followers means roughly 5k views as a floor); TikTok barely shows videos to followers.
- **YouTube (Shorts and long form):** slowest to grow, but one video keeps getting views for months or years; best for authority and trust. For Got Moles this is the evergreen "mole or vole" shelf.
- **Cross-post everything:** the video is already made, so each extra platform costs about 5 minutes. If it flops on one it may work on another. Facebook (a fit for a local service audience) and others can be added the same way.

### Upload settings per platform (07-06)
- Edit on a computer, then publish from the phone (the course claims desktop uploads cut views drastically; unverified, but phone upload is cheap). CapCut route: upload the project to a CapCut Space (cloud storage), log into the same account on the phone, open Space, download.
- **Export:** 1080p, 30 fps, bitrate "recommended", smart HDR off, AI Ultra HD off. Never 4K/60: TikTok downgrades it.
- **TikTok:** tick the AI content toggle on every upload; high-quality uploads on; privacy Everyone; set a cover and keep covers visually consistent across the grid.
- **Instagram:** tick the AI generated label every time; "upload at highest quality" under More options; optionally share to Story with each post.
- **YouTube Shorts:** visibility Public; Altered content = Yes for AI footage; audience question: choose "No, not made for kids" or the video is shown only to kids. Add title, description, hashtags.
- Use the Share-to buttons inside CapCut (TikTok, Instagram, Other, then YouTube) to skip the camera roll; after a few uploads it takes minutes.

### Channel branding (07-05)
- Pick a brandable, memorable name (ChatGPT can brainstorm); TikTok only allows a name change once every 30 days, so decide before launch. For Got Moles use the existing brand handle, not a new one.
- Make a custom logo in Canva with the account name in it and use it as the profile picture instead of a random photo; the course says this alone lifts follow rate. Keep covers, bio and tone consistent so viewers know what to expect.

### Mindset and plan (07-03)
Three phases. Builder: volume over perfection, build a repeatable workflow, track process not views, expect 10-50 low-traction posts. Experimenter: test hooks, formats, lengths, delivery style, editing pace and thumbnails against analytics (drop-off point, click-through, shares and comments) and look for a formula you could repeat ten times; remake any winner with a small twist. Example: a time-traveler POV account added voiceover, got more comments and story continuity, and stacked small changes to about 4M views per video. Operator: content calendar, standard workflow, delegate or automate, then monetize. Rules: treat flops as data; going viral means nothing if you cannot repeat it. For Got Moles read "monetize" as booked jobs: track calls and form fills against posts, not only views.

### Platform-specific rules (all TikTok; no Reels/Shorts/Facebook text exists)
Rules the course gives:
- **Posting time:** pick one time and hold it daily so metrics aren't distorted by timing.
- **Cadence:** 3-5 posts a day maximum (a creator-account figure, far above what a local service brand needs). Never post twice within an hour; wait 1-2 hours between posts or views drop.
- **Hashtags:** four at most; more "confuses" the algorithm.
- **Resolution:** export 1080p at 30 fps. 4K/60 gets downgraded by TikTok and looks worse.
- **Length and music:** don't use copyrighted audio on videos over 60 seconds; the video can be flagged unoriginal.
- **Niche discipline:** don't post off-niche content, "not even one video". Don't watch off-niche content on the posting account.
- **Audience geography:** content built around a place, plus place hashtags, steers who sees it. The author warns that logging in from another country, or replying to comments in another language, can shift the audience region.
- **Account hygiene:** warm a new account for about 7 days of niche viewing before posting. Don't log into someone else's account from your phone. Never delete videos; set them private instead.
- **Paid boost:** the author says never use TikTok's Promote feature because it supposedly limits organic reach. This is unverified folklore and conflicts with running paid ads at all. Do not adopt it for Got Moles; test paid vs organic on real numbers.
- **Content moderation:** TikTok is strict about visible weapons, cigarettes, cigars and the like. Relevance for us: body-gripping traps on screen may trip moderation. See section 5.
- **AI label:** the author has always ticked "AI content" on upload. Many big creators don't and still get millions of views. His position: it doesn't matter for reach, tick it if worried. Our position (section 5): always tick it.
- **Cross-posting:** make the video once, post it to YouTube, Instagram and TikTok to multiply reach 2-3x. Adapt pacing per platform (the Phase 9 ChatGPT lesson says each platform has different pacing).

### Hooks and retention
- First clip no longer than 3 seconds; a scene change that takes 5 seconds loses viewers.
- Watch time is called the most important metric. If viewers leave at 2 seconds, change the next video's opening.
- On-screen text: if people are reading, they are still watching.
- Consistent look: matching covers so the profile grid looks designed. Clear bio with emojis. A follower-goal line in the bio (for example 6500/7000) to prompt follows.
- Hook formulas (Phase 9, for talking-head clone scripts): ask for 10 opening lines for the first 2 seconds that feel natural, slightly unexpected and scroll-stopping. Sample tone: "This is going to sound fake... but it isn't." "People don't realise this yet." "No one warned me about this." Also: "20 ideas that feel controversial, insightful or pattern-interrupting in the first 3 seconds" and "15 things people don't realise about [topic]".
- Scripts for spoken delivery: short sentences, pauses, contractions, simple language, subtle emphasis, under 30 seconds.

### Formats and trend-riding
- Trend rule: if someone in your niche posted yesterday and got 2M views, recreate that exact video with a few changes and post fast. Don't reinvent. Save ideas to remix.
- Momentum: when a video is working, post again soon so viewers find your newest content.
- The niche prompt pack is the "viral AI content" lane: hyper-realistic anthropomorphic animals in noir/mafia scenes ("Tim Cheese"), animal fusion and hybrids, giant sea monsters on fishing vessels, animals helping/rescuing, fruit and food creations, miniatures, knights, cats, POV, stadium cam, live film set, evolutions, "Italian brainrot" characters (Crocodilo, Bombardino). Prompt style is one long cinematic sentence: subject, setting, lighting, mood, realism tag ("4K ultra-realism"). Method taught: paste a working prompt into ChatGPT and say "copy this style but change X". A note says ChatGPT Image 2.0 or Nano Banana Pro now beat Midjourney for these. This is entertainment content with no obvious service-business use, except as a pattern: one reusable prompt skeleton, swap the variable.

### Transferable mechanics from the niche walkthroughs (07-10 to 07-21)
The niches are entertainment (celebrity evolutions, first-person POV, animal fusion and hybrids, animal warnings, Tim Cheese noir characters, fruit and miniature characters, knights as emotions, sea monsters, Crocodilo, cats). Only the production method transfers:
- **Idea batching:** give ChatGPT a prompt, get 10 ideas or a scene-by-scene breakdown in the right frame (9:16, first person for POV), then pick one. Ask ChatGPT to rewrite a weak image prompt into a detailed one; a described moment ("waking up in the alley") beats a plain location.
- **Reference-first generation:** screenshot a reference (Windows Snipping Tool), upload it as a character, style or image reference, then write a short prompt. Reuse the first approved image as the character reference for every later shot in a series (Midjourney `--cw 100` character weight keeps the face stable).
- **Start frame and end frame:** generate the "before" still and the "after" still, load them as first and last frame in the video model so it morphs between them (Kling in the course, Pika keyframes for evolutions). Mole-control use: damaged lawn to clean lawn, as an illustration only unless it is a real job.
- **Subtle motion prompts:** ask for gentle movement so nothing jerks or looks unreal.
- **Clip length:** generators output about 5 seconds, which fits a single-clip TikTok. Download without watermark, assemble in CapCut with music and a transition between scenes.
- **Style choices:** bright, saturated images stand out in the scroll; music only, no sound effects, reads calmer; on-screen text names each scene; no-narration videos avoid language barriers.
- **Split-screen:** mask two images to half-frame each in CapCut (Mask, Rectangle) for an A/B or before/after layout, then add text.
- **Volume:** once a prompt skeleton exists, a format can be produced a few per hour.
Skip: celebrity likenesses, fake catches, content showing animals in harm, and anything implying real events that did not happen.

### What the algorithm supposedly rewards (the course's belief set)
Watch time and early retention; early comments in the first 1-2 hours; replies (full sentences or questions, not emoji-only); niche consistency; steady posting time; audience-matched geography; original audio.

### Engagement tactics: sort them
Usable and honest:
- Reply to every comment, preferably with a question. Spend the first hour after posting answering comments in real time and turning single comments into short threads.
- Engage 10-15 minutes a day with niche content so the platform learns your audience.
- Real friends or colleagues commenting when you publish (a small genuine community).
- Screenshot the post and share it to your Story.
- Comment keyword filters to hide spam or abuse (but see below for how the course uses them).

Do not use (deceptive or manipulative):
- Posting a second comment from an undisclosed second phone/account and arguing with yourself on the main account to fake a debate. This is astroturfing, breaches platform rules, and is a trust problem for a local brand.
- Planting deliberate factual errors ("Einstein invented gravity") to bait corrections. For a mole-control expert this undermines authority and spreads misinformation. A harmless honest alternative: ask a genuine either/or question ("Where do you see more hills, front or back yard?").
- Using comment-keyword filters to hide "this is AI" comments so the illusion holds. That conceals AI use from the audience.
- Hiding that content is AI (see section 5).

## 3. Clone yourself

### Framing
The course sells clones as leverage: stop being the bottleneck, one recording becomes endless videos, a version of you that "works 24/7". It calls cloning "the thing of 2026".

### Tools named
- **HeyGen:** face/avatar clone (video avatar), plus photo-to-video lip-sync. Brody (cloning-agency guest) teaches the clone lessons.
- **ElevenLabs:** voice clone, connected into HeyGen by API key. Course logic: ElevenLabs has the best voice engine, HeyGen the best visual engine.
- **ChatGPT:** scripts and ideas; also writes Nano Banana edit prompts.
- **CapCut** (or any editor): final assembly, music, speed, B-roll.

### Workflow: build the visual clone (09-02)
1. **Plan:** HeyGen free tier caps at 3 videos a month. Creator ($29/mo as quoted) gives unlimited videos, 1080p export, one avatar. Pro ($99) adds 4K, faster processing and more Avatar IV credits. Business gives five avatars (needed to clone several people). Check current pricing; one owner clone needs Creator at minimum.
2. **Record (2-3 minutes):** 1080p camera minimum (phone is fine). **Turn off HDR on iPhone or colors look wrong in HeyGen.** Tripod, soft even light across the face with minimal shadows, clean solid background (avoid fabric, it looks fuzzy), solid-color top that contrasts the background. Look straight down the lens and talk naturally about the day, the business, anything. Normal hand gestures are fine, but none above chest height and no counting on fingers. Any orientation works; vertical for social.
3. **Upload:** Avatars, plus, Clone a real person, skip to upload. Toggles: AI background removal optional, 4K on if on Pro, noise removal irrelevant (the HeyGen voice is not used), identity protection always on. Create avatar; processing is slow.
4. **Verify identity:** the avatar shows "needs verification"; the person records themselves reading an on-screen script and submits. This is a built-in consent gate and must be done by the person being cloned.
5. **Connect the voice:** avatar name, voice icon, New voice, Third party voice, ElevenLabs, paste an API key (ElevenLabs Developers, API Keys, Create key, unrestricted, no credit limit), switch on the cloned voice, save it as default. The voice label should read ElevenLabs multilingual.

### Workflow: render a clone video (09-02)
- Create, AI Studio, select the avatar (it may default to a random one), paste the script. Language: original accent. Engine ElevenLabs, speed 1, volume 100%.
- Advanced voice settings the guest uses: Multilingual v2, similarity 75%, stability 65-70 (67 in the demo), style 0.12, speaker boost on.
- **Four-line rule:** keep each AI Studio segment to four lines or fewer, end on a clean endpoint, and split a one-minute script into 4-5 segments. A long block gives ElevenLabs more room to fail and is harder to debug.
- **Audio troubleshooting:** play the preview before rendering. Unnatural pauses: remove commas. Awkward flow: reword. Letter-pair words like AI read oddly; try upper/lower case and punctuation changes.
- **Motion engine:** Avatar IV is more realistic and smoother but credit-limited (a 1-minute render cost 5 premium credits in the demo); Avatar III is solid and unlimited. If gestures look off, re-render on III or edit the script, since wording changes the gestures.
- Title, then export (demo: 4K MP4, 25 fps; 1080p/30 is enough for social). The end of a clip can look odd; trim or cover it. Never post the raw render; layer B-roll over weak sections.

### Changing look without re-recording (09-03)
- Avatars, New look, upload a photo or video; use a frame from the original recording that clearly shows eyes and teeth (Nano Banana edits images only, so it needs maximum facial detail). Create look; validation is quick.
- Editor, Base look, photo avatar. Engine Nano Banana Pro (best for precise edits; the guest alternates with Nano Banana). Format 9:16 for vertical.
- One change per prompt: background, hat, top. For backgrounds prompt **studio lighting**, never natural light, candles or anything that moves (trees, wind, flames). Have ChatGPT write the prompts. HeyGen returns two variations per prompt; delete the bad ones (check head cutout and body proportions).
- Then render in AI Studio as usual (Avatar IV, same voice settings).
- **Limit:** a recorded video avatar beats a photo avatar for realism because it knows your movement. For best realism record a second video avatar (different shirt, room) instead.

### Workflow: clone the voice (09-04)
1. ElevenLabs, Voices, create voice, Professional Voice Clone (instant clone is lower quality and for lower plans; use professional for a brand voice).
2. Training audio: at least 30 minutes of clean speech with no background noise, music, echo or compression; quiet room, decent microphone, natural delivery like an ad or video. Past recordings of the person talking work. Name the voice and submit; professional approval takes time.
3. Text to speech: pick the cloned voice, paste the script, adjust settings, generate.

### Photo lip-sync (09-05)
- HeyGen Create, Photo to video (Avatar IV lip sync). Left: a still of the speaker (a real photo of the person, or a generated character). Right: type a script or upload audio; uploading ElevenLabs audio keeps the same voice across generations. Select 1080p, Generate. Output about a minute; no script length limit.
- Limitation: little body movement. Veo 3.1 gives movement but only 8 seconds per clip, so it needs stitching; HeyGen is easier for long talking-head and ads.

### Long-form assembly (09-06)
- Put the avatar video in CapCut as the base, add music (silence is boring), speed 1.1-1.2x if slow.
- Change the visual every few seconds. Listen to the audio, generate matching B-roll (Kling or similar; ChatGPT can suggest B-roll from the transcript), layer it on top, cut back to the avatar briefly.
- B-roll hides lip-sync glitches without a regenerate and hides the join between two 8-second Veo clips (place a B-roll shot at the 8-second mark).
- When the speaker mentions a thing, show the thing.

### Quality tips (explicit in the course)
- If a section looks bad, don't regenerate the whole video. Lay B-roll over it and keep the clone's audio running.
- Never leave a clone talking alone for a full minute. Mix with B-roll for retention and naturalness.
- If it wouldn't sound normal said aloud, it will lip-sync badly. Write natural, spoken language.
- If speech is slow or stiff, speed audio/video up 1.1-1.2x.
- Clone-optimised scripts: short sentences, pauses, spoken not written. Reusable instruction: write it as if spoken by a real person, with natural pauses, contractions, simple language, subtle emphasis.
- Treat ChatGPT as a creative director (give context, role, outcome), not as "write me a script". Ask for angles, not ideas.

### Script multiplication patterns (useful for ad testing)
- One idea into five scripts of different tone: curious/subtle, confident/bold, slightly controversial, educational/casual, direct/blunt, each under 30 seconds.
- One concept becomes 3 hook styles x 2 pacings x 2 emotional tones x 2 CTAs = 12 variants without re-recording. Regenerate only the first 2 seconds to swap a hook, or only the end to swap a CTA.
- One script adapted for TikTok, Reels, Shorts and a talking-head ad: same message, different pacing.
- A 7-day content plan, each day with topic, hook, 20-30 second script and tone.
- Refine passes: "make this sound more human", "reduce formal phrasing", "add subtle personality without adding length", "make this feel like a real opinion".

### Uses the course lists for a clone
Daily short-form without a camera; ad variations and fatigue refreshes; A/B tests of tone and pacing; welcome/FAQ/pricing/objection-handling clips ("why choose us"); brand intros and outros; course lessons and corporate training; localisation; funnel front-ends where the clone greets leads, explains the offer and pushes a CTA; podcast face clips.

### Consent and disclosure
The course text is thin and leans the wrong way. It tells students to sell clones of founders, says brands "don't really care about authenticity now that we are in the age of AI", and that with correct editing and B-roll "no one will be able to tell it's fake". It positions clones as presence and speed, "not deepfakes". There is no consent checklist and no disclosure guidance. Our rules:
- Only clone a person who has given explicit written consent (HeyGen's terms require it too). Keep the signed consent with the project files.
- Clone only the owner's own face and voice; never a customer, employee, competitor or public figure.
- Disclose AI use. Tick the platform "AI content" label. Add a plain line in caption or end card (for example "AI-assisted video of Spencer, from his own script") on ads and any video where a clone speaks.
- The script must be words Spencer has read and approved. A clone saying something he has not vetted is an endorsement he never gave.

## 5. Got Moles applicability

### Fit assessment
- Strong: clone-of-owner recurring spokesperson; script-variant testing; B-roll-led editing; hook discipline; cross-posting; seasonal organic series.
- Weak: the viral AI-niche lane (anthropomorphic animals, fusion creatures). It builds audiences with no purchase intent and no link to a local service. Skip it as a growth strategy. A single branded mole character is possible but isn't covered by this course.
- Reject: fake-engagement and bait tactics, hidden-AI tactics.

### Cloning Spencer as a recurring spokesperson
Spencer is an Army veteran and the owner; his face and voice carry the trust. A clone fits the course's "founder presence without founder time" use case.
- Capture: one clean session, 2-3 minutes straight to camera on a phone (1080p+, HDR off) on a tripod, soft even indoor light, plain solid-color logo shirt against a plain solid wall, calm gestures below the chest, his actual cadence. Spencer does the HeyGen identity verification himself. For a professional ElevenLabs voice record 30+ minutes of clean quiet-room speech separately (past clean recordings count). Needs HeyGen Creator minimum and an ElevenLabs plan with professional cloning. Change looks with Nano Banana Pro under studio lighting only, or record a second video avatar for realism; disclose a new look as AI like any other.
- Persona prompt (master system prompt): plain-spoken, veteran, practical, no hype, US English, chemical-free and safe mole control, safe for pets and children, professional methods. Apply the standing Got Moles rules: lead with "chemical-free and safe mole control", not "trapping", in headline copy (trapping only as the method explanation); no body-gripping/kill/lethal wording in ads; no I-713 compliance claims; use the current review count per CLAUDE.local.md (283+ five-star reviews, 289 Google reviews, 5.0 across three locations), not "219+"; clarify "15+ years" as Spencer's own experience (company founded 2017); never "WA's #1"; never "hauled away" as a default (dead moles are double-bagged into the customer's garbage can).
- Format: 15-30 seconds, 1-2 on-camera lines per beat, then cut to real B-roll (lawn damage, hills, a technician at work). Never run the clone alone longer than a few seconds at a time. Script segments of four lines or fewer; preview audio and fix commas and wording before rendering; render the final on Avatar IV; 1.1-1.2x speed if slow.
- Real footage stays primary for proof (before/after lawns, an actual job). Use the clone for the explaining and talking parts.
- Spencer approves every script before render and the final cut before posting.

### Organic series ideas (each is one script multiplied across platforms)
1. "Mole or vole?" Show the difference between mole hills (volcano mounds, raised ridges) and vole runs (surface trails, plant damage). Hook: "That's not a mole, and treating it like one wastes your money." Check the claim with the technician team.
2. "Myth Monday": one myth per week (gum, castor oil, sonic spikes, poison). Frame as "what actually works and what's money down the drain". Calm, informed, no named competitors.
3. "What I'm seeing in [city] this week": local-area updates by zip, real photos with permission, Spencer or his clone on camera. Only real, current observations.
4. "Reading the yard": how to spot fresh sign versus old mounds (old, crusted, grass through them means quiet).
5. "Ask Spencer" FAQ shorts: price tiers by acreage, how long to see results, the no-catch guarantee, is it safe for dogs. Pricing: only the confirmed residential tiers; commercial and over-5-acre properties are in-person bids (Cory handles bids; route requests to him, not Spencer).
6. Objection library (from the course's idea 7): "Why not DIY?", "Do you use poison?", "What if you don't catch any?", "Is it humane?" Answer each with approved claims only.
7. Veteran story short: one honest 30-second origin clip in Spencer's real voice. Film this one for real; it is the trust asset.

### Seasonal and local hooks (Western Washington)
INFERRED from regional knowledge, to be checked against Got Moles call and job data:
- **Fall (Sept-Nov):** first heavy rains soften soil and bring worms up, drawing moles to the surface; fresh hills show after leaf drop and when mowing stops. Hooks: "The rain just started. Here's what's coming up in your lawn." "Fresh mounds after the first storm? Look at this before you do anything."
- **Winter:** wet ground, moles keep working below the surface; lean on prevention and the year-round protection program.
- **Spring (Feb-May):** breeding season and lawn green-up; new mounds right as people start yard work, re-seed or lay sod. Hooks: "Before you re-seed that lawn, read this." "Spring mowing and mole mounds: what to do first."
- **Summer:** drier soil, moles go deeper, less visible activity; tie to irrigation.
- Local flavor: name real service areas and use place hashtags (course says place hashtags steer audience; keep to four tags max); mention rain, clay and big-acreage Puget Sound lots. Post on real triggers (first storm of fall, first warm weekend of spring) rather than on a generic calendar.

### Practical posting cadence
The course's 3-5 posts a day suits viral creators, not us. Aim for 3-4 short videos a week at a fixed time, TikTok first for a few weeks then cross-post, the 1-hour comment-reply routine right after posting with question-style replies, identical edits cross-posted to TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts and Facebook (the course has no text on those three). Export 1080p 30 fps, first clip under 3 seconds, burned-in captions, four hashtags max.

### Risk flags
1. **Impersonation / consent:** cloning anyone other than Spencer, or making his clone say things he has not approved. Keep consent on file; no cloned employees or customers; no voice clone on phone lines pretending to be a live person.
2. **Undisclosed AI:** the course is relaxed about AI labels and encourages concealing AI (comment filters to hide "this is AI", "no one will be able to tell"). Always label. Also check current FTC and platform rules (Meta, TikTok, YouTube require AI labels on realistic synthetic media in some contexts) before running paid ads.
3. **Fake customers:** the course's clone-based UGC and testimonial-style ads would mean synthetic people giving reviews. Never generate a fake customer, testimonial, before/after or review. Testimonials must be real, consented, and compliant with review policy. Never present AI-generated lawn damage as a real customer's property. Any AI mole illustration must read as an illustration.
4. **Manufactured engagement:** self-arguing from a second account, seeded comment rings beyond genuine contacts, planted false facts. All breach platform terms and brand trust. Do not use.
5. **Claims and policy language:** no body-gripping/kill/lethal wording in ads. Don't show traps with a visible kill on social either; TikTok's strictness about weapon-like objects means mechanism footage may be removed. Show results, not mechanism.
6. **Deliberate errors:** an expert brand saying something wrong on purpose spreads misinformation about animals and treatments. Reject.
7. **Folklore tips:** never use Promote, never delete videos, don't take your phone abroad. Unverified; treat as hypotheses, don't plan around them.
8. **Spokesperson fit:** a smooth clone talking alone reads fake; keep B-roll heavy. Never imply the clone is Spencer live, or on a job site.
9. **Lane boundary:** social video is ours; website, schema and deploys stay with Roy. If a video should live on a site page, brief Roy.

### Operating checklist for the skill
- Script in Spencer's words, run through the claims guardrails, get Spencer's approval.
- Render the clone for talking beats only; cover with real B-roll; add captions.
- Add an AI-assisted line plus the platform AI label.
- Post at a fixed time; reply to comments within the first hour with questions.
- Log hook, length, 3-second hold and watch time per post; keep the winner and vary the first 2 seconds and the CTA.
- Nothing posts, spends or sends without Spencer's approval.
