# Strategy Frameworks

The named, citable structures used to synthesise a brand strategy from discovery answers.
A real engagement uses 3–5 together, not all. Every framework below is industry-standard
(Wheeler, Neumeier, Sinek, Moore, Jung/Mark & Pearson, Aaker, Unilever, NN/g).

## Contents
1. Golden Circle (purpose)
2. 12 Brand Archetypes (personality character)
3. Aaker Big-Five (measurable personality)

5. Brand Key / Brand Pyramid → Essence
6. Mission / Vision / Values / Promise

## 1. Golden Circle — Sinek (purpose)

Three concentric rings, worked from the inside out:
- **Why** — the belief/purpose. Not "make money"; the cause the brand serves. ("People don't
  buy what you do, they buy why you do it.")
- **How** — the values, process and differentiators that make the Why real.
- **What** — the products/services (the easy part).

Use to anchor the emotional core before anything tactical. The Why usually becomes the seed
of the brand story and often the essence.

## 2. The 12 Brand Archetypes — Jung, via Mark & Pearson (*The Hero and the Outlaw*)

Pick ONE primary (optionally a secondary). The archetype makes voice, tone and visuals behave
like one consistent character. Each has a core desire, a fear, and a voice.

| Archetype | Core desire | Voice / behaviour | Brand feel |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|------------|
| Innocent | safety, happiness | optimistic, honest, simple | wholesome, pure |
| Sage | truth, understanding | wise, measured, expert | authority, insight |
| Explorer | freedom, discovery | independent, adventurous | rugged, pioneering |
| Outlaw | revolution, disruption | bold, rebellious, blunt | challenger, edgy |
| Magician | transformation | visionary, charismatic | wonder, "make it happen" |
| Hero | mastery, courage | bold, determined, motivating | performance, triumph |
| Lover | intimacy, beauty | sensual, warm, intimate | premium, passionate |
| Jester | enjoyment, play | funny, irreverent, light | fun, playful |
| Everyman | belonging | friendly, down-to-earth, real | relatable, accessible |
| Caregiver | service, protection | nurturing, warm, reassuring | trust, care |
| Ruler | control, order | authoritative, refined, structured | luxury, leadership |
| Creator | innovation, self-expression | imaginative, expressive | design-led, original |

Pick from the user's Personality answers: who they say they are, what they'd never do, the
three-word description, and the slider positions. Justify the pick in one or two sentences
tied to their words.

## 3. Brand Personality — Aaker's Big Five (Jennifer Aaker, Stanford)

Score the brand 1–5 on each dimension for a measurable fingerprint (and a later benchmark):
- **Sincerity** (honest, genuine, warm)
- **Excitement** (daring, spirited, imaginative)
- **Competence** (reliable, intelligent, successful)
- **Sophistication** (premium, charming, refined)
- **Ruggedness** (outdoorsy, tough, durable)

The dominant 1–2 dimensions should agree with the chosen archetype; if they fight, revisit
the archetype.

## 4. Positioning statement — Geoffrey Moore (*Crossing the Chasm*) — THE SPINE

Fill every slot. This single sentence forces audience, frame, benefit, proof and competitor
into alignment, and is the strategic anchor everything else ladders off:

> **For** [target customer]
> **who** [need / opportunity],
> **[brand]** is the [category / frame of reference]
> **that** [key benefit],
> **because** [reason to believe],
> **unlike** [primary alternative].

Quality bar: the [unlike] must be a *real* alternative (a competitor, DIY, or doing nothing),
and the [because] must be a *real* proof (mechanism, track record, design) — not adjectives.
This is internal; the external tagline comes later (mkt-brand-voice / mkt-positioning).

## 5. Brand Key (Unilever) or Brand Pyramid → Brand Essence

Choose ONE to drive to the essence:

**Brand Key** — best with a real competitive frame. Nine interlocking parts:
Competitive environment → Target → Insight (a deep human truth) → Benefits (functional +
emotional) → Values & personality → Reasons to believe → Discriminator (the single most
compelling reason to choose) → **Brand Essence** (centre).

**Brand Pyramid** — best for showing how features become meaning. Ladders bottom-up:
Attributes → Functional benefits → Emotional benefits → Values / personality →
**Brand Essence** (apex).

**Brand Essence** = the soul in 2–3 words (e.g. "Authentic athletic performance",
"Effortless everyday luxury"). Derived, never invented — it's the through-line across the Why,
the archetype and the discriminator.

## 6. Mission / Vision / Values / Promise (the durable platform)

- **Vision** — the world the brand wants to create (aspirational, external).
- **Mission** — what it does daily to get there (active, present-tense).
- **Values** — 3–6 behavioural beliefs (each phrased as something the brand *does*, not a noun).
- **Promise** — what every customer can reliably expect, every time.

## 7. Verbal layer — coordinates for mkt-brand-voice (do NOT execute voice here)

Set, don't elaborate — `mkt-brand-voice` executes these into a full voice profile:
- **Four tone dimensions (NN/g):** plot the brand on Formal ↔ Casual, Serious ↔ Funny,
  Respectful ↔ Irreverent, Matter-of-fact ↔ Enthusiastic.
- **Message House (optional seed):** Roof = core message / value prop · Pillars = 3–4 proof
  themes · Foundation = evidence. Capture the roof + pillar headings; voice fills the copy.

## 8. Moodboard direction — words for mkt-visual-identity (do NOT generate images here)

Translate strategy + the admired references into a written visual territory:
- **Colour direction** — psychological intent (e.g. "trust + competence → deep blue base;
  one warm accent to stay human"), not final hex.
- **Type feeling** — e.g. "structured, authoritative → a strong grotesk display; humanist
  body so it doesn't feel cold."
- **Imagery / texture** — subject, treatment, mood ("real people mid-work, candid, warm grade").
- **Reference rationale** — for each admired brand the user named, the one cue to borrow.

This is the hand-off; the visual skill decides actual palette, fonts and logo.
