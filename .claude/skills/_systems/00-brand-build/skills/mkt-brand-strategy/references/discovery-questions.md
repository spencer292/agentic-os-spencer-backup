# Discovery Question Bank

The questions a brand strategist runs in a discovery / creative-brief session. Grouped as a
strategist groups them. Pull all, or a subset — and **skip any group already answered by
loaded context** (`icp.md` covers Audience; `positioning.md` covers much of Differentiation).

## How to run the session

- **Small batches.** Ask 3–4 questions, listen, react, then go again. A 30-question wall
  ends the session. Follow the threads the user opens — the gold is usually in the tangent.
- **Push for specifics.** "We're premium and approachable" is nothing. "We charge 2× the
  category and answer every email in an hour" is a strategy. When an answer is abstract, ask
  for the concrete behaviour or example behind it.
- **Capture their words.** The customer's and founder's exact phrasing is raw material for
  voice and messaging later. Write it down verbatim, don't paraphrase into marketing-speak.
- **The three load-bearing groups** are Audience, Differentiation and Personality & values —
  do not advance to synthesis until those have real answers.

---

## 1. Business & goals

- In one sentence, what does the business do — and what's the one thing you'd want a customer
  to repeat to a friend about you?
- Why does this company exist beyond making money? (the Why)
- Where do you want to be in 12 months? In 3 years? What does success actually look like?
- Is this a brand-new brand, a refresh, or a full rebrand? What's prompting it now?
- What's the budget and timeline for this work, and who signs it off?

## 2. Audience *(skip if `icp.md` exists — defer to it)*

- Who is the ideal customer — not just demographics, but their situation and mindset?
- What problem are they trying to solve when they come to you?
- How do they feel *before* using you, and how do they want to feel *after*?
- Where do they spend their attention (platforms, publications, communities)?
- Any important secondary audiences — investors, talent, partners, referrers?

## 3. Competitors & category

- Who are your top 3–5 competitors or alternatives (including "do nothing" and DIY)?
- How do they look, sound and position themselves? What's the category convention everyone
  follows that you could break?
- Who do you admire — in your category and outside it — and what specifically about them?

## 4. Personality & values

- If the brand were a person walking into a room, describe them in three words.
- Which of these feels closest: the caring helper, the rebel, the expert sage, the hero, the
  playful jester, the visionary creator, the dependable everyman, the luxury ruler…?
  *(maps to the 12 archetypes — see `strategy-frameworks.md`)*
- What does the brand believe? What would it never do or say, even if it cost a sale?
- Plot the brand on each slider: formal ↔ casual · serious ↔ playful · premium ↔ accessible ·
  classic ↔ modern · understated ↔ bold.

## 5. Differentiation *(competitive Qs skippable if `positioning.md` exists)*

- Why do customers *really* choose you over the alternatives — in their words, not your pitch?
- What can you say that no competitor can honestly say?
- What's the single most compelling reason to believe you'll deliver? (proof, mechanism, track record)
- Finish this: "We're the only ___ that ___."

## 6. Visual preferences & mood

- Brands or logos you love — and ones you hate. Why, in each case?
- Colours that feel right for this brand, and any that feel wrong.
- Anything mandatory (an existing colour, mark, or name with equity) or forbidden?
- Modern or traditional? Minimal or expressive? Warm or cool?
- Can you point me to 5–10 reference images / sites that feel like the right territory?
  *(capture the why — these seed the moodboard direction, not the final design)*

## 7. Practical constraints

- Where will the identity show up — web, app, print, packaging, signage, merch, decks?
- Any legal, trademark, or naming constraints?
- Existing brand equity to preserve (a colour, a symbol, a tagline customers know)?
- Accessibility requirements (contrast, legibility, reading level)?
- Who are the decision-makers and approvers for the final brand?
