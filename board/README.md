# Your AI Board of Executives — Quickstart

Your Agentic OS now has a standing board of executives: nine seats (an independent Chair, CEO,
Finance, Operations, Marketing, Sales, People, Technology, and an outside NED) that argue every
big decision from nine different angles before you commit. The board argues. You decide.

## Get it

Easiest way: open Claude in your Agentic OS folder and say "Fetch and merge the latest OS
update from origin." Or in a terminal:

    git fetch origin
    git merge --no-edit origin/main

That brings in `board/` (the seats, the board's memory) and the `str-board-sitting` skill.
The update only adds new files; your own work is untouched.

## Run your first sitting (two minutes to start)

Open Claude in your OS folder and say:

    board sitting: should we [a real decision you're facing]?

Bring something with actual tension in it — hire the second tech or put the money into ads,
raise prices or hold, take the awkward contract or pass. Here's what happens:

1. **The board interviews you first.** Five to seven sharp questions — the ones a real board
   would ask before advising. Answer honestly, with real numbers.
2. **The seats argue.** Each of the nine looks at your decision through its own lens,
   independently. No seat is allowed to just agree with you.
3. **The Chair closes.** For each option: the case for, the case against, and what would have
   to be true for it to be the right call. Then one recommendation for your consideration —
   never an order. The call stays yours.
4. **The minutes are kept.** Every sitting is logged with a review date, and the board opens
   the next sitting by checking how its last advice actually worked out.

The first time you run it, the board will offer to build your Business Brief (your numbers,
team, goals, problems) — say yes; ten minutes, once, and every future sitting argues from your
real situation.

## The habit that makes it pay

Any decision over $1,000 or over a week of your time goes past the board before you commit.
Five minutes of arguing with a machine catches the expensive mistake while it's still cheap.

And the test of whether it's working: **at least one seat should sting.** If everyone agreed
with you, say "that was too agreeable, run the sitting again and argue properly."
