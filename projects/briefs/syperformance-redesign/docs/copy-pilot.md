## Pilot 1 — B Series Single Lobe Billet Rockers

*In-house · Honda B-series · Valvetrain · currently 9 words of copy*
**Primary phrase:** `B series single lobe rockers` · **secondary, from existing tags:** VTEC killer rockers, B16 rockers, B18 rockers, GSR rockers, Civic Si rockers

### LEAD — `body_html` (78 words)

A single lobe rocker conversion for the Honda B-series, manufactured by SYPerformance. It replaces the factory VTEC rocker assembly with one-piece billet rockers running a single cam lobe profile throughout the rev range — no changeover, no VTEC hardware, nothing in the valvetrain waiting to be actuated.

Fits B16, B18, GSR and Type R heads. Sold as a set of 8. Also known in the B-series community as a VTEC killer conversion, and it is the same idea our K-series set applies to the K20 and K24.

### WHY THIS PART EXISTS — `custom.why_this_part` (186 words)

VTEC is a mechanical changeover. At the switch point, oil pressure drives a pin through three rockers to lock them together so they follow the high-lift lobe as one. It is an elegant system and it works for the job Honda designed it for — a road engine that spends most of its life below the crossover.

A built B-series does not live that life. Once cams get aggressive and spring pressures come up to control them, the valvetrain spends its time in the rev range where the locking pin, the lost-motion springs and the three-piece rocker assembly are all doing work under load. Every one of those is a moving part in a system that is already the highest-stress area of the engine, and every one is a part that can fail.

A single lobe conversion removes the changeover entirely. One profile, one rocker, no pin, no lost motion. You give up the drivability of a low-lift lobe below the crossover, which is a real trade on a street car and no trade at all on a car that is built to be driven hard.

This is a conversion worth understanding before your next head build, because the decision is easier to make with the head already apart.

### BEFORE YOU BUY — `custom.before_you_buy` (94 words)

This is a valvetrain decision, not a bolt-on. Things to have settled first:

- **Your cam choice drives this.** Single lobe rockers run one profile, so the cam has to be right for how the car is actually used. Fitting these to a mild street cam gives up VTEC's low-lift manners for nothing.
- **Below the crossover changes.** Expect different idle and low-rpm behaviour. On a street-driven car this is the trade you are making.
- **Set of 8, B-series heads only.** The K-series conversion is a separate part — do not cross them.
- **Springs and retainers are not included.**

## Pilot 2 — SYP B-Series Billet Halfshaft Carrier Assembly

*In-house · Honda B-series · Drivetrain · currently on sale at $265 from $305*
**Primary phrase:** `B series halfshaft carrier` · **secondary:** intermediate shaft support, B-series axle carrier, bearing support

### LEAD — `body_html` (71 words)

A billet halfshaft carrier assembly for the Honda B-series, manufactured by SYPerformance. It replaces the factory intermediate shaft support — the bracket and bearing that carry the right-hand halfshaft between the transmission and the hub.

It is the part most people find out about the hard way, usually while diagnosing something else. Available for B-series here; the K-series carrier is a separate part.

### WHY THIS PART EXISTS — `custom.why_this_part` (191 words)

The intermediate shaft support does a job nobody thinks about: it holds one end of the right halfshaft in a fixed position so the inner joint runs at the angle it was designed to run at.

The factory part is a stamped bracket with a bearing in it, sized for a stock engine's torque. Under a built engine's torque it flexes. Not dramatically, and not visibly — but enough that the shaft's position moves under load, and once it moves, the inner CV joint is working at an angle it was never meant to see. Every launch repeats it.

What that produces is a set of symptoms that get blamed on other things. Vibration that comes and goes with load. Inner joints that wear out faster than they should. Axles that keep failing and get replaced with progressively more expensive axles, which keep failing, because the axle was never the problem — the thing holding it was.

A billet carrier does not flex. That is the entire point of it, and it is why this part tends to get bought twice: once by people who researched it, and once by people who have already replaced two sets of axles.

### BEFORE YOU BUY — `custom.before_you_buy` (88 words)

- **This is a supporting part, not an axle.** If you are already breaking axles it belongs in the same order, but it does not replace them.
- **B-series specific.** The K-series carrier is a different part; the families share a case but not this component.
- **Check what else moved.** If the car has been launched hard on a flexing carrier, inspect the inner joints on the existing shafts before reusing them.
- **Assembly vs. bare carrier** — confirm which variant you need for your build.

## Pilot 3 — SYP Billet HD Vanjen Clamps

*In-house · all platforms · Fabrication & hardware · $85*
**Primary phrase:** `HD Vanjen clamp` · **secondary:** vanjen coupler, charge pipe clamp, boost pipe connection

### LEAD — `body_html` (66 words)

A heavy-duty Vanjen-style clamp manufactured by SYPerformance, for joining charge piping and boost tubes without a coupler and two worm-drive clamps.

A Vanjen connection is a machined interlock: the two pipe ends locate into each other with an O-ring seal, and the clamp holds them together. It fits where a silicone coupler would go and it does not care how hot or how boosted the pipe gets.

### WHY THIS PART EXISTS — `custom.why_this_part` (178 words)

Charge pipe connections are where boost gets lost, and they are almost always the cheapest part of the system.

A silicone coupler and two worm clamps work fine until the engine makes enough torque to move the pipe. Then the joint sees a pull it was never intended to resist, and one of two things happens: the coupler walks off the pipe, or it balloons enough to break the seal. Both feel the same from the driver's seat — a sudden loss of boost, usually at the worst moment, usually under full load.

Heat cycling makes it worse over a season. Silicone that has been to temperature and back a few hundred times is not the silicone that was installed.

A Vanjen connection removes the failure mode rather than reinforcing it. The pipes locate mechanically into each other, so the joint carries load through metal rather than through a rubber sleeve's grip. It cannot walk off. It also takes up less room than a coupler, which matters in the engine bays these parts live in.

Nobody plans for clamps. Everybody replaces them.

### BEFORE YOU BUY — `custom.before_you_buy` (92 words)

- **Your pipes need Vanjen ends.** This is a clamp for a machined interlock joint, not a universal band clamp — it will not join two plain pipe ends. If your piping is not already set up for it, that is fabrication work.
- **Confirm your pipe diameter** before ordering.
