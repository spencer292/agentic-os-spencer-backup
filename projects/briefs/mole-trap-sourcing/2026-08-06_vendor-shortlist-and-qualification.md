# Vendor Shortlist & Qualification Plan

## The single most important sourcing decision

**Buy from a precision spring shop, not from a trap factory.**

Chinese trap factories (the ones that come up first when you search "mole trap
manufacturer") make zinc-plated carbon-steel snap traps by the container for the
retail channel. They will quote you $1.88 all day and deliver exactly the wrong
product — because their entire process is built around plated mild steel and they
have no way to hold a spring temper or measure closing force. That $1.88 quote is
almost certainly one of these.

A **precision spring / CNC wire-form shop** is a different animal. They make
torsion springs for automotive and appliance customers, usually to IATF 16949,
which means they buy certified spring wire by grade and temper, run stress-relief
ovens, and own force-testing equipment as a matter of course. Your trap is five
wire parts with no welds — it is a trivially easy job *for that kind of shop* and
an impossible one for a trap factory.

Everything below is organized around finding the former.

## Finding more candidates yourself

Search these exact strings on **Alibaba**, **Made-in-China.com**, and
**GlobalSources.com**:

- `custom torsion spring wire form stainless 302 OEM`
- `CNC wire forming bending stainless steel custom parts`
- `precision spring manufacturer stainless spring temper ASTM A313`
- `wire form assembly OEM stainless passivation`

Do **not** search "mole trap," "animal trap," or "rodent trap" — those queries route
you straight into the commodity trap channel you're trying to escape.

Platform notes:
- **Made-in-China.com** tends to surface a higher ratio of genuine factories to
  trading companies than Alibaba. Worth running in parallel.
- On Alibaba, filter for **Verified Supplier** (on-site third-party inspected) and
  read the **reorder rate** — above 30% means customers come back, which is the
  hardest metric to fake. On-time delivery above 95% is table stakes.
- **1688.com** shows real domestic pricing but is Chinese-language and won't export
  directly. Useful only as a price sanity check, via an agent.

Contact **10–12**. Expect ~8 replies, ~4 serious technical quotes, ~3 worth
sampling. That funnel is normal and the work is front-loaded — you do it once.

## Qualification scorecard

Score each responding vendor 0–2 per row. Anything under 14/20 doesn't get a sample.

| # | Criterion | What a 2 looks like |
|---|-----------|---------------------|
| 1 | **Factory, not trader** | Names their own address and workshop; will do a live video walkthrough of the wire-forming floor on request. A trader dodges the video. |
| 2 | **Answers the material question unprompted** | Talks about 302 vs 304 vs 17-7 PH, temper, and stress-relief temperature in their own words. This is the fastest fraud filter on the list. |
| 3 | **Owns force testing** | Has a spring force/torque tester and will report measured closing force. If they ask what a force gauge is, stop. |
| 4 | **Certs** | ISO 9001 minimum; IATF 16949 is a strong plus. Ask for the PDF and check the certificate number and expiry date on the issuing body's site. |
| 5 | **Quotes setup, not "mold cost"** | Understands CNC wire forming needs no hard tooling. |
| 6 | **In-house assembly** | Can assemble all five parts, not just form the wire. Subcontracted assembly adds a quality blind spot. |
| 7 | **An engineer on the thread** | You get a named technical contact, not only a sales rep. |
| 8 | **Response quality** | Answers all 8 RFQ questions in order. Vendors who answer 3 of 8 now will answer 3 of 8 forever. |
| 9 | **Export track record** | Ships to the US regularly; knows what a mill cert and a salt-spray report are. |
| 10 | **Willing on terms** | Accepts 30/70 against pre-shipment inspection and third-party QC. Refusal here is disqualifying. |

## Red flags — walk away

- Quote comes back in under an hour with no technical questions. A real spring
  engineer always has questions about a new part.
- "Same as your sample, no problem" with no discussion of grade or temper.
- Payment requested to a personal account, WeChat/Alipay, or a company name that
  differs from the contract. This is the classic scam pattern.
- Refuses third-party pre-shipment inspection.
- Pushes DDP hard and won't quote FOB. You are the importer of record; if they
  under-declare, the penalty is yours.
- Can't produce a mill certificate for the wire.
- Quotes a large "mold cost" for a wire-formed part.

## Sample and first-order protocol

1. **Shortlist 3.** Send 2 golden samples each (DHL, marked samples, low declared
   value) plus your dimensioned sketch.
2. **Pay for samples.** Free samples buy you a vendor's B-team. $150–500 each is
   the cheapest money in this project.
3. **Require with every sample:** a dimensional report, a measured closing-force
   number, the mill cert for the wire used, and a photo of the stress-relief oven
   log or process sheet.
4. **Test the samples yourself in the field.** Set them next to genuine traps on
   real runs — same properties, same day, same tech. Nothing on paper beats catch
   rate. Give it two weeks across a few routes.
5. **First order small — 1,000 units, not 5,000.** Accept a slightly worse unit
   price for a smaller first run. You are buying information about the supplier.
6. **Book third-party pre-shipment inspection** before releasing the 70% balance.
   SGS, TÜV, Bureau Veritas, or QIMA all do this for roughly $300/man-day. On a
   1,000-piece order that's ~1.5% of order value for the thing that prevents a
   container of bad traps.
7. **Ship after 2026-10-30.** Set it in the PO as a hard ship-not-before date.
8. **Dual-source by year two.** Once one supplier is proven, qualify a second at
   lower volume. Single-sourcing a consumable your field operation depends on is
   the risk you'll care about in 2028, not the price.

## Run this in parallel — costs nothing, may win

Send the identical RFQ to **three US precision spring / wire-form job shops.**
Search "custom torsion spring manufacturer" plus "wire forming job shop" in
Washington, Oregon, and the Midwest; the Spring Manufacturers Institute (SMI)
member directory is a clean list of real shops.

Rationale, from the tariff model in `brief.md`: with Section 232 potentially adding
50% on steel content, a $3.25 US quote can land cheaper than a $2.50 China quote —
with two-week lead times, no freight, no customs exposure, no golden samples in the
mail, and a shop you can drive to. It may well come back at $5 and settle the
question. Either way you learn it for the cost of three emails, and a real domestic
quote is the best leverage you'll have in the China negotiation.
