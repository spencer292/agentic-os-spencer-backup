# US Spring Shop Shortlist — Parallel RFQ

## The finding: the Pacific Northwest cluster is in Oregon, not Washington

No strong precision spring shop turned up in Washington state. Several results that
*looked* local were not — Stalder Spring Works is Springfield, **Ohio**; Western
Spring Manufacturing is Hugo, **Minnesota**. Both came back high in searches for
"Washington custom springs," which is a good reminder that spring-shop SEO is noisy
and worth verifying by address every time.

The real regional cluster is **Portland metro** — Canby, Wilsonville, and Sherwood
sit within about 15 miles of each other. All three are **2.5–3.5 hours from Puget
Sound**: a day trip, and close enough that you can stand on the floor and watch your
part get made. For a supplier relationship meant to last years, that is worth real
money.

## The US-specific question that decides everything: who assembles?

Most spring shops **form wire and ship parts in a bag.** They do not assemble
multi-piece products. This is the single biggest reason a domestic quote falls over,
and it is the first thing to ask — before price.

Three possible answers, all workable:

1. **They assemble in-house.** Best case. Quote is apples-to-apples with the China
   FOB number.
2. **They form, someone else assembles.** Ask them to recommend a local assembly
   shop, or price it against a sheltered-workshop / light-assembly contractor. Simple
   wire assembly runs roughly $0.30–0.75/unit at this volume. Add it to the quote.
3. **You assemble.** At 5,000/yr this is genuinely on the table — it is five wire
   parts and no fasteners. A tech doing 60–80/hour in the shop off-season is maybe
   70–85 hours a year. That is real labour, but it is *your* labour at *your* pace,
   with zero freight, zero customs, and a two-week reorder cycle.

Get every US shop to quote **formed parts only** and **assembled**, as separate lines.

## What to send them

The same RFQ from `2026-08-06_rfq-technical-spec.md`, with three changes:

- Drop the questions about export experience, salt spray reports, and mill certs
  from China — a US shop working to ISO 9001 supplies material certs as standard.
- **Lead with the sample.** US shops will quote from a physical trap far more
  readily than an overseas supplier will. Mail one trap plus your measurement sheet
  and let them tell you what it costs to make.
- Ask for **formed-only** and **assembled** pricing at 1,000 / 3,000 / 5,000 /
  10,000, plus setup.

Then call. Oregon spring shops answer the phone, and a ten-minute conversation with
a coiling technician about your part will teach you more about this trap than
another week of research.

## What a win looks like

From the tariff model in `brief.md`: with Section 232 potentially stacking 50% on
steel content, the China landed cost sits somewhere between **$3.49 and $4.74**.

**Any US quote at or under about $3.50 assembled wins outright** — and it wins on
more than price: two-week lead times instead of ninety days, no customs exposure, no
importer-of-record liability, no golden samples in international mail, no 20%
deposit sitting with a company you've never visited, and reorders in units of
hundreds instead of thousands.

A US quote of $4.50–5.50 still beats Trapline's $6.50 and is worth taking seriously
as the low-risk option or as a second source.

Above roughly $6, the domestic route is dead and you go to China with a real
domestic quote in your pocket — which is itself worth having, because it is the
best leverage you will get in that negotiation.

Either way: **three emails and two phone calls to find out.**
