Close rate alone hides this. Same window, both salespeople:

| Spencer, since 06-01 | 218 | 47% (103) | $871 |

Muhammad closes at 68% and Spencer at 83% — a 15-point gap. But Spencer sells the annual **three
times as often**, and that is worth more than the close-rate gap: **$315 per sale**. Across the 27
sales Muhammad has already made, closing at Spencer’s plan mix would have been worth roughly
**$8,500** more than closing at Spencer’s *rate* would have been.

This is coachable and it is the same lever as everything else: **TMCP is sold by beat 5** — moles
are territorial, catch this one and the next moves into the empty territory, so it is not if but
when. He only started landing beats before the price on 08-17 (four of five on the Kamat call,
including the net for the first time). The plan mix should follow the block.

One thing to watch, on n=1 so far: **of his four multi-quote leads, the only one that has resolved
took the cheaper option** (Mccormick converted the $450 Quick Fix while the $1,200 TMCP sits open),
and the other three are all still unresolved. Sending both plans may not just halve the measured
rate — it may anchor the customer down. Another reason to recommend one.

## 3. Why: six of eight were never opened

Every one of his 14 wins was a quote the customer opened. All fourteen. **Six of the eight that
didn't close have never been opened at all** — $4,125 of the $5,325 left on the table.

Your own matured book explains what that costs:

| Your matured quotes | Win rate |
|---|---:|
| Customer opened it | **80%** (132/164) |
| Customer never opened it | **17%** (3/18) |

An unread quote is worth about a fifth of a read one. That single fact is the entire difference
between his 64% and your 83%.

**He is improving on this.** Follow-up touches rose during the week — Laidley and Breiland both went
from 1 to 2, Hemphill and Crenshaw-Burns are at 3. But Yattaw and Thompson are still sitting on a
single send, and his average on open quotes is **1.9** against your **3.5 on quotes you are losing**.
You chase hardest when it is going badly; he still mostly sends once and waits.

## Method / caveats

- Won = `converted` or `approved`. Opportunity = one client's quotes inside a 14-day window.
- Realization curve and the opened/unopened base rates come from Spencer's 185 quotes with 21+ days
  to land, using `transitionedAt − createdAt`.
- **n = 22.** One customer moves the rate ~4.5 points. Treat 69–72% as a direction, not a decimal.
- **None of this sees leads that never became a quote** — the 08-10 call-6 buyer, the 08-11 Kirkland
  buyer, the 98444 Parkland refusal, the 08-12 Fall City caller. Those are his real losses and they
  sit outside every number here. His lead-to-sale rate is lower than his quote-to-sale rate, and that
  failure is capture, not closing.
- One week-1 close is invisible to all of it: Beverly Ott, an existing customer booked directly with
  no quote. 112 quote-less jobs in the period carry no salesperson, so that lane cannot be measured
  as things stand — worth fixing if direct bookings become common.
