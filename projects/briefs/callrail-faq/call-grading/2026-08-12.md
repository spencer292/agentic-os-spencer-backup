## 4. What needs work — ranked by what it cost

### 4.1 He had a yes and asked it away (Fall City, 15:14) — the most expensive four seconds of the day

The caller said he'd shop around, and Mo did the right thing and offered the quote anyway. The caller
**accepted, and volunteered the channel**:

> Caller: *"Yeah, sure. You can send it to my email if that works."*

Then:

> Mo: *"Yes, I'm gonna need like your first and last name, also the phone number, the physical address.
> Is that okay?"*
> Caller: *"I don't want to go that far for now."*
> Mo: *"No problem. No problem at all."*

Three fields in one sentence, one of them the address, to a man who had just asked for an email. The
rubric says capture fields go **one at a time, waiting for each answer** — and here the answer he
needed was already on the table. He should have said:

> **"Perfect — what's the best email? … Got it. And your first name so I can put it on the quote?"**

Take the email alone, send the quote, get the rest when he approves it. Nothing was captured, nothing
is in Jobber, and a 98024 buyer who liked the answers is now somebody else's customer. **Fourth
consecutive live day that a long, engaged, going-well call ends with nothing on file.** This is still
the most expensive habit he has.
