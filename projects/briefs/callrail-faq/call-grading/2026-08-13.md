## 4. What needs work — ranked by what it cost

### 4.1 The Ott booking — recorded, but not as anything that reaches a route

**Update, confirmed with Spencer:** Mo worked out afterwards that the property was an existing
customer, called Beverly back, and scheduled the job — without a quote, on the **John Ott** record.
Unprompted, before anyone raised it with him. He caught his own mistake and fixed it, and that is the
headline on this call.

**What I can and can't verify.** Spencer says it is in Jobber under John Ott. Through the API I can't
see it, and I checked hard: no new job on the John Ott record (his only job is still **#1253,
archived, 2021**), no new quote, no new Ott client created today, and — scanning **every visit
scheduled between 08-13 and 09-15** — **no visit at 5719 Upland Terrace**.

There is exactly one Jobber object my API token cannot read:

> `"An object of type Request was hidden due to permissions"`

So if it was logged as a **Request**, that is precisely where it would be and I am blind to it. That
is the most likely explanation, and it matters operationally rather than as a bookkeeping point:
**a Request is not a scheduled visit.** It doesn't carry line items, it doesn't get a tech, and it
doesn't reach OptimoRoute. She was told Monday. Unless it is converted to a job with visits before
then, nobody is routed to her.

**When it is converted, build it like the two that went out correctly today.** **#8370 Shawn Tobius**
(Enumclaw) and **#8374 Richard Kaumans** (Olympia) were both built as a $450 Quick Fix line item plus
a **−$75 Repeat Customer Discount line item = $375** — exactly the build discipline that was a defect
on 08-10, now landing. The remaining open item either way is the **$150 setup fee**: with no quote
there is no approval step, so nothing has collected it.

### 4.2 He answered a household question with a first-name lookup, and closed it

The file is under **John Ott — her husband.** So a search for "Beverly Ott" was never going to find
it, and strictly speaking *Beverly* had not hired us before. What she asked about, though, was the
property:

> Caller: *"We had somebody last year, and I'm not sure who."*
> Mo: *"Okay, give me your first and last name, so I'll check on my file."* … *"Okay, no, you never
> hired us before."*

She told him the address had prior service, he went looking under her first name, found nothing, and
gave her a definitive **no**. The right answer was one search away: the number she was calling from is
the number on the record, and her own email — `bott@nventure.com` — is already on her husband's file.

The lesson is exactly the same one, and cleaner for it: **a name identifies a person, a number
identifies a household.** Spouses, property managers, adult kids calling for parents — the name will
miss and the number will hit. This is the identical failure to yesterday's Travis Bruce call and to
the duplicate-records write-up. Two days running.

He quoted her the standard price rather than the **$375 returning rate** — and he demonstrably knows
that rate, having applied it correctly with the discount line item twice today on other jobs. (Whether
a 2021 job still earns it is Spencer's call — see §7.)

**The mitigation is real and it changes the coaching read.** He worked it out afterwards without being
told and called her back to fix it. So the gap is not knowledge or diligence — it is the single
mechanical habit of typing the caller's number into search instead of their name. That is one drill,
not a lecture.

**Scoring note:** this was originally marked −5 as a confident wrong answer, putting the call at 72.
Given the file is her husband's, the statement was narrowly true of Beverly and the miss is better
described by the rubric's *"−3 mushy answer to a factual question he could have answered correctly."*
**Re-scored 74.**
