# Spencer's close — his exact words

Transcribed verbatim from 47 of Spencer's own inbound sales calls (CallRail, 2026-04-30 → 07-20).
Nothing here is invented. Percentages are how often each beat appears across those 47 calls.

---

## The structure

Spencer's close is four beats, always in this order:

| | Beat | Appears in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Take control of the logistics** — "I've got everything I need" | 49% |
| 2 | **Announce the quote** — "I'm going to send you over a quote" | 51% |
| 3 | **Shrink the ask** — "All you have to do is approve it" | 64% |
| 4 | **Name the day** — "I'll get you on the schedule for Monday" | 55% (specific day named: 57%) |

**The thing that makes it work: the quote is beat 2 of 4, not the finish line.** It is announced as
a step on the way to a date that has already been named. The customer is never left deciding
whether to buy — they are told what happens next and asked only to tap approve.

This is the entire difference from Muhammad's close, which stops at beat 2: *"I'm going to send it
over in a moment."* That hands the decision to an inbox.

---

## Beat 1 — take control of the logistics

> "Perfect. **I've got everything I need.**"
> "All right, **I got everything I need.**"
> "Okay, **I think I have everything I need.**"
> "All right, **let me get all your information** and then…"
> "Okay, **I'm gonna do this. I'm gonna get all your information.**"

Signals the selling is over and the paperwork has begun. There is no "so what do you think?" —
he never asks for the decision, he assumes it.

## Beat 2 — announce the quote

> "**I'm going to send you over a quote.**"
> "**I'll shoot you over a quote.**"
> "**I will send over the quote.**"
> "I'm going to **shoot you over a text.**"

Note the verbs: *send over*, *shoot over*. It is a small, already-in-motion act, not an offer.

## Beat 3 — shrink the ask

> "**All you have to do is approve it.**"
> "**Just approve it.**"
> "I just need you to **approve it**, and then I can get out there next week."
> "I **do have you approve it.** I have you put $150 deposit down."
> "Read through it, **approve it**, and we'll see you this week."

The entire remaining commitment is reduced to one tap. When the deposit is mentioned (32%) it is
attached to this beat, never left as a separate hurdle.

## Beat 4 — name the day

> "I'll get you on the schedule **for Monday**."
> "I'll get you on the schedule **for tomorrow**."
> "…and I will **get out there next week** and get started trapping them."
> "Once you approve the quote, **I'll get you on the schedule.** And then once I get you on the
>  schedule, I'm going to confirm the date then."
> "I'll get you on the schedule and **we'll get out there by the end of the day today.**"

He names a specific day in **57%** of calls, and ties it to the real route in 13%:

> "**I do North Seattle every Tuesday**, so that'll be the soonest I can be out there."
> "**We service that area every Wednesday and Thursday.** I got to see where you fall in that route."
> "**I do that area every Monday.**"

## Two variants worth having

**When they want to think about it / talk to a spouse** — he still closes, he just closes on the send:

> "Perfect. I will send over the quote, just approve it and we will get you on the schedule for
> next week."
> — *Deborah Cheng, 07-10, after she said she needed to talk to her husband*

**When they're torn between the two plans** — he sends both rather than losing the moment:

> "I can send you over the quote so you can look at it… and then I'll send you both quotes. I'll send
> you one for like the one month service, I'll send you one for the total mole control program."
> — *Malia Cranmer, 07-10*

> "I'm going to send you both quotes. **I do need you to approve one of them for me to come out on
> Monday.**"
> — *Rudd Ted, 06-26*

Note the second one: even sending two, he binds approval to a named day. (This is also the origin of
the double-quote habit — worth knowing it costs measured conversion rate at quote level, so use it
only when they are genuinely torn.)

## What to hand Muhammad

The one-line swap that carries most of the value:

| Muhammad says today | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "I'm going to send it over in a moment." | "I've got everything I need. I'm going to send you over a quote — **all you have to do is approve it, and I'll get you on the schedule for [day].**" |

And the rule underneath it: **never end a sales call without a day named out loud.** The quote is
what confirms the day. It is not the thing being decided.
