# Spencer's story bank — what to tell, when, and how Muhammad tells it

Pulled verbatim from 47 of Spencer's own inbound sales calls (CallRail, 2026-04-30 → 07-20).
Nothing invented. Every quote below is something Spencer actually said on a recorded call.

**The one rule that matters:** most of these are *Spencer's* stories. Muhammad cannot say "I."
He says **"Spencer — the owner —"** and the story gets *stronger*, not weaker, because now
it's a guy vouching for his boss instead of a guy bragging about himself. Each story below
has a **Muhammad's version** line showing the swap.

---

## Tier 1 — the three that close deals

### 1. "You picked the wrong house" — Spencer's own four acres

**Told in 10 of 47 calls. This is the single most-used story and the one that sells TMCP.**

> "I've got four acres out in Enumclaw and 50 acres of abandoned jungle behind me. When I moved
> in, there was no lawn. I put in about an acre of grass because I wanted my kids to be able to
> go play barefoot. And before I even had grass growing, I had moles tearing up my yard. And
> that's not a good look for the mole guy. So I started trapping them in the yard, and then I
> pushed everything out to the perimeters. I was catching two, three moles a week to start. Then
> a mole a week. Then a mole every other week. Then a mole a month. Now I go check the traps
> every couple weeks and sometimes there's a mole in there and sometimes there isn't — and I
> have never had a hill in my yard since. The traps never leave."
> — *Wireless Caller Bethesda 06-29, Gyasi Ross 07-01, Timothy Van Strien 06-29, and 7 more*

**Why it works:** it is the entire TMCP pitch as a lived experience instead of a product
description. It also quietly proves the perimeter method works on the hardest possible property
(50 acres of untrapped land feeding moles at him forever).

**Where it goes:** right after the two options are laid out, before the price.

**Muhammad's version:** *"Spencer does this at his own place. He's got four acres in Enumclaw
with 50 acres of woods behind it, so the moles just keep coming. He put in a lawn for his kids
and had moles in it before the grass was even up. He perimeter-trapped it and went from two or
three moles a week down to maybe one a month — and he hasn't had a molehill in his own yard in
years. The traps never leave."*

### 2. "Nobody calls me once"

**Told in 6 calls. This is the argument that makes the Quick Fix feel temporary.**

> "I've been doing this for nearly 20 years, and I can count the customers who called me one
> time on one hand. Everybody calls multiple times, and most often it's multiple times a year.
> The ones who only called once — it's because they sold the house or moved out of state."
> — *Wireless Caller Tacoma 06-30, Wireless Caller Bothell 07-09, Gyasi Ross 07-01*

**Why it works:** it converts "is it a chance they come back?" into "it's a question of *when*"
without arguing with the customer.

**Muhammad's version:** *"Spencer's been trapping moles for close to 20 years, and he says he
can count on one hand the customers who only ever called once — and those had sold the house or
moved. It's not really a question of if they come back, it's when."*

---

### 3. The $40,000 crawl space

**Told once, but it is the strongest damage story on the board.**

> "I've seen them do damage to the foundations of homes. I had one guy — this was the craziest
> thing ever. He had a water feature fountain in the backyard. A mole tunneled up into that and
> then tunneled all the way into his crawl space. He was on vacation when it happened. It
> flooded his whole crawl space. Cost him about $40,000 to repair all the damage."
> — *Timothy Van Strien, Tacoma, 06-29*

**Why it works:** answers "is this really worth $450?" with a number 90x bigger, without any
scare-selling tone — Spencer tells it as a weird story, not a warning.

**Where it goes:** only when the caller asks whether moles actually cause damage, or is treating
it as cosmetic. Never lead with it.

**Muhammad tells this one straight** — it's a company story, not a personal one: *"We had a
customer whose mole tunneled from a backyard water fountain into his crawl space while he was on
vacation. Flooded the whole thing. About forty grand to fix."*

## Tier 2 — the myth-killers

### 4. The wagyu steak vs. the Beyond Meat steak

**Told in 3 calls, near-identically. This is Spencer's best explainer, full stop.**

> "Imagine you eat wagyu beef every day, and then I try to trick you with one of these Beyond
> Meat 3D-printed bean-curd steaks. You're going to know instantly it's not the right thing.
> Moles smell extremely well. Your yard is already full of worms — that's the food source. They
> smell the chemicals on those poison worms and they're just like, yeah, no, that ain't it."
> — *Nexus Capital 05-05, Peter Meechan 07-03, Waaramaki Leah 06-15*

**Where it goes:** the moment someone mentions poison worms, pellets, castor oil, or "I tried
everything from Home Depot."

**Muhammad owns this one** — it's an analogy, not a memory. Tell it as his own.

### 5. The Harry Potter marathon

> "Moles live underground in the dark and they're very sensitive to light. You just spent all
> weekend watching your Harry Potter marathon and then you walk outside and the sun's bright —
> same concept. If you open the tunnel up and don't cover it, they walk into that bright light
> and they know something's up. So we keep everything covered and staked down. And that also
> means you never have to look at a dead mole."
> — *Peter Meechan, 07-03*

**Does double duty:** explains why there's a bucket in the yard *and* answers the "will I see
dead moles?" question in the same breath. **Muhammad owns this one too.**

### 6. Caddyshack meets the Beverly Hillbillies — the origin story

> "My dad has 10 acres out in Buckley and he's really into his lawn. When I was about 15 we were
> doing everything we could to kill these moles. It was like Caddyshack meets the Beverly
> Hillbillies. We got to the point of pumping oxy-acetylene into the runs and blowing up the
> runs — blowing up my dad's yard. We tried everything. And then I figured out the trapping, and
> the rest is history. I trapped for my dad, then his HOA, then the airport, then the dairy
> across the street. Everybody found out I was the mole guy. I did it for beer money for years
> as a kid, and then it became my entire life."
> — *Peter Meechan, 07-03, when the caller asked "how'd you get into this?"*

**Why it works:** it is the "I've tried everything that doesn't work" credential, and it's funny.
It also explains why the company is trapping-only without sounding like a policy.

**Muhammad's version:** *"Spencer got into this at fifteen — his dad had ten acres in Buckley and
they tried literally everything to kill the moles, up to pumping acetylene into the tunnels and
blowing up the yard. Nothing worked until he figured out trapping. Then he was doing the
neighbor's HOA and the dairy across the street, and it just became his life."*

## Tier 3 — the trust builders

### 7. The dog that yanked the trap (tell the truth, it sells better)

> "I've been doing this nearly 20 years and I'd say I've never had an issue with a dog — but
> last week I did. The dog didn't get caught in the trap. It heard the trap go off, knocked the
> bucket off, yanked the trap out of the ground with the dead mole in it, and was fighting the
> mole. Thank God the guy it happened to is a super cool New Yorker — he called me up jacked
> about it. I was like, thank God this happened to you and nobody else."
> — *Ratkoviak Anna, Seattle, 07-14*

**Why it works:** the honest version outperforms "that never happens." He still lands the safety
point (the dog was never in danger; everything is covered and staked), but the customer now
believes him about everything else. Clean up the language for the phone.

### 8. Cat and mouse — "I've got one right now"

> "I've got one right now where I showed up the first week, there was a bunch of molehills, I set
> the traps, and the moles disappeared. Two weeks later they reappeared, so I moved everything to
> a different side of the yard — and the mole disappeared on me again. And I'm like, this is fun.
> Now this week it popped up in a different part of the lawn and I'm after it. I haven't caught
> him yet. And I'm not going to leave you with moles."
> — *Jessica Sherman, Auburn, 07-10*

**This is the guarantee, told as behavior instead of policy.** Use it right after quoting $450 —
it answers "what if you don't catch it" before the caller has to ask.

**Muhammad's version:** *"There's a job going right now where the mole keeps moving on us — the
tech's moved the whole setup twice and we still haven't got him. We just stay out there. There's
no extra charge for that. We're not going to leave you with moles."*

### 9. "I've been trapping on your street for years"

> "All right, dude, I'm just going to tell you right now — I've been trapping the moles on Waller
> Road for years and years and years. I trapped the moles at that soccer field down there, right
> off River Road. I've been trapping there for 10 years, and every time I'm out there it is
> never-ending, non-stop."
> — *Timothy Van Strien, Tacoma, 06-29 — said the second he heard the address*

Also in the bank: **Marymoor Park**, **Renton Municipal Airport**, a **28-acre job in Federal
Way**, and **18 acres in Enumclaw** where he traps the entire perimeter.

**Why it works:** the caller stops evaluating a vendor and starts hearing a neighbor. Say it the
*instant* you hear the street or the town, not later.

**Muhammad's version:** he can't claim the history, but he can claim the route — *"Oh, Waller
Road — we've been trapping that street for years. We do the soccer field off River Road too."*
Then hand off to service-day language (see `service-day-lookup/`).

### 10. Mom's house

> "I trap my own house this way. I trap my mother's house this way."
> — *Jessica Sherman 07-10, Rudd Ted 06-26*

Nine words, and it's the whole "would you do this to your own family" test. Muhammad: *"He traps
his own place this way and his mom's house too."*

### 11. Cory quit a $100k job

> "You're going to get the same thing out of Cory as you get out of me. He's been my best friend
> since we were fifteen. When I started trying to hire people for this business, I had to convince
> this guy to quit a hundred-thousand-dollar-a-year job five or six years ago. I couldn't afford to
> pay him — the only way I could do it was to offer him a stake in the business. He's been a
> blessing."
> — *Hunter Colleen, Seattle, 06-29*

**Use when the caller asks "will it be you personally who comes out?"** — the answer is no, and
this is how you make no feel like yes. Muhammad should swap in whichever tech owns that territory.

### 12. "It has a soul" — why they didn't sell to private equity

> "My friend Luke Rambo got offered a whole bunch of money a few years ago to sell his business
> to a private equity group. He thought about it, because it's life-changing money for people
> like us with families. He didn't, because he's still having fun. I've been watching private
> equity groups buy up each other's pest control businesses, and I've seen the way they get run —
> they just get run into the ground, and nobody's happy with them. This business has seen us
> through good times and bad, and it has a soul. I'm not selling that."
> — *Hunter Colleen, Seattle, 06-29*

**Use when they're comparing against a big national pest company.** Positions on ownership, not
price. Muhammad tells it in the third person, which is honestly the better delivery.

### 13. Why there's a one-year term

> "When I first started this I didn't have an early cancellation fee. It was a handshake deal.
> And I had several people hire me, go for one month, pay me the hundred dollars, and then cancel
> — when I'd just done $450 worth of work up front. Ninety-nine percent of people are honest and
> decent. But that's why the term exists."
> — *Deborah Cheng, 07-10*

**The only good answer to "why am I locked in for a year?"** It reframes the term as protection
against the 1%, not a trap for the 99%, and Spencer says the honest-people part out loud.

### 14. The Enumclaw customer who does the math for you

> "I've got a guy in Enumclaw with about an acre and a half that backs up to an 18-acre farm, and
> the moles just keep coming. I've been trapping there five years. Rather than calling me every
> other month and paying $450 each time, he just pays me a hundred bucks a month and I'm out
> there all the time."
> — *Anne Nguyen, Seattle, 05-27*

**This is the price comparison, told as a person.** Use it when someone says $100/month sounds
like a lot — the alternative is $450 several times a year.

### 15. "Lived here 30 years and never had a mole" — steering someone *away* from TMCP

> "The Quick Fix is really good for somebody who's never dealt with moles before. If you called
> me and said 'dude, I've been dealing with these for five years,' I'd steer you in a different
> direction. But I think the place to start for you is the Quick Fix."
> — *Rancourt Kyle 06-26, Jessica Sherman 07-10, Kulapaditharom 05-26*

**Spencer talks people out of the more expensive plan on the record, repeatedly.** This is the
highest-trust move in the whole call and it costs nothing — the Quick Fix converts to TMCP later.
Muhammad should copy this exactly.

### 16. Mole Masters made him — *handle with care*

> "I've known Nick and that business a long time. The reason I'm in business is because of them.
> Nick came out to my dad's house to trap the moles, and my dad was too cheap to pay the guy — so
> my chore as a kid was to trap the moles. That's how I got into this. Because of Mole Masters."
> — *Hunter Colleen, Seattle, 06-29*

**Only for callers who are actively price-comparing against Mole Masters and bring them up first.**
Spencer can afford to be generous about his biggest competitor because it's his origin story.
Muhammad should not volunteer a competitor's name — if the caller names them, this is the reply.
