# Why Muhammad doesn't sell TMCP

**Measured 2026-09-01** against live Jobber data (every quote since 2026-06-01, refetched today)
and against 15 of his sales calls read in full from 2026-08-28 and 2026-08-31.

---

## 1. The number

| | Muhammad | Spencer |
|---|---:|---:|
| Wins (converted + approved) | 85 | 227 |
| …that are TMCP | **19 = 22%** | **109 = 48%** |
| Quotes he *sends* that are TMCP | 34 / 118 = **29%** | 144 / 305 = **47%** |

Spencer's 48% is not a good month — it is 52% in June, 46% in July, 49% in August. It is a
constant. Muhammad's 22% is his whole book.

**The gap is not in closing. It is in what gets offered.**

| Of every lead who received a quote | Muhammad | Spencer |
|---|---:|---:|
| Quick Fix only — TMCP never quoted | **69%** | 48% |
| TMCP only | 22% | **41%** |
| Both quotes sent | 8% | 12% |

Seven out of ten of his leads never receive a TMCP quote at all. The decision is already over
by the time anything reaches an inbox.

### And when both quotes do go out, his lose

| Both quotes sent → which won | Muhammad | Spencer |
|---|---:|---:|
| Quick Fix won | 4 | 3 |
| **TMCP won** | **0** | **14** |
| Neither (still open / dead) | 5 | 14 |

**Nought for nine.** Every multi-quote lead Muhammad has decided has gone to the $450.
Spencer's split the other way, 14 to 3. Two people sending the identical pair of PDFs to the
same kind of customer, with opposite results — which means the pair of PDFs is not what decides
it. **The call decides it, and the PDFs record the decision.** For Spencer the TMCP quote is
paperwork for a sale already made. For Muhammad it is a second option a customer is being asked
to weigh on their own.

## 2. The mechanism, from the transcripts

Fifteen sales calls read in full across 08-28 and 08-31. TMCP was mentioned on 14 of 15 — so
this is not that he forgets it. It is *how* it arrives.

**Quick Fix went first on 14 of 14.** Every time. And it goes first with the whole apparatus
attached — the price, the deposit, the split, and the risk reversal:

> *"The first one is a quick fix. One month of service, five weeks of visits. It costs you 450,
> but it does require a setup fee like $150 deposit and rest of the 300 will be charged after the
> job is done. **And if we don't catch anything, then we wouldn't charge you anything other than
> the setup fee.**"* — Craig Suffian, 08-28

Then TMCP arrives, second, as a feature list with no argument in it:

> *"Other option. The second option is tmcp. That's Total Mold Control Program. This hundred
> dollars a month all year round unlimited visits. It does not require any upfront pay."*
> — Craig Suffian, 08-28
>
> *"And the other option is TMCP. That's our most successful plan. It's $100 a month, all year
> round, unlimited visits. It does not require any upfront payment."* — Matthew Young, 08-31
>
> *"So that is why our second option is the best plan. It's tmcp. Total Mold Control Program.
> That's hundred dollars a month all year around. Unlimited visits. Does not require any
> deposit."* — Centralia caller, 08-31

Read those in sequence the way a caller hears them. Option A: **cheaper, and you are protected
if it doesn't work.** Option B: **$1,200, a twelve-month commitment, and three features.**
Calling it "our most successful plan" is an assertion, not a reason, and nobody buys the
expensive one on an assertion.

Matthew Young did not even let him finish the sentence:

> Mo: *"…unlimited visits. It does not require any upfront payment."*
> Caller: *"Let's do the initial setup one. The 450."*

**Beat 5 is the entire argument for TMCP, and it lands after the price or not at all.** The
five-beat card exists so that "it's not if they come back, it's when" is established *before* any
number — which makes the annual plan the obvious answer instead of the expensive one. Across 15
calls, beat 5 arrived before the price **zero times**. Where it appeared at all it appeared after
the Quick Fix price had already anchored the conversation.

### The proof that the argument works when he makes it

**Stephen Heller, 98387, 08-31.** Thirteen minutes. Mo pitched both, Heller was lost, and then
asked outright:

> *"Which one's the best service?"*

And Mo delivered it properly — beat 5, the tunnel system, the territory:

> *"Our best one is the yearly service because the thing with moles that they are territorial…
> their tunnel system is so extensive that you could catch the moles but you cannot do anything
> about the tunnel system. So it's just a matter of time. The new moles is going to find those
> empty territory and they're going to move in."*

Heller's reply, in his own words: **"that seems more like in my budget."** He was there.

Then Mo asked *"do you want me to send you the one for a quick fix or, you know, the yearly one,
or both?"* — Heller said both, both are sitting unapproved, and Heller spent the afternoon
ringing back trying to work out which of three quotes was which. **A sale that was made verbally
was handed back to the customer as homework.**

**Sandra Anchors, 98055, 08-31.** His best TMCP pitch on record. Beat 5, the perimeter
explanation, the DIY teardown, the flat-rate save, the three-calls-a-year ROI, and Spencer's own
line —

> *"That's $3 a day."*
> *"So the best option I would recommend to go with TMCP…"*

— the only unprompted **recommendation** in fifteen calls. She pushed back on price and said she
was still shopping. He closed the call with: *"I'll send you a quote for a Quick fix now."*
She had asked him for *"your best quote."* He sent the cheap one.

**Kitrick Wulf, 98516, 08-31 — the control case.** Here the customer supplied the argument
himself, before Mo could bury it:

> *"I think I'd rather go with your hundred dollars a month… we just spent a few thousand dollars
> laying down our own sod, so I'd rather make sure they're gone."*

TMCP, $1,200, approved, in under five minutes. Mo's contribution was *"that is the right option"*
— **after** the customer had chosen it. That is the whole difference between 22% and 48%: on the
22%, the customer brings the reason. On the 48%, Spencer brings it.

## 3. What it costs

Muhammad has 85 wins in the ~25 working days since 08-07. At Spencer's 48% mix that would be
**41 TMCP instead of 19** — 22 more annual customers, 22 fewer one-month jobs.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Year-one revenue difference per swap ($1,200 − $450) | $750 |
| × 22 swaps | **$16,500** |
| less the ~21% of Quick Fix customers who upgrade in the field anyway (2026-08-18 study) | −$3,500 |
| **Net, on roughly one month of his book** | **≈ $13,000** |

**Caveats, stated plainly.** (a) One month extrapolated from a 25-day sample — an order of
magnitude, not a forecast. (b) Spencer's 48% is measured over three months and his leads are not
identical to Mo's. (c) It *undercounts* the real value, because a TMCP customer renews and a Quick
Fix customer does not — the enterprise-value gap is larger than the revenue gap. (d) It excludes
his ~59 connected outbound calls a week, which CallRail does not record.

---

## 4. What to change — three things, in order

**1. Flip the order. TMCP first, Quick Fix second.**
Not more education, not a longer pitch — the same two paragraphs in the opposite order. The plan
described first, at length, with the reasoning attached, is the plan that wins; right now that is
always the Quick Fix. One structural change to a script he already delivers fluently, and worth
more than everything else on this list.

**2. Beat 5 goes before the first number, every call.**
*"Before I give you prices — the thing about moles is they're territorial. The tunnel network
stays in your yard after we take the mole out, and sooner or later the next one moves into it. So
it's not really if they come back, it's when. That's what the year-round plan is built for."*
Then quote. This is the grading rubric's ceiling rule doing exactly what it was written to do —
he has never once cleared it — and it is the same sentence he already produces perfectly when a
customer asks him which plan is better.

**3. Recommend, then send ONE quote.**
He asks *"which one do you want to go with?"* and *"do you want both?"* — that is a menu. Spencer
says *"my most popular option."* The rule should be: he names the plan he recommends, the customer
agrees or doesn't, and **one** quote goes out for the plan agreed. Sending both is 0-for-9. Sending
both is how Stephen Heller ended up with three quotes and no service.

**Do not drill:** the biology, the qualifiers, the capture, the guarantee, the service-day lookup,
the DIY teardown. All of it is landing, and none of it is what is costing the annual plan.
