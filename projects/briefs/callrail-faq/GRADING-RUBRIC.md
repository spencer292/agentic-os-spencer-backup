## What gets a 100-point score

Only **sales calls** — a caller who could buy today. That means new leads and returning
customers. Everything else is graded on the letter scale in the second half of this file.

| Dimension | Points | What it measures |
|---|---:|---|
| Accuracy | 30 | Prices, terms, policy, mole facts, service area, service day |
| Script arc | 20 | Qualifiers → education block → price, **in that order** |
| Education & objections | 20 | The five beats, and what he does when pushed |
| Capture | 15 | Name, cell, email, street address, "how did you find us?" |
| Tone | 15 | Warmth, patience, non-defensiveness under pressure |

---

## Accuracy — 30

Start at 30. Deduct:

- **−8** any wrong price, term, or policy stated as fact
- **−8** a guessed mole fact (the "hundreds of miles" failure)
- **−8** a promise the company does not make (e.g. *"we guarantee we'll catch them"*)
- **−5** a wrong service day, or a day named without checking the lookup
- **−5** an out-of-area referral he made up
- **−3** a soft overpromise (*"you'll never have to worry about them again"*)
- **−3** a mushy answer to a factual question he could have answered correctly

**The correct facts are in `2026-07-20_muhammad-faq-training.md`.** Check against it, never
against memory. Service days: `service-day-lookup/service-day-sheet.md`, by **zip**, never city.

### Hard gates — these cap the whole call at 60

1. A confident wrong **mole fact** presented as knowledge.
2. Quoting a price outside the standard sheet, or inventing a discount that is not documented.
   (The $375 returning-customer rate **is** documented and does not gate.)
3. Promising a **weekend** visit.
4. Accepting vole, gopher, or other non-mole work.
5. Quoting **commercial** work, or **residential over 5 acres**, by phone — those are Cory's
   in-person bids.

---

## Script arc — 20

The order is the point. Full marks require:

1. **Location** (get the **zip**, not the city)
2. **Acreage** — over or under an acre
3. **How long have you been dealing with them?**
4. Residential or commercial, if not obvious
5. **Then the five beats**
6. **Then the price**

- **−5** any number leaves his mouth before all four qualifiers are in
- **−5** any number leaves his mouth before the beats
- **−3** per qualifier never asked at all
- **−3** assuming which plan they want instead of confirming it

---

## Education & objections — 20

**The five beats** (`2026-08-04_five-beat-card.md`): 1 the food (70% of body weight) · 2 the net ·
3 the dinner bell · 4 the new hills · 5 territorial → not if, but when.

- **12 points** for the beats: `beats_delivered / 5 × 12`, **halved** for any beat delivered
  only as a reactive answer after the price rather than as part of the block
- **8 points** for objection handling: the flat-rate save, the three-calls-a-year ROI, the $150
  credit bridge, holding price under a discount request, the "I'll just do the one month" reply

**Ceiling rule:** a call where fewer than 5 beats landed **before the price** cannot score above
**85 overall**, however good everything else was. This is the rule that keeps the block honest.

### The guarantee — tracked separately, every call

**"Catch nothing and the $150 is all you pay."** Log hit or miss on **every** sales call, and
carry the running lifetime tally in the report. Answering a guarantee question with anything
else is **−8 accuracy** as a promise we don't make.

---

## Capture — 15

Three points each, and they must be asked **one at a time, waiting for each answer**:

**Name** · **Cell** · **Email** · **Street address** · **"How did you find us?"**

- **−3** address not read back in full and confirmed
- **−2** phone not read back
- **Cap at 8** if the call ends with no email and no address on a live lead — we cannot quote
  them, so the call did not happen

Existing customers: verifying the file is on the right property counts as the address point.

---

## Tone — 15

Warmth, patience, no defensiveness. **−3** interrupting a customer mid-sentence. **−3** telling a
customer they are wrong when they are right. **−5** naming an objection they hadn't raised
(*"it's not a ripoff"*). This has been 14–15 on every call since 2026-08-04 — if it drops, that
is a real signal, so say so.

## Non-sales calls — letter grade, no number

Existing-customer service, complaints, out-of-area, wrong-number.

| Grade | Means |
|---|---|
| **A** | Answered from the file, correct policy, clear next step with a real timeframe |
| **B** | Right outcome, clumsy route to it |
| **C** | Customer had to do the work, or an answer sitting in the file went unread |
| **D** | Wrong information, or a real problem in the call went unnoticed |

**What to check on these:**

- Did he search by **phone number** first? (It works; address search does not.)
- Did he **read the file before asking the customer** something it could answer?
- Did he give a **specific** callback window, not "I'll get back to you"?
- Did he catch the operational red flag? (*"I don't see any traps"* on a program property.)
- Complaint: apologise once, don't explain, log the note. Never admit a service failure he
  hasn't verified against the record.

---

## Jobber cross-check — every call where he promised something

For each quote he said he'd send, confirm in Jobber:

- **Sent at all**, and **inside the window he promised**
- **Correct plan** — the line item, not the total
- **Correct deposit** — Quick Fix carries **$150**; TMCP carries **$0**
- **Discounts as line items**, per `2026-08-05_muhammad-jobber-training.md` — a bare
  quote-level discount leaves the customer looking at an unexplained number and does not
  attribute in reporting
- **Correct property address**

A promised quote that never went out is worth more than any scoring line in this file. Lead with it.
