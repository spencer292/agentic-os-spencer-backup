# Muhammad's conversion rate — what the 38% actually means

**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Source:** Jobber GraphQL — all 368 quotes created since 2026-06-01, with salesperson attribution, send times, client-hub view timestamps, linked communication counts, and client records.

---

## Bottom line

The 38% vs 73% comparison is not a like-for-like measurement, and it is not evidence of a
performance gap. Corrected for the three things that break it, Muhammad is at **47%
today, projecting to ~66% when his quotes mature**, against Spencer's baseline of 73%.
That residual gap is **0.6 standard deviations** on n=19 — statistically indistinguishable
from zero.

**The rapport theory is not supported.** When a customer actually opens Muhammad's quote,
he converts **9 of 11 = 82%**. Spencer converts **207 of 254 = 81%**. Identical. Whatever
is happening, it is not that people decline to buy from him once they have the offer
in front of them.

The real gap is mechanical and coachable: **he sends the quote once and stops.**

## 1. Why the report reads 38%

The Jobber Salesperson Performance Report shows `converted / total quotes`. Three defects:

| Issue | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Two quotes are training tests he wrote to himself** — #13921 ($450) and #13922 ($1,200), client "Muhammad Javed", zip 03743, both archived, both created 2026-08-06 before he went live | Inflates his denominator by 2 |
| **#13961 (Travis Bruce, $450) is `approved`, not `converted`** — a win the report scores as a loss | Costs him 1 win |
| **He sends two quotes to the same customer** (Quick Fix + TMCP) — Charles Hemphill, Caitlin Mccormick, and the 08-10 pair | Mechanically halves his rate on those customers |

Corrected: **9 / 19 = 47%**, and at opportunity level (one customer = one opportunity) **53%**.

> **Note on the double-quote habit:** Spencer's assumption that sending two quotes *inflates*
> his own number is backwards. It **deflates** it. Spencer is 74% at quote level and **83% at
> opportunity level** — his real close rate is *higher* than the report shows, not lower.
> Muhammad has picked up the same habit, and it costs him the same way.

## 2. The measurement is comparing a finished race to a race in progress

| | Median quote age |
|---|---|
| Spencer's 73% denominator (10 weeks of quotes) | **27.4 days** |
| Muhammad's 38% denominator (5 days of quotes) | **1.8 days** |

Spencer's own quotes do not convert instantly. Measured on his 194 fully-matured quotes
(created 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-25, all with 18+ days to land):

|---|---|---|
| 1 day | 45% | 61% |
| 2 days | 55% | 75% |
| 3 days | 61% | 83% |
| 5 days | 70% | 96% |
| 14 days | 73% | 100% |

**A 73% closer looks like a 45% closer on day one.** That is the entire shape of the alarm.

### The clean head-to-head

Same week, same quote ages, both salespeople, quotes created 2026-08-07 onward:

| | n | Converted | Rate | Projected when mature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Spencer** | 14 | 7 | **50%** | 71% |
| **Muhammad** | 19 | 9 | **47%** | 66% |

Spencer's own number for this week is 50%, not 73%.

### The decisive test

If Muhammad were performing at *exactly* Spencer's 73%, then given the precise age of each of
his 19 quotes, his report today would read **10.3 wins = 54%**.

It reads **9 wins = 47%**.

Standard deviation on that count is 2.2. The gap is **-1.3 wins, or -0.61 standard deviations.**
One week cannot detect a difference this small. It would take roughly 8–10 weeks of his volume
to distinguish a 66% closer from a 73% closer with confidence.

## 3. The one real signal: his quotes don't get opened

This is not an age artifact — it holds at every age band.

| Quote age | Spencer opened | Muhammad opened |
|---|---|---|
| under 1 day | **7 / 7 (100%)** | **3 / 7 (43%)** |
| 1–2 days | 4 / 5 (80%) | 4 / 5 (80%) |
| 4+ days | 243 / 274 (89%) | 4 / 7 (57%) |
| **Overall** | **89%** | **58%** |

**It is not a data-capture failure.** All 19 of his quotes went to a client with a valid email
and phone on file — 0% missing, against Spencer's 1%. Median send lag is 0 minutes for both;
he gets quotes out fast and to the right address.

### The cause: follow-up

Linked communications per quote (initial send plus every subsequent message):

| | Avg touches | Quotes with only 1 touch | Quotes with 2+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spencer** (last 60 quotes) | **3.32** | 2 / 60 = **3%** | 97% |
| **Muhammad** (all 19) | **1.68** | 6 / 19 = **32%** | 68% |

And critically — Spencer follows up on the ones he is *losing*, too:

| | Avg touches |
|---|---|
| Spencer — quotes he won | 3.36 |
| **Spencer — quotes open or lost** | **3.20** |
| Muhammad — quotes he won | 2.00 |
| **Muhammad — quotes open or lost** | **1.40** |

Inside Muhammad's own book the correlation is absolute: **every single quote he won had 2+
touches. Six of his ten unwon quotes had exactly one.** He sends it, and that is the end of it.

Spencer chases a quote three times whether it is going well or not. That is the difference,
and it has nothing to do with rapport, accent, or veteran status.

## 5. The leak that never reaches this report

The salesperson report can only measure calls that *became* quotes. Muhammad's larger loss is
calls that never became a quote at all — and those are invisible here.

From the call grading (`call-grading/2026-08-10.md`, `2026-08-11.md`):

- **2026-08-10, call 6:** 9m29s with the most engaged buyer of the day. Ended with no email,
  no address, no quote.
- **2026-08-11, Kirkland:** 7m26s, ~20 mounds, failed DIY, a competitor actively pitching her.
  He offered the quote once, she talked past it, he never asked again. No name, no email,
  nothing in Jobber.
- **2026-08-11, 98444 Parkland:** refused as out of area. It is in area — Luke, Tuesdays.

That is three genuinely winnable leads in five days that never entered the denominator.
His true lead-to-sale rate is therefore *worse* than 47% — but the failure is **capture, not
closing**, which is the same conclusion the quote data reaches from the other direction.

## 6. What to actually do

**Stop tracking weekly conversion rate for him.** It cannot carry the weight being put on it.
Use a 4-week rolling window at opportunity level, and read it at day 14, not day 1.

**Three fixes, in order of value:**

1. **Follow-up cadence — the single highest-value change.** Every quote gets a text at +24h and
   a call at +72h if unopened. Spencer does this instinctively 3.3 times per quote; Muhammad
   does it 1.7. This alone likely closes most of the residual gap. It is a checklist, not a skill.
2. **"Nobody hangs up without an email"** — already the standing drill; three live days running
   it has failed on the longest, most engaged call of the day.
3. **One quote per customer, not two.** If he is unsure which plan, recommend one and mention
   the other in the message body. Two quotes halve the measured rate and give the customer a
   decision they did not ask for.

**On the veteran/rapport hypothesis:** there is no evidence for it in this data, and one strong
piece of evidence against it — his 82% close rate on opened quotes. Spencer's advantage over a
brand-new hire is real, but it lives in the *mechanics* of working a quote to completion, not
in the first thirty seconds of the call.

## Method / caveats

- Won = `converted` or `approved`. "Warm" = client existed on the books >1 day before the quote.
- Maturity curve built from Spencer's 194 quotes created 2026-06-01..2026-07-25 using
  `transitionedAt` minus `createdAt`.
- Touch counts come from `Quote.linkedCommunications.totalCount`. Jobber's API does not expose
  message bodies or channel (`MessageInterfaceEdge` has no `node` field), so email-vs-text
  delivery still cannot be verified programmatically — the count is a proxy for follow-up
  volume, not its content. Won quotes generate some confirmation traffic endogenously, which is
  why the open/lost comparison (3.20 vs 1.40) is the load-bearing one.
- n=19 is small. Everything here should be re-run at 4 weeks.
