# Clock-in and clock-out policy — field technicians

Effective 2026-09-22. Applies to every hourly technician. Cory is salaried and does not clock.

## The rule

1. **Clock in when you arrive at your first stop.** Not when you leave the house. The drive to the
   first stop is your commute.
2. **Clock out when you leave your last stop.** Not when you get home. The drive home is your
   commute.
3. **Take your breaks in Gusto.** A 30-minute unpaid meal break when you work over 5 hours, and a
   paid 10-minute rest break every 4 hours, are yours by Washington law. Log the meal break in the
   app so your hours are right.
4. **Forgot a punch? Fix it the same day and say the time.** A note that says "forgot to clock
   out" without a time cannot be checked. Write "forgot to clock out, left last stop 3:48 PM".
5. **The Jobber stamp is the record.** When a punch and the Jobber completion time disagree, the
   sheet is corrected to the Jobber time. That protects you when you are underpaid and protects
   the company when you are over.
6. **Anything after the last stop that is work gets a note.** Shop time, a meeting, a supply run,
   a customer call that ran long: write what it was in the note so the time stays paid.

## What gets checked every Monday

Every timesheet row is compared to your Jobber completion times before hours are approved. The
checks are:

- Clock-out more than 45 minutes after your last completed visit, with no note.
- Clock-in more than 20 minutes after your first completed visit (you were working before the
  clock started, and we owe you).
- Clock-in more than 45 minutes before your first completed visit.
- A correction note whose time does not match the sheet.
- A day with completed visits and zero hours (off the clock, we owe you).
- A day with hours and no visits.
- Any day at 11 hours or more.
- Weekend punches.

A flag is a question, not an accusation. Most are a missed button. You will be asked the same day
and the sheet corrected to what the stamps show.

## Why

In the five weeks to Sep 18 the checks found one forgotten clock-out that paid almost three hours
for no work, and three cases where a technician was underpaid because a correction was never
applied or was set before the last job. Both directions matter. The rule above is what makes the
sheet match the work.
