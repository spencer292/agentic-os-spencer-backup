# Cascade HR — exit plan

**Do not cancel first.** Reclaim what they hold, then cancel. Once the retainer ends, a firm has
little reason to spend time assembling files for a former client, and some of what they hold is
legally required to exist.

Verified 2026-08-26: Cascade HR (Puyallup, WA, cascade-hr.com) is an **HR consulting and advisory
firm** — not a PEO, not an employer of record, not a payroll provider. Their published services are
audits, compliance and risk, policy and contract management, performance management, talent
acquisition, training, organizational development.

**What that means practically:** there is no co-employment to unwind, no L&I or ESD account to
re-register in your own name, no benefits master policy to replace, and no payroll migration. Every
one of those is what makes leaving a PEO a six-month project. None of them apply here. This is a
notice letter and a document handover.

---

## Step 1 — Read the agreement before anything else

Pull the signed service agreement and find:

- [ ] **Notice period** — 30 days is typical, 60 and 90 exist. This sets the whole timeline
- [ ] **Auto-renewal date** — if there is one, the cancellation must land before it or you buy
      another full term
- [ ] **Termination-for-convenience clause** — whether you can leave mid-term at all, and at what cost
- [ ] **Who owns the work product** — the handbook, job descriptions and policies they wrote.
      Usually the client owns them; confirm it in writing rather than assuming
- [ ] **Data return obligations** — what they must hand back, in what format, and by when

Pull the last 12 months of invoices too. The annual total is the number this whole project is
measured against.

## Step 2 — Ask what they actually delivered

Send one email requesting a written summary of work performed in the last 6–12 months. Two purposes:
it is the honest test of whether the retainer earns its fee, and the answer tends to be short.

Then check that answer against the findings in `open-findings.md`. Specifically:

- **Did they flag the 2026 exempt salary threshold change** ($80,168.40, effective 2026-01-01) for
  the salaried Operator seat? (Finding F1.) This is core compliance advisory work, it moved this
  year, and missing it is expensive.
- **Did they produce an Accident Prevention Program?** (Finding F2.) Required of every WA employer
  at any size.
- **Did they flag that any non-compete on file is void**, and that all WA non-competes end
  2027-06-30? (Finding F5.)

Whatever the answers, they belong in the file. If all three were missed, the value question answers
itself. If they were all raised and acted on, that is worth knowing too — it changes what "in-house"
has to cover.

## Step 3 — Reclaim everything, in writing

Request delivery in an editable format, not just PDFs — the handbook has to be maintained.

- [ ] Employee handbook, current version, editable
- [ ] All policies and standalone procedures
- [ ] **Accident Prevention Program** and any safety documentation
- [ ] Job descriptions they wrote
- [ ] All personnel files they hold, per employee
- [ ] **I-9 forms** — confirm who holds the originals. These have their own retention rule and must
      be stored separately from personnel files
- [ ] Any disciplinary records, warnings or investigation notes
- [ ] Any compliance calendar, audit or assessment they produced
- [ ] Any employee training records
- [ ] Confirmation of what they will **delete** after the engagement ends, and by when

Route everything to the right tier on arrival: policy and documentation into
`projects/briefs/hr-in-house/`, anything with SSNs, medical content or I-9s straight into
`hr-private/`. See `.claude/skills/ops-hr/references/data-handling.md`.

## Step 4 — Close the gaps before the notice period ends

Do not cancel with F2 open. Whatever they hand back, these must exist and be yours:

- [ ] Handbook — reclaimed or rebuilt, WA-compliant, acknowledged by every employee
- [ ] Accident Prevention Program — exists, current, delivered to the crew
- [ ] Personnel file per employee (F6)
- [ ] Compliance calendar live with owners (`compliance-calendar.md` — done)
- [ ] Finding F1 resolved or with an attorney

## Step 5 — Line up the replacement for judgement work

The retainer is buying two different things: routine work, and judgement on the rare hard call.
In-house covers the routine. For the hard calls, identify **one WA employment attorney** and have an
intake conversation before you need them — the escalation list in `SKILL.md` is what they are for.
Hourly access to a lawyer for a handful of situations a year costs a fraction of a monthly retainer
and is better advice when it matters.

## Step 6 — Cancel

Written notice, per the agreement, with the effective date stated explicitly. Keep a copy in this
folder. Confirm the final invoice and that no auto-renewal has triggered.
