# Module 4 — How to Walk a Property

This is the biggest module in the program. It is the skill the entire job rests on — every
trap you set is only as good as the walk that chose the spot. Two techs can set the same four
traps on the same property and one catches moles and one doesn't, and the difference happened
before either of them touched a trap.

The walk is a **fixed, repeatable pattern.** It is not "wander around and look for mounds."

---

## 1. What you carry — the three trips

The walk has a specific place in the visit, and you make three trips to the truck. Do them in
this order:

| Trip | You carry | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nothing** | Walk up to the door empty-handed. Confirm the house number, ring or knock, have the conversation. (Module 3.) |
| 2 | **Probe rod + shovel** | Back to the truck, grab these two, then walk the property. **Pre-dig your holes as you go.** |
| 3 | **Traps + equipment** | Back to the truck once you know what the property needs. Grab exactly that, go set them, and head out. |

**Why not just carry everything up front?** Because you don't know what the property needs
until you've walked it. Walking up with an armload of traps tells the customer you already
made up your mind about their yard, and you'll carry the wrong gear half the time anyway.

**The whole point of the walk is that you commit before you carry.** You see the entire
property, you pick your trap locations, and you pre-dig every hole — all before you touch a
single trap. So when you go back to the truck on trip 3, you already know **exactly** how much
equipment to bring out. You are not guessing, and you are not making a fourth trip because you
came up two traps short.

> ### The tool rule
> **Everything you carried out goes back in the truck before you pick up the traps.**
>
> Trip 3 starts with the probe rod and shovel going *in*, not with the traps coming *out*. Do
> it in that order every time and you will stop leaving tools in people's yards. Do it the
> other way and eventually you drive off without a shovel.

**Why the probe and shovel come on the walk.** You are not just finding spots — you are opening
them. Pre-digging while you're already standing on the run means trip 3 is set-and-go, not a
second assessment. It also means you find out a "perfect" spot is actually a dead run *while
you still have the whole property in front of you* to pick another one.

## 2. The first thirty seconds — stand and look at the house

Before you take a step, you stop and read the property. A new tech looks at a yard and sees
grass. You are looking for two things, and they are the only two things that matter.

### A. Where is the water?

Moles eat earthworms — 55 to 93 percent of their diet, and a coast mole eats 100 to 150 worms
a day. **Find the worms and you've found the moles.** Worms are where the soil stays moist.
So you're reading:

- **What gets watered year-round?** Irrigation zones, sprinkler heads, the lawn somebody
  actually maintains versus the back corner nobody touches.
- **Where does water run?** Grade, downspouts, the slope of the yard, the path water takes
  when it rains.
- **Where does water hang out?** The low spots. Where it sits, pools, and stays soft after
  everybody else's yard has dried out.

This matters most from June through October. When the soil dries, the worms go deeper and the
moles follow them — and the only reliably wet ground left is the irrigated ground. In dry
season, the watered zones aren't just a good bet, they're often the whole game.

### B. What are the objects?

A mole tunneling along runs into something solid, and it doesn't dig through it — it turns and
**runs along it.** That's how a deep run gets made, and deep runs are your primary trap target.
So every hard edge in that yard is a probable mole highway. Look for:

- **Fences** — property-line fences, garden fences, anything with a footing
- **Retaining walls**
- **Curbs**, walkways, driveways, patio edges
- **The house footing itself**
- **Landscaping** — bed borders, edging, rock walls, tree rings

Then put the two together. **You are looking for where the main runs are going to be** — and
they are going to be along an object, near the water. That's the read. Everything you do on
the two loops is confirming or correcting the map you just made standing still.

## 3. The two loops

> **This pattern is the standard for every property an acre and under** — which is most of what
> you will run. Properties over an acre are a different animal; see §6.

### Why 10 feet

Because at 10 feet you can see everything.

On a property under an acre, walking a circuit 10 feet in from the property line and a second
circuit 10 feet off the house footing puts your eyes over every square foot of that yard. The
two bands overlap in the middle. **You shouldn't miss anything.**

That is the whole reason for the number. It isn't 10 feet because 10 is tidy — it's 10 feet
because that is the offset where nothing on a normal lot ends up behind you or outside your
sightline. Walk it tight against the fence and you're looking at fence. Walk it down the middle
and you never see the edges, which is where the runs are.

```
                    ┌─── property line ──────────────────┐
                    │                                    │
                    │   ┌── OUTER LOOP ──────────────┐   │
                    │   │  (~10 ft in, full circuit) │   │
                    │   │   ┌── INNER LOOP ──────┐   │   │
                    │   │   │  (~10 ft off the   │   │   │
                    │   │   │   house footing)   │   │   │
                    │   │   │   ┌──────────┐     │   │   │
                    │   │   │   │  HOUSE   │     │   │   │
                    │   │   │   └──────────┘     │   │   │
                    │   │   └────────────────────┘   │   │
                    │   └────────────────────────────┘   │
                    │                                    │
                    └────────────[ TRUCK ]───────────────┘
                       start here ──┘  └── finish here
```

### Loop 1 — the outer loop

Walk the perimeter of the property, roughly **10 feet in** from the property line, and go
**all the way around — a full circuit, ending back at the truck.**

Not to the far side and back. Not three sides because the fourth looks empty. All the way
around, every time. The side of the property you'd have skipped is the side with the neighbor's
irrigation running under the fence.

### Loop 2 — the inner loop

Then walk a second circuit, **clockwise, roughly 10 feet off the footing of the house.**

This is your highest-value ground. The footing is the single longest hard edge on most
properties, and the drip line, downspouts, and foundation plantings keep the soil there wetter
than open lawn. Foundation edge runs are the highest-probability trap location we have.

### As you walk both loops

- **Probe where the map says a run should be.** When the rod drops into a void, you've found a
  tunnel. Probe multiple spots to trace its direction and depth before you commit.
- **Pre-dig the hole** once you've confirmed a run you want.
- **Fresh or old?** Fresh mounds are loose and damp and match the color of the soil underneath.
  Old mounds are dried, crusted, grass growing through — that's where moles *were*.
- **Never set in a mound.** Mounds are dump sites, not highways. Stay 18 inches off.
- Straight lines between mound clusters are deep runs. Activity on both sides of a walkway
  means a tunnel underneath it. Aimless meandering ridges are exploratory — lowest priority.

Full trap-location priority ranking is in Module 5.

## 4. The customer walks with you

**The customer does not have to walk with you — but you want them to, as often as you can get
them to, on a first visit.**

Ask. Every first visit. "Come walk it with me and I'll show you what I'm seeing."

This is not a courtesy. It is the single best trust-builder we have, and it is where TMCP gets
sold — not by pitching, but by a homeowner watching a professional read their yard and explain
what's happening in it. A customer who watched you find a run under their fence line understands
what they're paying for. A customer who watched you walk around alone for twenty minutes saw a
guy walking around their yard.

If they can't or won't come, walk it alone and then bring them out to show them what you found
before you leave. What you don't do is find everything and tell them nothing.

Conversation content and how to explain it is Module 6.

## 5. How long it takes

**The whole first visit is 30 minutes.** Park, approach, conversation, both loops, pre-dig,
gear trip, set the traps, document, gone.

That number is the point of the fixed pattern. A tech who wanders a yard looking for mounds
burns 30 minutes on the walk alone. A tech who reads the water and the objects from a standing
start, then runs two loops with a purpose, walks the same property in a fraction of it — and
picks better locations, because he was looking for something specific instead of hoping
something caught his eye.

If you are consistently over 30 minutes on sub-acre residential, the problem is almost always
that you are searching instead of confirming.

## 6. Properties over an acre

**Over an acre is a different animal.** The 10-foot perimeter stops doing its job — a circuit
10 feet in from the property line on five acres is a very long walk that never brings you
within sight of the middle.

## 7. Repeat visits get the exact same walk

**A repeat visit is not "go check the traps."**

Every repeat visit gets the full property evaluation — same two loops, same sequence, same
read. You are there to find out what the moles have done since you left, and **new activity
shows up in places your existing traps aren't.** Checking your existing sets happens inside
that same sequence, not instead of it.

This is a real change from how the Field Guide reads today, and it is the standard.

## Field checklist — The Walk

- ☐ Door approach empty-handed (Module 3), then back to the truck for **probe rod + shovel**
- ☐ Stopped and read the house before walking: **where is the water** — irrigated year-round,
  where it runs, where it hangs out
- ☐ Read the objects: fences, retaining walls, curbs, footing, landscaping edges
- ☐ Asked the customer to walk it with me (first visit)
- ☐ **Outer loop** — ~10 ft in from the property line, full circuit back to the truck
- ☐ **Inner loop** — clockwise, ~10 ft off the house footing
- ☐ Probed to confirm runs before committing; traced direction and depth
- ☐ Fresh vs. old called on every mound and ridge
- ☐ No trap spot chosen within 18" of a mound
- ☐ **All trap locations committed and all holes pre-dug before carrying any equipment**
- ☐ **Probe rod and shovel back IN the truck before the traps come out**
- ☐ One trip back to the truck for exactly the traps this property needs — no fourth trip
- ☐ Customer shown what I found before I left
- ☐ Whole first visit inside 30 minutes
- ☐ **Repeat visit:** full walk done, not just a trap check

## Known dependencies

- **Module 3 (Park & Approach):** the empty-handed door approach and the three-trip gear
  sequence are one continuous flow — the two modules must stay in sync.
- **Module 5 (Trapping):** the Rule of Four picks from what this walk finds. Priority ranking
  in Field Guide §2.5 folds in there.
- **Module 6 (Talking):** when the customer walks along, the conversation and the walk are the
  same event.
- **Module 8 (Jobber):** the activity code assigned on this walk sets the follow-up interval.
  Current rule (Spencer, 2026-08-05 — supersedes the 2026-07-26 graded ladder):
  - **Total Mole Control Program:** ANY activity (`L/A`, `M/A`, `H/A`) → next visit ~7 days.
    The activity *level* is irrelevant — only activity vs. none. `N/A` with no catch → monthly.
  - **A catch of ≥1 mole → weekly, regardless of the code written** (2026-08-01 rule).
  - **Quick Fix:** always weekly — it is a 5-week weekly series by construction. If the series
    runs out and there is still activity, that is a sales decision (add a visit, or sell TMCP /
    another month) — the tech flags it, nobody auto-adds.
  - Product comes from the job's **line item**, not the job type — every job is "Recurring."
