# Module 3 — How to Park & Approach

## Why this matters

The customer forms an opinion of Got Moles before you say a word. They hear a truck, they
look out the window, and they decide what kind of company just showed up. Parking is the
first thing you do on every property and it is the easiest thing to get wrong in a way the
customer remembers.

There is no single rule that covers every driveway in Pierce, King, and Thurston County.
What follows is a decision order. Work down it, and apply common sense at the bottom.

---

## 1. Where the truck goes — residential

**Default: park on the street. Do not block the driveway.**

That is option A, and it is the answer on most homes. You are a guest. Their driveway is
where their life happens — cars leave for work, kids get picked up, deliveries come.

**Park in the driveway only when the street won't work.** That includes:

- No shoulder to park on
- A road narrow enough that a parked truck becomes a hazard or blocks traffic
- Anywhere parking on the street is prohibited or clearly unsafe

**If you do park in the driveway:**

- Never park in front of a garage door. Somebody is getting boxed in.
- Never park behind another vehicle. Same reason.
- Pull to the side, leave the path clear, and make sure any car in that driveway can still
  get out without you moving.

**Never park on the grass.** Not the lawn, not the parking strip, not "just the edge." A
truck on wet turf leaves ruts, and we are the company that is supposed to be fixing their
lawn, not adding to the damage.

**Back in or nose in is your call.** It makes no difference to us.

**Use your judgment.** These are guidelines for a real world with steep drives, gravel,
fresh pavement, and cul-de-sacs. The test is simple: *could the homeowner leave right now
without knocking on their own front door to find me?* If the answer is no, park somewhere
else.

---

## 2. Where the truck goes — commercial and complexes

- **Commercial jobs:** park in the commercial lot.
- **Apartment and condo complexes:** park in the visitor spaces the complex allows.
  Not fire lanes, not resident-assigned stalls, not on the curb.

---

## 3. Locking the truck

You do not need to lock the truck at every stop. On a residential driveway or a quiet
street in front of the customer's house, leave it.

**Lock it any time you leave it in a public place.** Commercial lots, apartment complexes,
street parking in a busy area, anywhere people who aren't the customer are walking past it.

---

## 4. The approach — first visit

Follow this in order. Do not skip step 1.

1. **Walk up empty-handed.** No traps, no tools, no probe. Just you.
2. **Confirm the house number.** Look for the number on the house itself and check it
   against the job. Every property, every visit. Working the wrong yard is a bad day for
   everybody.
3. **Ring the doorbell** if there is one — including a Ring or other camera doorbell.
   If there is no doorbell, **knock**.
4. **Start gathering information.** Once they answer, this is where the visit really begins:
   where are they seeing activity, how long has it been going on, what bothers them most.
   (Full conversation in Module 6. The walk itself is Module 4.)

### If they answer through the camera

Have the conversation through the camera. A customer talking to you through a Ring doorbell
is a customer who is home and engaged — treat it exactly like a doorstep conversation. Do
not cut it short because you can't see their face.

### Why empty-handed

You are there to find out what is going on before you decide what to carry. Walking up with
an armload of traps tells the customer you have already made up your mind about their
property, and it means you carry the wrong gear half the time anyway.

**Empty-handed applies to the door, not the whole visit.** There are three trips to the truck:

1. **Nothing** — up to the door, confirm the number, knock, have the conversation.
2. **Probe rod + shovel** — back to the truck, then walk the property and pre-dig your holes.
3. **Traps + equipment** — back to the truck once you know what this property needs, set, leave.

The walk itself is Module 4.

---

## 5. The approach — repeat visits

**On a repeat visit, do not knock. Go straight to work.**

These are established accounts. They are paying us to handle it without being managed. The
customer does not want a doorbell every week and you do not have time for it.

**But "straight to work" does not mean "straight to the traps."** Every repeat visit gets a
**full property evaluation** — the same walk, in the same sequence, as a first visit. You
are not there only to check what you set last time. You are there to find out what the moles
have done since you left. New activity shows up in places your existing traps aren't.

Full walk sequence is in Module 4.

---

## 6. Nobody home

1. **Try calling the phone number attached to the job** in Jobber. Do not just start working.
2. If you reach them, confirm access and proceed as normal.
3. If there is no answer and you can get in, complete the service.
4. **Once the traps are set, send a text** letting them know you were out, you got everything
   going, and everything was fine.

That text is not optional. A customer who comes home to flags in the yard and no message
from anybody is a customer who calls the office confused. Get ahead of it.

> **Note:** We do not have door hangers yet. Until we do, the text *is* the notification.
> Do not skip it.

### Locked gate

1. Try calling the customer.
2. **No answer — reschedule.** Do not climb the gate, do not go looking for another way in,
   do not work only the front yard and call it a visit.

Note it in Jobber and move to your next stop.

---

## 7. Dogs

**A loose dog in the backyard is a judgment call, and it is your call.**

- **Dog appears friendly:** go ahead and continue with the service.
- **Dog seems aggressive at all:** leave and reschedule.

There is no in-between and there is no toughing it out. "At all" means at all. Nobody at Got
Moles has ever been in trouble for backing out of a yard because of a dog. Reschedule it,
note it in Jobber, and move on to the next stop.

---

## Field checklist — Park & Approach

- ☐ Parked on the street unless the street genuinely won't work
- ☐ Not blocking the driveway, a garage door, or another vehicle
- ☐ Never on the grass
- ☐ Commercial: in the lot. Complex: in visitor parking.
- ☐ Truck locked if it's sitting in a public place
- ☐ **First visit:** walked up empty-handed, confirmed the house number, rang or knocked
- ☐ Talked through the camera if that's how they answered
- ☐ **Repeat visit:** no knock — straight to the full property evaluation
- ☐ No answer → called the number on the job
- ☐ Locked gate + no answer → reschedule, note it, move on
- ☐ Dog check — friendly, proceed; aggressive at all, leave and reschedule
- ☐ No answer + service completed → text sent confirming it's done
