A deep cleaning checklist covers everything a standard recurring clean intentionally skips: inside the oven and fridge, baseboards, window tracks, vent covers, and behind furniture that doesn't move on a normal visit. It's a periodic job (quarterly, seasonal, or "first clean" for a new client), not a one-time exit clean like a move-out. Below is a printable room-by-room template, how it differs from both a standard clean and a move-out clean, and how to price it so it doesn't quietly eat your margin.

## What's the difference between a deep clean and a standard clean?

A standard clean maintains a home that's already reasonably clean; a deep clean resets one that's accumulated buildup a weekly or biweekly visit never reaches. Standard cleans hit surfaces, floors, bathrooms, and kitchens on a repeating cadence. A deep clean adds the things that get skipped because they don't get visibly dirty fast: inside appliances, baseboards, light fixtures, ceiling fans, vent covers, and window tracks.

Most cleaning businesses run a deep clean in one of three situations: the first visit for a brand-new recurring client (so the maintenance cleans start from a clean baseline), a seasonal reset (spring/fall), or a one-off request from an existing client who wants more than the usual scope. It is not the same job as a move-out clean, which is tied to a lease or sale and has its own [checklist and disclaimer needs](/guides/move-out-cleaning-checklist-template/).

## What should a deep cleaning checklist include, room by room?

Everything on the standard-clean list, plus the buildup items specific to each room. A checklist that only lists "kitchen, bathrooms, floors" doesn't tell your crew or your client what makes this visit different from a regular one.

**Kitchen**
- Inside oven and stovetop grates/burner caps
- Inside microwave
- Inside refrigerator (if included in scope)
- Range hood and filter degreased
- Cabinet fronts and handles wiped, inside cabinets on request
- Backsplash and grout
- Sink, faucet, and disposal area

**Bathrooms**
- Grout and tile scrubbed, not just wiped
- Showerhead and faucet descaled
- Exhaust fan cover dusted
- Baseboards and behind the toilet
- Mirrors, light fixtures, and vanity drawers wiped out

**Bedrooms and living areas**
- Baseboards throughout
- Ceiling fans and light fixtures
- Window sills and interior window glass
- Behind and under furniture that can be safely moved
- Switch plates and outlet covers

**Whole-home / systems**
- Vent covers and returns dusted or wiped
- Doors, frames, and handles
- Floors edge-to-edge, including corners a quick mop skips
- Trash bins wiped out, not just emptied

## How do you price a deep clean without underselling it?

By treating it as its own line item priced above your standard rate, not as a "bigger version" of a normal visit billed at the same hourly rate. A deep clean takes noticeably longer per square foot because of the buildup items, and pricing it like a standard clean is the fastest way to make the job unprofitable.

| Home size | Standard clean (typical) | Deep clean (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| Studio / 1 bed | $100–$150 | $180–$250 |
| 2 bed / 1-2 bath | $150–$220 | $250–$350 |
| 3 bed / 2 bath | $200–$280 | $340–$460 |
| 4+ bed / 2+ bath | $280–$400+ | $460–$650+ |

These are US suburban starting ranges and vary by market and condition. A home that's gone a year or more without a deep clean, or has pets, tends to push toward the top of its range; add-ons like inside-cabinet cleaning or interior windows are worth quoting separately rather than folded in.

## Should a deep clean be the first visit for every new recurring client?

For most cleaning businesses, yes — and it pays to make that a stated policy rather than leaving it to negotiation. Starting a recurring relationship on a home that hasn't had the buildup items addressed means your crew is either quietly doing deep-clean work at standard-clean pricing, or the home never actually reaches the baseline your regular visits are meant to maintain. Framing the first visit as a required deep clean, priced and quoted separately, sets the expectation up front and protects the profitability of every recurring visit after it.

## FAQ

**How often should a deep clean happen for a recurring client?**
Most cleaning businesses recommend seasonal (quarterly) or twice a year, depending on the home and household. Build it into your recurring cadence instead of leaving it as an ad hoc upsell nobody remembers to offer.

**Is a deep clean the same as a move-out clean?**
No. A deep clean is periodic maintenance on an occupied home; a [move-out clean](/guides/move-out-cleaning-checklist-template/) is a one-time exit clean tied to a lease or sale, and it usually needs its own disclaimer about landlord inspection standards.

**Should the deep-clean checklist go on my price list, or get quoted separately?**
List it as its own line on your [price list](/guides/cleaning-service-price-list-template/) with a stated range, but still walk the home before finalizing the number — buildup varies too much between homes to quote blind.

**Can I skip some deep-clean items to save time on a budget quote?**
You can scope it down, but do it explicitly on the [quote](/guides/cleaning-quote-template/) — list what's excluded (inside fridge, inside cabinets) so the client isn't surprised later, rather than quietly cutting corners on a job billed as a full deep clean.

**Do I need a different checklist for a first-time deep clean vs. a seasonal one?**
The room-by-room scope is the same. The difference is framing: explain a first-time deep clean as a one-time reset before recurring service starts, while a seasonal deep clean is just a scheduled part of an existing relationship.
