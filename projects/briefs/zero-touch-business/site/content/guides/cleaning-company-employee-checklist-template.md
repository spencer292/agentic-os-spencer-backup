A cleaning company employee checklist template needs four parts: a new-hire onboarding list (paperwork, background check, training), a daily job checklist so every employee cleans to the same standard, a supply/equipment checkout list, and an offboarding list for when someone leaves. Skipping any one of the four is where most first-employee hires go sideways in year one.

## What should be in a cleaning company employee checklist?

Four separate checklists, not one long list. Onboarding, daily job routine, equipment checkout, and offboarding each solve a different problem, and mixing them into a single document is how items get missed. A new hire doesn't need the offboarding steps on day one, and a five-year employee doesn't need the I-9 reminder every shift.

The daily job checklist is the one that actually protects service quality once you're not the one cleaning every house. It's the difference between "my cleaner did a good job" and "my company does a consistent job," which is what turns a solo operation into something sellable later.

## What goes on a new-hire onboarding checklist?

Onboarding covers everything that has to happen before an employee is alone in a client's home. Skipping steps here is the highest-risk shortcut in the whole hiring process, since it's the part with legal and liability exposure attached.

| Onboarding step | Typical timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Job offer + pay rate confirmed in writing | Before start date | Verbal offers create disputes later |
| I-9 and W-4 (or 1099 paperwork, if contractor) | Day 1 | Federal requirement, not optional |
| Background check | Before first solo visit | $20–$60 per check depending on provider and depth |
| Bonding/insurance addition | Before first solo visit | Confirm with your insurance agent; coverage varies by policy and state |
| Non-disclosure / non-solicitation agreement, if used | Day 1 | Verify locally; enforceability varies by state |
| Ride-along or shadow shift(s) | First 1-3 shifts | Number of shifts should scale with experience level |
| Chemical safety and PPE walkthrough | Day 1 | Cover dilution ratios, glove/eye protection, SDS location |
| Uniform, badge, or ID (if used) | Before first solo visit | Optional but common once past 2-3 employees |
| Company chemical/equipment kit issued | Before first solo visit | See supply checkout list below |
| Client-facing conduct expectations (arrival window, phone use, pets) | Day 1 | Set the tone before the first client interaction |

Background checks and bonding are the two steps new owners are most likely to skip under time pressure, and they're also the two with the most downside if something goes wrong in a client's home. Both are worth treating as non-negotiable, not "when we get to it."

## What's on a daily job checklist for cleaning employees?

This is the checklist that runs every time an employee walks into a job, and it's what keeps quality consistent across a team instead of depending on who's working that day.

- Arrive within the scheduled window; text client or office if running more than 10-15 minutes late
- Confirm scope against the job note (standard clean, deep clean, move-out, add-ons)
- Walk the space before starting to flag anything unusual (damage, pets loose, unexpected mess level)
- Follow room order top-to-bottom, back-to-front (ceiling fans and high dusting before floors, every time)
- Use color-coded cloths correctly (kitchen/bath separation, at minimum)
- Photograph any pre-existing damage before starting, timestamped
- Complete a final walkthrough against the job checklist before leaving
- Log completion, any add-ons performed, and any issues in the job system or paper log
- Lock up, reset alarm codes, and return keys/lockbox codes per client instructions

The room-order and photo-documentation steps are the two that prevent the most disputes. A "was that scratch already there" argument is a lot shorter with a timestamped photo from before the job started.

## What goes on an equipment and supply checkout checklist?

Once there's more than one employee, supplies and equipment need a checkout system or they walk off in the wrong vehicle or go missing entirely. This doesn't need to be complicated, a simple sign-out sheet or shared spreadsheet covers most solo-to-small-team operations.

- Vacuum (assigned or shared, note which)
- Mop system and bucket
- Caddy stocked to the standard supply list
- Chemical bottles labeled and within their use-by window
- Microfiber cloths, color-coded set
- PPE (gloves, knee pads)
- Company vehicle or mileage log, if applicable
- Client keys or lockbox codes, logged in and out

## What should an offboarding checklist include?

Offboarding matters just as much as onboarding, particularly for a business built on recurring access to clients' homes. The goal is closing every door that was opened during employment.

- Collect all keys, fobs, and lockbox codes; change any codes the employee knew
- Retrieve company equipment, uniforms, and vehicle if assigned
- Remove access to scheduling software, client list, and any shared accounts
- Confirm final paycheck timing and any owed reimbursements per state law (varies by state, verify locally)
- Document the reason for departure and whether they're eligible for rehire
- Notify clients only if the employee had standing recurring visits with them directly

The client-notification step gets skipped most often, and it's the one that causes the most confusion. A recurring client who's used to seeing the same face shows up better informed with a short heads-up than with a surprise new cleaner and no explanation.

## FAQ

**Do I need a written checklist if I only have one or two employees?**
Yes, sooner than most owners expect. Memory covers the first hire fine; it stops covering the second one, and a written checklist is what lets you delegate hiring itself later without training every new person from scratch yourself.

**How much does a background check cost for a cleaning employee?**
Realistic 2026 US range: $20–$60 per check depending on provider and depth (basic criminal history vs. a more thorough multi-state search). Treat it as a fixed cost of hiring, not an optional add-on, given employees work unsupervised in clients' homes.

**Should the daily job checklist be different for standard cleans vs. deep cleans?**
The core sequence (arrival, room order, photo documentation, final walkthrough) stays the same. What changes is scope, deep cleans add baseboards, inside appliances, and detail work, so the cleanest approach is one base checklist with an add-on section per job type rather than separate documents.

**What's the biggest onboarding mistake new cleaning company owners make?**
Skipping or delaying the background check to get a body on the schedule faster. It's the step most tempting to shortcut under time pressure and the one with the most exposure if skipped, since employees are alone in clients' homes almost immediately.

**Can I use the same checklist template for W-2 employees and 1099 contractors?**
The daily job and equipment checklists, yes. The onboarding and offboarding paperwork differs (I-9/W-4 vs. contractor agreement), and misclassifying workers has real tax and legal consequences — verify locally or with an accountant before deciding which structure fits.
