A move-out cleaning checklist needs to go deeper than a standard recurring clean: inside every appliance, inside cabinets and drawers, baseboards, light fixtures, and window tracks, room by room, with nothing marked "as needed." The reason is simple: this clean is usually tied to a deposit return or a lease handoff, and a client or landlord will look in places a weekly clean never touches. Below is a full room-by-room template, how to price it against a standard clean, and where to draw the line on what's actually included.

## How is a move-out clean different from a standard clean?

A move-out clean is exhaustive and one-time; a standard clean is maintenance and recurring. Standard cleans skip things that don't get dirty fast (inside the oven, inside cabinets, baseboards) because a client living in the home doesn't need those touched every visit. A move-out clean can't skip them, because the next person judging the space (a landlord doing a walkthrough, a new buyer, an incoming tenant) is looking specifically at the spots a routine clean leaves alone.

That's also why move-out cleans take longer and cost more per square foot than a standard clean of the same size home. Quoting one off your normal rate card is the most common way cleaning businesses under-price these jobs.

## What should a move-out cleaning checklist include, room by room?

Every room, plus the systems that cut across rooms (baseboards, switches, vents). A checklist that's just "kitchen, bathrooms, bedrooms" leaves too much interpretation for what "clean" means at move-out.

**Kitchen**
- Inside and outside of all cabinets and drawers
- Inside oven, stovetop, and range hood/filter
- Inside refrigerator (if left behind) and behind/underneath if accessible
- Inside microwave
- Sink, faucet, and disposal
- Countertops and backsplash
- Interior and exterior of dishwasher

**Bathrooms**
- Inside medicine cabinets and vanity drawers
- Tub, shower, and grout
- Toilet, including behind and around the base
- Mirrors and light fixtures
- Exhaust fan cover

**Bedrooms and living areas**
- Inside closets, including shelving
- Baseboards and window sills
- Light fixtures and ceiling fans
- Interior window glass and tracks
- Switch plates and outlet covers

**Whole-home / systems**
- All floors vacuumed and mopped by surface type
- Doors, door frames, and handles wiped down
- Vent covers and returns dusted
- Trash removed from every room, bins left empty
- Garage or storage areas swept (if in scope)

## How do you price a move-out clean against a checklist like this?

By home size and condition, with a clear surcharge over your standard-clean rate, not by trying to itemize each checklist line separately. Clients don't want a 40-line invoice; they want one number that reflects the deeper scope.

| Home size | Standard clean (typical) | Move-out clean (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| Studio / 1 bed | $100–$150 | $150–$220 |
| 2 bed / 1-2 bath | $150–$220 | $220–$320 |
| 3 bed / 2 bath | $200–$280 | $300–$420 |
| 4+ bed / 2+ bath | $280–$400+ | $420–$600+ |

Figures vary by market, so treat these as a US suburban starting range, not a fixed rate. Two things move the number up from there: a home left in poor condition (heavy grease, pet hair, neglected bathrooms) and add-ons like inside-window washing or carpet shampooing, which are worth quoting separately since they're genuinely optional add-ons rather than part of the base scope above.

## Should the checklist go to the client, or stay internal for your crew?

Both, but trimmed differently. Your crew needs the full room-by-room list as a working document, something to physically check off during the job. The client version can be shorter: a plain-language summary of what's included, mainly so they know what they're paying for and don't assume things not on the list (inside-window washing, wall touch-ups, carpet stains) are automatically covered.

If the clean is tied to a security deposit, it's worth telling the client directly that a cleaning checklist is not the same as a landlord's move-out inspection standard. Those are set by the lease and by state or local law, and they can require more (or less) than a general cleaning checklist covers. That's not something your business can guarantee against; it's worth saying so plainly rather than implying the clean guarantees deposit return.

- [ ] Confirmed home size and current condition (not assumed from a standard-clean history)
- [ ] Priced off the move-out range, not the standard-clean rate
- [ ] Add-ons (inside windows, carpets, garage) quoted as separate line items

- [ ] Deposit/inspection disclaimer given if the clean is tied to a lease move-out
- [ ] Crew given the full room-by-room working checklist, not a summary

## FAQ

**Do I need a different checklist for tenants moving out vs. homeowners selling?**
The room-by-room scope is the same either way. What changes is the framing: a tenant move-out is usually deposit-driven, so the disclaimer about inspection standards matters more. A seller's move-out clean is about presentation, so staging-adjacent extras (streak-free windows, baseboards) tend to matter more to that client.

**Should I guarantee the clean will get a deposit back?**
No. Deposit return depends on the lease terms and local landlord-tenant rules, which vary by state and county, so verify locally rather than promising an outcome your cleaning can't control.

**What if the home is in worse condition than expected?**
Build a walkthrough into your quoting process before finalizing the price, and note on the [quote](/guides/cleaning-quote-template/) that the price assumes normal wear, with a re-quote clause for anything found on-site that changes scope materially.

**Is a move-out clean a good add-on to advertise, or a separate service line?**
Most cleaning businesses list it as a distinct service on their [price list](/guides/cleaning-service-price-list-template/) rather than folding it into "deep clean," since the room-by-room scope and pricing are different enough to confuse clients if blended together.

**How do I bill for a move-out clean? Same as any other job?**
Yes, standard [invoice](/guides/cleaning-business-invoice-template/) practice applies: send it the same day, itemize any on-site add-ons separately, and state payment terms clearly since these are usually one-off jobs with no recurring relationship to fall back on.
