**File 12 — Policies & Letters** | v1.0 | 2026-07-26

## How to use this

Four tools in one file, and each one exists because a real problem will eventually walk through your door:

- **(a) Weather & Rescheduling Policy** — lawn care runs on weather and on how fast grass grows. Put this in your welcome packet from day one so a rained-out Tuesday is "our policy," not an argument.
- **(b) Cancellation, Skip & No-Access Policy** — the "can you skip this week?" question and the locked-gate problem will both cost you money until you've answered them in writing.
- **(c) Price-Increase Letter** — you'll need this sooner than you think. Raise recurring prices annually and modestly instead of rarely and painfully.
- **(d) Review-Request & Complaint Scripts** — copy, personalize, send within hours.

Replace every `{PLACEHOLDER}`. These are business templates, not legal documents — fee limits and notice requirements vary by state/county, so verify locally before enforcing fees.

# (a) Weather & Rescheduling Policy

*Insert into your service agreement and welcome packet.*

---

## {BUSINESS NAME} — Weather & Scheduling Policy

Lawn care is outdoor work on a living surface. Two things drive our schedule: the weather, and how fast your grass is actually growing.

**Your service day.** You're scheduled for **{DAY OF WEEK}**, and we work the same route every week so your visit stays predictable. If rain moves the route, we generally run **one to two days behind** for the rest of that week rather than skipping anyone.

**When we don't mow.** We'll reschedule for:
- **Steady or heavy rain** — mowing saturated turf tears the grass, ruts the soil, and leaves clumps that smother what's underneath.
- **Standing water or saturated ground** — the damage from mowing a soaked lawn takes months to grow out.
- **Lightning or storms** — we don't run equipment in it.
- **High wind** — blowing and cleanup become pointless, and debris becomes a hazard.

We don't reschedule for light drizzle on otherwise firm ground; that's a normal working day in this trade.

**Growth-based service.** In peak growing season your lawn may need every-week service. In midsummer heat, drought, or dormancy, growth slows and a weekly cut can do more harm than good. We'll tell you honestly when your lawn doesn't need cutting — **{DESCRIBE YOUR POLICY: e.g., "we'll skip the visit and you won't be charged," or "we'll switch to every-other-week and adjust billing"}**. We'd rather protect the lawn than run a mower over it for the invoice.

**Heat and drought.** During drought or heat stress we'll raise the cutting height and reduce frequency. Taller grass shades its own roots and holds moisture — a lawn cut short in August is a lawn that browns in August.

**Leaf season.** Once leaves drop, visit times get longer and cleanup may be billed separately per your agreement. We'll tell you before that starts, not after.

**How you'll know.** If weather forces a change, we'll contact you at **{PHONE / EMAIL / TEXT}** as early as we can. Missed days are made up within **{NUMBER}** business days whenever possible.

**Your part.** Please keep the lawn clear of toys, hoses, furniture, and pet waste on your service day, and leave gates unlocked. It keeps us fast and keeps your property safe.

Questions? {PHONE / EMAIL}.

# (b) Cancellation, Skip & No-Access Policy

---

## {BUSINESS NAME} — Cancellation & Access Policy

**Recurring service.** Recurring lawn maintenance continues through the season unless you tell us otherwise. We ask for **{NUMBER — commonly 7–14}** days' notice to end recurring service.

**Skipping a visit.** If you'd like to skip a scheduled visit, let us know at least **{NUMBER — commonly 24–48}** hours ahead. Please understand what skipping actually does: the next cut removes far more material, takes longer, and can stress the lawn — so a skipped week may be billed at **{FULL RATE / A HIGHER RATE OF $X}** when the growth requires it. We'll tell you in advance if we expect that.

**Repeated skipping.** If service is skipped **{NUMBER — commonly 2–3}** times in a season, we may need to move you off the recurring route. Routes are built around consistency; we hold your day for you every week.

**No access.** If we arrive and can't service the lawn — locked gate, dog in the yard, blocked access, vehicles or items on the turf — we'll contact you and, where possible, return. A trip where we can't work may be billed at **{$X / a no-access fee of $X}**, because the truck, the crew, and the route slot were all committed to you.

**Dogs.** We love them and we can't work around them. Please secure pets before your service window, and please clear waste from the lawn — we can't mow a yard we can't safely walk, and neither can our equipment.

**Weather rescheduling is never charged.** See our Weather & Scheduling Policy.

**Non-payment.** Invoices are due **{TERMS}**. Service may pause on accounts past **{NUMBER}** days, and we'll always contact you before that happens.

# (d) Review-Request & Complaint Scripts

## Review request — text (send the day of service, while they can see it)

> Hi {FIRST NAME}, {YOUR NAME} with {BUSINESS NAME} — we finished up today and the yard's looking great. If you're happy with it, would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? It genuinely helps a small local business more than anything else we do. Here's the link: {REVIEW LINK} — thank you!

## Review request — after a big job (cleanup, aeration, first cut)

> Hi {FIRST NAME}, hope the yard's looking sharp after {THE SPRING CLEANUP / THE AERATION / today's visit}. If you've got 30 seconds, a quick Google review would mean a lot — it's how people around {TOWN} find us. {REVIEW LINK}. Thanks again for having us out.

*Ask when the result is most visible: the first cut of spring, right after a cleanup, or the week the lawn greens up after fertilization.*

## Complaint response — first contact (within the hour, by phone if possible)

> Hi {FIRST NAME}, this is {YOUR NAME} from {BUSINESS NAME}. I got your message about {ISSUE} — thank you for telling me directly, I'd rather know. I'm sorry it wasn't right. Here's what I'd like to do: {SPECIFIC FIX — come back tomorrow and re-cut that section / repair the damage / credit the visit}. Does {TIME} work for you?

**The rules:** respond fast, don't get defensive, name the fix, give a time. Most lawn complaints are about a missed edge, clippings left on a walk, or a wheel rut — all cheap to fix and all extremely expensive to argue about.

## Complaint response — property damage

> Hi {FIRST NAME}, {YOUR NAME} with {BUSINESS NAME}. I want to make this right. I'm going to {COME LOOK AT IT TODAY / SEND SOMEONE OUT} so I can see exactly what happened, and then I'll tell you straight whether it was us and how we'll fix it. If it was us, we'll take care of it — that's what the insurance is for and that's how I run things.

*Then actually go look. Sprinkler heads and invisible fence lines are the two most common damage calls in this trade — see File 13 for how to prevent both from becoming your problem in the first place.*

## The "you missed a spot" reply

> Thanks for pointing that out, {FIRST NAME} — I'll get it on {DAY}. If you ever spot something, text me a photo and I'll take care of it on the next visit at no charge. I'd always rather hear it from you than have you quietly put up with it.

*This single sentence — "text me a photo" — converts more silent cancellations into long-term clients than any other line in this file.*
