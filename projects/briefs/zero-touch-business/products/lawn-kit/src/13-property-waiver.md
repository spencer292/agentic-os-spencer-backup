## How to use this

Two things will damage a client relationship faster than a bad cut: **a severed irrigation head** and **a cut invisible-fence wire**. Both are invisible from the seat of a mower, both are the homeowner's responsibility to mark, and neither conversation goes well if the first time you raise it is *after* it happened.

This document exists so that conversation happens on day one instead.

The core idea is simple. Your equipment operates at ground level across property you didn't install and can't see under. Sprinkler heads, pet-fence wire, landscape lighting cable, drainage covers, septic lids, shallow cable and utility lines, decorative edging, tree roots, and buried anything — the client knows where those are. You don't. So you ask, you write it down, you both sign, and you mark what matters.

Use it in three parts:

- **(a) Property Condition Acknowledgment** — the walkthrough, completed with the client before the first service.
- **(b) Service Waiver & Damage Policy** — signed once, covers the risks inherent to power equipment on turf.
- **(c) Client-Directed Service Acknowledgment** — for when a client insists on something you've advised against.

Replace every `{PLACEHOLDER}`. **These are business templates, not legal documents.** Waiver enforceability varies by state — some states limit what a waiver can release, and none let you waive your own negligence. Have a local attorney review before you rely on it, and carry general liability insurance regardless. A waiver is a communication tool first and a legal shield a distant second.

**Photograph the property before your first cut.** Wide shots of every zone, close-ups of anything already damaged. Ten minutes, once, and it settles nearly every dispute you'd otherwise be losing.

**2. Irrigation heads.** Even properly marked heads can sit above grade, shift, or be installed improperly. {BUSINESS NAME} will use reasonable care, and damage to a marked head caused by our negligence will be repaired at our cost. Damage to unmarked, improperly installed, or above-grade heads is not our responsibility.

**3. Thrown objects.** Mowers and trimmers can propel rocks, sticks, wire, pet toys, and other debris at speed. The client agrees to keep the lawn clear of debris and to accept that {BUSINESS NAME} cannot guarantee against damage from objects concealed in the turf. **4. Trimmer damage to trees and structures.** String trimmers can damage bark, fencing, siding, and edging. To prevent it, we recommend mulch rings around trees. Where a tree is surrounded by turf and no ring exists, incidental contact is a known risk and the client accepts it — or accepts that we may leave a small collar of grass uncut around the trunk instead.

**5. Turf conditions and results.** Lawn appearance depends on soil, sun, water, mowing, weather, and the previous condition of the turf. {BUSINESS NAME} controls the mowing and maintenance; it does not control weather, watering, soil, disease, insects, or existing turf health, and does not guarantee a specific appearance.

**6. Wet conditions.** Mowing saturated turf can rut soil and damage grass. {BUSINESS NAME} may reschedule for weather. If the client requests service on saturated ground, section (c) applies.

**7. Existing conditions.** {BUSINESS NAME} is not responsible for conditions documented on the Property Condition Acknowledgment or otherwise existing before service began.

**8. Pets and pet waste.** The client agrees to secure pets and remove waste before each visit. {BUSINESS NAME} may skip a visit where pets are loose or waste makes safe service impossible, and may bill for the trip per our Access Policy.

**9. What this does not release.** Nothing here releases {BUSINESS NAME} from responsibility for damage caused by its own negligence or willful misconduct. {BUSINESS NAME} carries general liability insurance; a certificate is available on request.

**10. Notice of concerns.** Please report any concern within **{NUMBER — commonly 3–7}** days of service so we can inspect it while the cause is still determinable.

## Field notes — how to actually use these

- **Hand the client the flags.** Don't ask them to "point out" the sprinkler heads — give them a bundle of marking flags and ask them to walk the yard before your first visit. Costs you a few dollars and it's the single highest-return thing in this file.
- **The one-third rule is your best defense.** Never remove more than one-third of the blade height in a single cut. It's the standard, it protects the turf, and it's what you point to when a client asks you to scalp it.
- **Mulch rings end the trimmer argument permanently.** Recommend them at the walkthrough, on every property with trees in turf. Offer to install them as a paid add-on — it solves your risk and adds revenue at the same time.
- **When you do cause damage, say so first.** Call before they notice. "I clipped a sprinkler head on the north side today — I've already got the part, I'll have it fixed Thursday." Clients forgive damage. They don't forgive discovering it themselves.
- **Keep signed forms and walkthrough photos organized by address**, for at least the length of your state's statute of limitations on property claims — verify locally.
