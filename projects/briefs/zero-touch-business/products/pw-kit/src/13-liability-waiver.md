# Pressure Washing Business Starter Kit
**File 13 — Liability Waiver & Property Condition Acknowledgment** | v1.0 | 2026-07-26

---

## How to use this

This is the most important piece of paper in your kit, and it's the one most new washers skip.

Pressure washing is the rare trade where doing the job correctly can still reveal damage that was already there. You soft-wash a north-facing wall and the mildew comes off along with paint that had already failed underneath. You clean a 1970s aluminum-sided house and the chalky oxidation lifts, leaving a blotchy finish that was invisible under the dirt. You wash a deck and boards that were rotting under the grime finally show it. In every one of those cases the homeowner sees a "before" they remember as fine and an "after" they blame on you.

The fix is not to argue better. It's to **walk the property with the client before you pull a trigger**, write down what you both saw, and have them sign it. Ten minutes up front kills nearly every dispute you'd otherwise be having at the end.

Use this document in three parts:

- **(a) Property Condition Acknowledgment** — the pre-job walkthrough. Fill it in *with* the client, on site, before setup.
- **(b) Service Waiver & Release** — signed once, covers the known risks inherent to the work.
- **(c) High-Risk Surface Addendum** — only when the client wants something done that you'd advise against.

Replace every `{PLACEHOLDER}`. **These are business templates, not legal documents.** Waiver enforceability varies significantly by state — some states limit what a waiver can release, and none of them let you waive your own negligence. Have a local attorney review this before you rely on it, and carry general liability insurance regardless. A waiver is a communication tool first and a legal shield a distant second.

**Photograph everything you note here.** A signed form plus timestamped photos is close to unbeatable. A signed form alone is an argument.

## Field notes — how to actually use these

- **Do the walkthrough with your phone out.** Narrate as you photograph: "here's the chalking on the south wall, here's the cracked pane." A 90-second video with the client's voice in it settles disputes that a form alone won't.
- **Say the hard thing before you start, not after.** "That paint is already failing — cleaning will take some of it off. I can soft-wash it to be gentle, but I want you to expect that." Clients respect this. It is also the entire reason section (c) exists.
- **Never sign a waiver in place of turning down a job.** If the roof is too far gone or the deck is rotted through, the professional answer is "I won't do that, here's why" — a signed form doesn't make an unsafe job safe.
- **Keep every signed form and photo set for at least the length of your state's statute of limitations** on property claims — verify locally, but multiple years is the norm. Cloud storage, organized by address.
