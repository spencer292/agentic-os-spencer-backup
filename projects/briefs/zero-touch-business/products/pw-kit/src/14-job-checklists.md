# Pressure Washing Business Starter Kit
**File 14 — Pressure Washing Job Checklists** | v1.0 | 2026-07-26

---

## How to use this

Print these, laminate them, keep a set in the truck. A checklist isn't an insult to a good tech — it's how you deliver the same result whether it's you, your best guy, or someone on day three. Every callback you ever get will trace back to a line someone skipped.

Two rules run through all of it. **Water goes top to bottom** — always work down a surface so dirty runoff never crosses clean work. And **the cleaning solution does the work, not the pressure** — dwell time is the difference between a professional soft wash and a damaged wall. If you find yourself reaching for more pressure, you almost always need more dwell instead.

Every job runs the same three phases: **pre-job walkthrough**, **the work**, **final walkthrough with the client**. Skip the walkthroughs and you'll spend the money you saved on callbacks.

---

## CREW STANDARDS — Every Job, Every Time

1. **Arrival:** Arrive inside the window. Text the client on arrival and again at completion.
2. **Walkthrough first:** Complete the Property Condition Acknowledgment (File 13) with the client. Photograph everything noted. **If you didn't photograph it, you own it.**
3. **Windows and doors:** Confirm closed and secured before any water runs. Check the garage side door — it's the one everybody forgets.
4. **Protect first:** Cover or pre-wet plantings, cover exterior outlets and fixtures, move or note anything in the work zone.
5. **Test spot:** On any unfamiliar surface, test in an inconspicuous area and check the result before committing to the whole elevation.
6. **Right method, right surface:** Soft wash where the material demands it. Pressure is a last resort on anything painted, aged, or wood.
7. **Runoff:** Know where your water is going before you start. Containment and disposal rules vary by municipality — **verify locally**, especially for commercial flatwork.
8. **Final walkthrough:** Walk the finished job *with the client*. Ask directly: "Anything you want me to look at again?"
9. **Photos:** After photos on every job, every time. This is your marketing library and your dispute insurance in one.
10. **Leave it better:** Coil hoses, pick up trash (yours and theirs), rinse overspray off walks and cars, close gates. The last impression is the one they describe to a neighbor.

## PRE-JOB WALKTHROUGH — Every Job

- [ ] Confirm scope with the client out loud — what IS and ISN'T included today
- [ ] Complete Property Condition Acknowledgment; note and photograph pre-existing damage
- [ ] Identify water source, test flow and pressure, confirm you may use it
- [ ] Locate electrical panels, exterior outlets, low-voltage lighting, and any exposed wiring
- [ ] Confirm all windows, doors, and openings are closed and secured
- [ ] Confirm vehicles moved, furniture/grills/décor cleared or noted
- [ ] Note pets, children, and where they'll be during service
- [ ] Identify sensitive plantings and the runoff path
- [ ] Confirm parking and equipment staging that won't block the client or neighbors
- [ ] Set expectations out loud on anything that may not fully come clean

# CHECKLIST 1 — HOUSE WASH (SOFT WASH)

*The bread-and-butter job. Low pressure, correct solution, proper dwell. Pressure damages siding, forces water behind it, and strips paint — soft washing is the professional method for a house exterior.*

## Setup

- [ ] Confirm siding material and approximate age (vinyl, aluminum, wood, stucco, fiber cement, brick)
- [ ] Test spot in an inconspicuous location; confirm result before proceeding
- [ ] Mix solution to the strength appropriate for the surface and level of growth
- [ ] Pre-wet all landscaping thoroughly along the full work path
- [ ] Cover exterior outlets, light fixtures, and any exposed connections
- [ ] Confirm downspouts and grade will carry runoff away from the foundation

## The wash

- [ ] Apply solution **bottom to top** — prevents streaking on the dry surface below
- [ ] Allow full dwell time; keep the surface wet, do not let solution dry on it
- [ ] Re-apply to heavy growth areas rather than increasing pressure
- [ ] Agitate stubborn areas by hand (soft brush) where needed
- [ ] Rinse **top to bottom** with low pressure, working in overlapping passes
- [ ] Rinse under eaves, soffits, and gutter faces
- [ ] Clean around and behind downspouts and utility penetrations
- [ ] Wash exterior window frames and sills; rinse glass so it dries clean
- [ ] Address door frames, porch ceilings, columns, and railings
- [ ] Rinse all landscaping again thoroughly at completion — this is not optional
- [ ] Rinse walks, drives, and any hardscape that caught overspray or runoff

## Check before you call it done

- [ ] Walk every elevation and look up — missed spots hide under the eave line
- [ ] Check for streaking at downspouts and corners
- [ ] Confirm no solution dried on glass or trim
- [ ] Check neighboring property for overspray; rinse if needed
- [ ] After photos of each elevation

# CHECKLIST 2 — CONCRETE & FLATWORK (DRIVEWAYS, WALKS, PATIOS)

*Where a surface cleaner earns its price. The mistake that defines an amateur is visible wand stripes — inconsistent overlap that leaves zebra lines a client sees for years.*

- [ ] Sweep or blow the surface clear of debris before any water
- [ ] Note and photograph existing cracks, spalling, oil stains, and prior discoloration
- [ ] Identify and pre-treat oil, rust, and organic staining with the appropriate product
- [ ] Confirm where runoff goes; contain or divert as required locally
- [ ] Pre-wet adjacent plantings and cover anything sensitive
- [ ] Apply cleaning solution and allow dwell — do not let it dry
- [ ] Clean with a **surface cleaner** at consistent speed and overlap; do not free-wand open areas
- [ ] Wand only the edges, corners, and transitions the surface cleaner can't reach
- [ ] Keep passes consistent — inconsistent speed shows as stripes once dry
- [ ] Rinse the full area, working with the slope
- [ ] Rinse residue off adjacent walks, curbs, and the street apron
- [ ] Post-treat remaining organic staining if needed
- [ ] Rinse landscaping again
- [ ] After photos taken **after the surface has dried** — concrete looks uniform when wet and tells the truth when dry

# CHECKLIST 3 — ROOF (SOFT WASH ONLY)

*Never, under any circumstance, put high pressure on a roof. It destroys shingles, voids manufacturer warranties, and turns a cleaning job into a roof replacement claim. If you aren't equipped and trained for roof work — including fall protection — decline the job.*

- [ ] Assess roof age, material, and condition from the ground; photograph
- [ ] Decline if shingles are lifted, brittle, or visibly failing — document why
- [ ] Confirm fall protection and safe access; never work a wet roof without it
- [ ] Pre-wet ALL landscaping and the full runoff path generously
- [ ] Cover or divert downspouts as needed to control where solution lands
- [ ] Apply approved low-pressure solution evenly; allow dwell
- [ ] Do not scrub, brush, or blast — the growth releases and weathers off
- [ ] Re-apply to heavy areas rather than mechanically agitating
- [ ] Rinse or leave per the method used and the product's directions
- [ ] Rinse all landscaping thoroughly, twice
- [ ] Rinse siding, windows, walks, and drive of any runoff
- [ ] Clear gutters of released debris if included in scope
- [ ] After photos from the same angles as the before photos

# CHECKLIST 4 — DECKS & FENCES (WOOD / COMPOSITE)

*Wood is the easiest surface to permanently damage and the easiest to be blamed for. Low pressure, wide fan, consistent distance — always with the grain.*

- [ ] Identify material: pressure-treated, cedar, redwood, hardwood, or composite
- [ ] Probe for soft or rotted boards; photograph and show the client **before** starting
- [ ] Note loose boards, popped nails, protruding screws, and railing wobble
- [ ] Test spot in a hidden area; confirm before proceeding
- [ ] Clear furniture, grills, planters, and rugs
- [ ] Pre-wet surrounding plantings
- [ ] Apply appropriate wood cleaner; allow dwell
- [ ] Clean at low pressure with a wide fan tip, consistent distance, **always with the grain**
- [ ] Never stop or dwell in one place — that's how you etch the wood
- [ ] Clean railings, spindles, stairs, and fascia
- [ ] Apply brightener/neutralizer if the process calls for it
- [ ] Rinse thoroughly; rinse landscaping
- [ ] Advise the client on dry time before furniture returns, and on sealing timeline if applicable
- [ ] After photos

---

# CHECKLIST 5 — GUTTER FACE BRIGHTENING

*Sold as an add-on and worth real money because "tiger striping" is invisible to the homeowner until you show them.*

- [ ] Photograph the striping and show the client the before/after expectation
- [ ] Confirm scope: exterior face brightening ≠ gutter cleaning (interior debris removal)
- [ ] Apply gutter cleaning solution to the exterior face; allow dwell
- [ ] Agitate by hand with a soft brush — this job is hand work, not pressure
- [ ] Rinse top down, working the full run
- [ ] Verify no streaking remains at the seams and end caps
- [ ] Rinse siding and windows below
- [ ] After photos from the same angle — this is one of the best before/after sells you have

# FINAL WALKTHROUGH — Every Job

- [ ] All equipment, hoses, and materials removed from the property
- [ ] Overspray rinsed from vehicles, walks, and neighboring surfaces
- [ ] Gates closed, pets accounted for, outlets uncovered
- [ ] Walk the job **with the client**; ask "anything you'd like me to look at again?"
- [ ] Address any concern on the spot if it's reasonable — a five-minute touch-up beats a callback
- [ ] After photos captured for every service performed
- [ ] Invoice presented or scheduled per your terms (File 10)
- [ ] Review requested while you're standing in front of the result (File 12)
- [ ] Next service discussed if the surface is on a maintenance cycle
- [ ] Completion report / photos sent before leaving the driveway
