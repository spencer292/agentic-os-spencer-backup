## 1. Why creative is the main lever (Andromeda) — APPLIES

- Meta's retrieval system (Andromeda) no longer matches on audience labels. It reads the ad itself (pixels, video frames, text overlays, captions, copy) and decides which ad a given person sees. Dan's line: "creative is the targeting." [02/01]
- It balances three things per impression: user value (likelihood this person converts), advertiser value (bid and optimization), user experience (will they stay or scroll). [02/01]
- Consequences Dan teaches: [02/01]
  1. Broad targeting wins about 98% of the time. Manual targeting is mostly a constraint. Exceptions: a brand-new pixel can get a short interest-targeting nudge until conversion events fire, and geographic or demographic limits are fine when the offer demands it.
  2. Consolidate the account: fewer campaigns and ad sets, far more creative variety inside them. Dan's high-spend accounts run roughly one prospecting campaign (optionally split by angle or awareness level) plus one retargeting campaign.
  3. About 90% of effort goes into the ad: creative, copy, headline. Be specific about who the ad is for.
- Hard rule on perceived variety: ads that share the same key visual attributes are treated as one ad (one "entity ID"). Three UGC videos of a woman in a kitchen with the product count as one creative. [02/02, 02/14]
- Dan's stat: about 95% of ad results come from the creative. [02/09]

Got Moles use: for a local service in three counties, geography is the one targeting constraint that is legitimate (service area). Everything else stays broad. The agent's effort goes into angle diversity, not audience slicing.

## 2. The Creative Matrix: how to get real variety — APPLIES

True variety means differing across four dimensions, not just script wording. [02/02]

| Dimension | Question | Examples Dan lists |
|---|---|---|
| Persona | Who delivers the message? | founder, customer, expert, male/female, older/younger |
| Format | How is it delivered? | UGC video, static, text on screen, carousel, talking head, podcast set |
| Environment | Where is it happening? | kitchen, car, office, outdoors, studio, screen share, walking |
| Benefit | Core reason to buy | saves time, saves money, status, avoid pain |

Worked example: baseline = founder / talking head / office / "saves 10 hours"; shift = customer / UGC review / car / "stops my team quitting"; contrast = no person / static / solid-color background / "manage 50 clients". Build each new test by deliberately changing several dimensions at once. [02/02]

Volume rules: [02/02]
- Typical account runs 15 to 30 genuinely distinct concepts at once; a smaller budget can start with 5. At $50/day you do not need 50 ads. Budget sets the count.
- Andromeda will starve most ads and put the money behind 2 to 3. That is normal. Keep the pool wide enough that something is always working.
- Expect most tests to fail. Winners live in the scaling campaign, tests live elsewhere.

Three-bucket pipeline ("angle grinding"), all three active at all times: [02/02, 02/14]
1. New angles: different persona/format/benefit, discovery bets.
2. Angle extensions: a winner's argument with a new hook, format, proof moment or creator.
3. Proven workhorses: current winners; do not touch or duplicate them, leave them in the scaling campaign. Use their data to feed buckets 1 and 2.
Only having bucket 3 means one fatigue event from a crash.

Execution fatigue vs message fatigue: execution fatigue = people saw the creative too often (fix with new visual, creator, opening frame, format). Message fatigue = the market stopped responding to the promise (needs a new angle). [02/14]

Audit action: map your live ads onto the four dimensions; if they all look alike (same person, same clothes, same car), plan three new concepts that each change format, environment and angle. [02/02]

## 3. Static vs video, and "test with static, translate to video" — APPLIES

- Video is high-friction: it educates, builds trust, creates new demand, suits complex or high-ticket offers, skeptical markets and cold top-of-funnel storytelling. Static is low-friction (message lands in under a second), captures existing demand, suits simple offers, clear discounts or guarantees, retargeting and rapid angle testing. [02/03]
- Dan's rule: "a basic local service or straightforward lead magnet" does not need a 60-second video; a strong image with a strong headline often converts cheaper. [02/03]
- Claim: roughly 60% of people will not watch a video, so statics matter in nearly every account. [02/06]
- Test and translate: launch about five simple statics, each a completely different angle, on a small budget. Read results (cheapest CPM, clicks, conversions). Turn the winning static's headline into a video hook and its body into the video script, then scale with video. Statics find the signal, video scales it. [02/02, 02/03]
- Format-to-job matching: do not explain a complex product in a static; do not pay for video production to announce a simple discount. [02/03]

Got Moles fit: the service is simple to understand (moles in lawn, safe removal, guarantee), so statics are a primary format. Video suits the trust job: show a technician, a lawn before/after, how the process works.

## 4. Eight static formats — APPLIES (most), ADAPT (a few)

From the Static Image Master Resource [02/06]. Meta's old 20% text rule is gone, so text can fill the image. Dan says the "ugly" native ads are currently winning almost every time [02/06, 02/13].

1. Native camouflage: raw, unedited phone photo that looks like a friend's post. No logo, no polish, messy real environments, simple text over it. Works because it bypasses the viewer's "ad blocker." APPLIES: a real photo of a torn-up lawn or a real job.
2. High-contrast callout: dark or neon background, massive heavy text, one polarizing claim, one CTA. It qualifies or disqualifies in 3 seconds. APPLIES (lead gen, bold specific promise).
3. Text-heavy informational: image as a mini landing page with checklist, numbered list, chart. For analytical buyers. ADAPT: a "how to tell if it is moles vs voles/gophers" checklist.
4. Notes-app screenshot: raw phone-notes list of tips or mistakes. Near-zero production. APPLIES (e.g., "5 mistakes homeowners make with mole mounds").
5. Twitter/X thread screenshot: borrowed authority. ECOM-ONLY / low fit for a local service.
6. Email or DM screenshot: voyeuristic "behind the scenes" look, personal advice or mini audit. ADAPT: a real customer text or review thread, with permission.
7. "Us vs. them" comparison: two columns, red X vs green check. ADAPT carefully: compare DIY/poison/ultrasonic gadgets vs a professional method, without any kill or trap wording (see Fit section).
8. Founder selfie + long copy: casual selfie on top, long personal-story copy below. APPLIES (Spencer or a technician as the face).

Homework Dan sets: take the best video message and rebuild it as three statics (native camouflage, high-contrast callout, text-heavy informational) and test with a small budget. [02/06]

## 5. Specs: sizes, safe zones, files — APPLIES

[02/10]
- Make every creative in two sizes: 4:5 (1080 x 1350) and 9:16 (1080 x 1920). Prefer 4:5 over 1:1 because it fills more of a mobile screen. [02/10, 02/15]
- Keep headline and CTA away from the top and bottom edges. Meta sometimes crops 4:5 to square. Dan suggests leaving roughly a quarter of the space top and bottom clear of anything important.
- Do not make one 9:16 and crop it into feed; it looks bad in Stories and Reels.
- Export PNG for statics. Canva's "copy and resize" converts a 4:5 design to 9:16 in one step; then adjust the layout.
- When prompting AI images, ask for negative space at the top for text overlay. [02/11]
- AI output often puts text too close to edges; tell the model to add padding, and always proof spelling. [02/15]

## 6. Video ad structure — APPLIES

Timeline of a roughly 60-second ad mapped to AIDA. [02/05]

| Phase | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (Attention) | first 3 seconds | stop the scroll AND filter in the right person; call out a pain, desire or identity, never "my name is..." |
| Bridge (Interest) | next ~7 seconds | prove it is worth listening to by empathy (you understand the problem) or authority (concrete reason to trust) |
| Body (Desire) | bulk | Problem - Agitation - Solution; introduce a named mechanism: the specific reason it works when others failed |
| CTA (Action) | final 5 to 15 seconds | one unmistakable action and what happens after the click; no options, no bait |

Modular production: film or write one body, three or more hooks (and optionally several CTAs) and recombine like Lego. At scale: 10 hook-only clips, 10 bodies, 10 CTAs. [02/05, 02/07, 02/09]

The lesson "AIDA Framework" [02/04] has no transcript. AIDA is defined in [02/05] and [03/01] (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action).

Hook-timing note for short ads: Dan states hooks are 3 seconds. He gives no separate rule for placement-specific lengths.

## 7. UGC-style production on a small budget — ADAPT

[02/07] UGC means content that looks native, "like a FaceTime call with a friend." Ring lights and actors hurt performance.

Zero-budget frameworks:
1. Founder story: owner on a phone, in the car or on a walk, handheld, no tripod. Hook = why you built it / what angered you about the industry. APPLIES (Spencer).
2. "Fake customer review": Dan has an employee or friend speak as a customer would, specific not generic ("I used to spend hours every Sunday on spreadsheets..."). ADAPT / DO NOT COPY LITERALLY: for Got Moles, do not have a staffer pose as a customer or invent a testimonial. Use real customers (with permission), or have a technician speak openly as a technician. Any customer-voice ad must be a real customer saying real things.
3. Screen-record walkthrough with voiceover. ECOM-ONLY / software. Low fit.
4. B-roll hack: film about five minutes of B-roll, record five voice-over hooks in a phone memo app, lay each over the B-roll in CapCut to get five ads from one shoot. Variant: no voice, big on-screen text hook over short B-roll, add licensed music from Meta's ad tool. APPLIES (technician walking a yard, mounds, tools).

Hiring creators (only needed to scale): Billo, Fiverr, Upwork or search "UGC creator + niche"; roughly $150 per video at the time of recording. Diversify faces; some buyers will not connect with one demographic. Do not let creators edit. Send a script with three hooks and one body, get raw files, edit yourself in CapCut. Use a teleprompter app so delivery is natural but scripted. [02/07] ADAPT: for a local service, the technicians and owner are the creators; outside UGC creators cannot speak to doing the job.

## 8. Hooks and headlines — APPLIES

[02/08]
- If an ad underperforms, 90% of the time the problem is nobody stayed past the hook.
- Old clickbait ("you won't believe...") now triggers defenses. Current style: direct, value-driven, calls out the avatar immediately. Golden rule: focus on the viewer, not yourself. The hook must answer "why should I care in three seconds?"
- Three hook types Dan says consistently win:
  1. Direct call-out: name the person. Template: "If you're a [who] [situation], stop scrolling."
  2. Dilemma / pain point: enter the problem already in their head. Template: "Should you [common choice]? The answer is no. Here is what to do instead."
  3. Counter-narrative: tell them a widely accepted belief is wrong; creates a curiosity gap.
- Video hook = first 3 seconds spoken or on-screen text (job: earn the next 5 to 10 seconds). Static headline = big text on the image, which must stop the scroll and deliver the core value at once because nothing follows. [02/08]
- Steal and adapt: save hooks that stop you while scrolling, strip the topic, keep the structure ("How to achieve [desire] in [time frame] without [pain]"), apply to your niche. When adapting listicles, shuffle the item order. [02/08]
- Dan's AI workflow: run the "30 Hook Generator" prompt (section 14), pick the top five, plug into one-body-five-hooks structure. "Your job is to curate from volume." [02/08]

## 9. Ad angles (the strategic layer under the hooks) — APPLIES

[02/14] An angle is the argument for why a specific buyer should care about this offer right now. A hook, new face, new thumbnail or background color is execution, not an angle.

A usable angle has three parts: real buyer tension (desire, objection, moment or identity), one clear promise or point of view, and a reason to believe (mechanism, customer story, demonstration, process, other proof you actually have). If you cannot state the argument in one sentence, do not brief the ad.

Dan's example of feature vs angle: "our software puts jobs in one dashboard" is a feature; "stop doing quotes at 7 p.m. after a full day on site" is an angle.

Five places to find angles:
1. Buyer tension (what they are tired of or avoiding).
2. Purchase moment or use case (what finally makes them search).
3. Complaints: 1 to 3 star reviews of competitors, Reddit, forums, YouTube comments, app-store reviews; for services, adjacent products or books. Look for the moment the buyer felt let down, the workaround they use, and what they wish existed. Dan's AI shortcut: paste the negative reviews or call transcripts into Claude, ask for the top three complaints, then ask for three to five angles with reasons to test them. Do the manual reading first so you understand the buyer.
4. Mechanism or point of view (real reason it works, never invented to sound clever).
5. Proof story (customer result, demonstration, founder explanation, side-by-side process).

Angle Opportunity Card (write one per idea before it reaches production): buyer evidence (actual quotes), market default (what everyone else says), the gap, angle statement ("For [buyer in tension/moment], [offer] helps them [outcome] through [real mechanism]"), proof available, recommended execution, priority. [02/14]

Five filters before an idea becomes a brief: True (can we honestly say it), Distinct (would swapping old copy/proof/visual still work? then it is an extension), Relevant (to someone who can become a good customer), Provable, Practical (can a credible version be made fast; assume 9 out of 10 tests lose). [02/14]

Caveat from Dan: owners think differently from customers. Test ideas that feel uncomfortable; some of his best angles were ones he expected to flop. [02/14]

## 10. Swipe file / evidence library — ADAPT

[02/13] Do not start from a blank screen. Keep a pattern library, not a screenshot dump. Three tabs: Capture Inbox (save fast: competitor ads, customer review lines, landing pages, comments), Evidence Library (organized), Next Briefs (one clear direction per pattern).

Evidence Library columns: source, buyer tension, angle/promise, proof, execution, "why I saved it" (one sentence; if you cannot write it, you saved decoration). Sources are not only competitors: customer reviews, sales/phone call notes, support tips, Reddit, YouTube comments, emails, ads in other categories solving the same communication problem. Meta Ad Library (free) is the competitor source; sort by longest running or highest impressions as a proxy for performing. [02/13, 03/06]

Weekly 20-minute review: tag useful items, delete junk, look for repeated customer phrases and repeated objections, move ONE (not ten) pattern into a brief. Do not copy another advertiser's wording, testimonial or footage. Break a customer sentence into tension, angle, proof, then write an original brief direction. Tool mentioned: Foreplay (paid, optional). [02/13]

Got Moles use: the richest source is the Got Moles call transcripts (CallRail), Google reviews and Jobber notes, which are first-party and need no permission to mine for language. The agent should use these before any competitor research.

## 11. Creative brief and AI production workflow — APPLIES

The master creative brief has five sections: [02/09]
1. Campaign name, avatar, funnel stage, delivery folder link.
2. Brand assets (guidelines, logos, photo/video library).
3. Video script and editing direction: tech specs, pacing, references, full modular script (hook 1, 2, 3, body, CTA).
4. Static ad design direction: dimensions, inspiration, graphic copy organized by angle and version.
5. Meta ad copy: three short captions, two long captions, five standalone headlines, ready to paste.

Rules: a vague brief gives a vague ad, whether the producer is a designer, a creator or an AI. If AI output is poor, the brief was not clear enough. Two-step AI use: first have AI write the brief (Master Creative Brief Generator prompt in Dan's library, item 16 in his private version), then upload the finished brief and ask for the creative. Do manually first at least once to learn it. Dan also has an agent tool scan competitor ads and draft a brief, then produce better creatives. [02/09]

AI image prompt formula: subject + environment + lighting style + aspect ratio, plus negative space for text. Dan's example: "generate a photorealistic vertical 4:5 image of a tired, frustrated 30-year-old man sitting on a bench in a modern gym... cinematic lighting... leave negative space at the top for text." [02/11]

Tools Dan mentions (tool names change fast; treat as examples): Midjourney (photorealism, Discord), ChatGPT image generation (easiest, follows instructions, can look "plastic"), Adobe Firefly (licensed data), Manus (end-to-end, links to ad account), Claude (his pick for copy). Canva for assembling statics. ElevenLabs for voice-over from a script. [02/11, 02/12, 02/10]

Production system ("variation matrix") [02/12]:
- Four AI-producible assets: copy variations (10 headlines, 10 primary texts, 5 CTAs from a brief in about 10 minutes), image variations, video scripts, voice-overs.
- Three levers: hook, visual, format. 3 hooks x 3 visuals x 2 formats = 18 ads from one brief in an afternoon. Dan's goal: run 20 while others run 2 or 3.
- Sample prompt: "Use the creative brief below. Generate 5 scroll-stopping hooks (first line of primary text, max 10 words each), 3 headline variations, 2 primary text body variations (max 150 words each), 3 CTA options." Then generate three different image concepts matching the hooks.
- Voice-over ad with no camera: script (30-second UGC style) + ElevenLabs voice + visual background (generated or stock). Fit for Got Moles: ADAPT (see Fit section on AI-ad policy).

Static Ad Prompt Vault (Dan's tool, community-only) [02/15]: library of 100+ static formats with prompts, filtered by business type (includes "local services" and "lead gen"). Flow: pick a format, fill your details once (who it is for, offer, CTA text, size), tick "use my real logo/photo," copy the generated prompt into ChatGPT, attach assets, download. A "5 variations" batch mode creates five different statics from one prompt. Always default to 4:5, then regenerate at 9:16. Not accessible to this agent; replicate the idea (a reusable prompt template with the fill-in fields above).

## 12. Core copywriting rules and frameworks — APPLIES

Direct response, not brand advertising: the job is a trackable action; every line must push toward the click. The first sentence only exists to get the second sentence read. The ad's job is to sell the click; the landing page's job is to sell the booking. [03/01]

AIDA in primary text: Attention (hook, calls out avatar/pain), Interest (mechanism or solution), Desire (picture the outcome), Action (exactly what to do next). Use AIDA as the editor's filter on AI copy: if desire is missing, tell the AI to add it. [03/01]

Three primary-text frameworks [03/02]:
- PAS: Problem (state the pain immediately), Agitate (twist the knife on why it is hurting), Solution (product as the specific answer + CTA). Leans on pain avoidance, which motivates more than pleasure.
- BAB: Before (current frustrating reality), After (life with the problem solved), Bridge (product as the vehicle). Sells the destination, not the vehicle.
- Story / epiphany: Hook (start mid-situation or with a belief), Epiphany (the moment the old way was exposed), Pivot/offer (how the solution was built and how to get it). Suits retargeting and founder-led brands.

AI prompt skeleton Dan gives [03/02, 03/05]: "Act as an expert direct response copywriter. Write the primary text for a Meta ad selling [offer]. The target audience is [avatar]. They are at [awareness stage]. Use the [PAS/BAB/story] framework. The tone should be direct, confident and conversational. Don't use emojis. Keep sentences short and punchy."

Four mandatory inputs for any AI copy request: avatar (age, situation, exact pain), awareness stage (unaware / problem aware / solution aware / product aware), offer (value, friction, what makes it a no-brainer), framework. Missing one = generic output. [03/05]

Five things AI gets wrong that must be stripped [03/05]: emoji overload (at most one at the end); cliche openers and "imagine a world..."; "it is not X, it is Y" constructions and fake empathy ("we know how hard it is"), replace with an objective statement of the problem; weak CTAs ("click below to learn more") replace with exactly what to do and what they get; word-salad transitions (just say "this is why we built X"). Treat the first draft as a draft: generate, read aloud, diagnose (shorten by 20%, request five alternative hooks, make the problem more specific), finalize and read aloud again. Dan's pick for copy: Claude or Manus. Save the final prompt as the baseline for that client.

## 13. The 24 copy techniques (reference list) — APPLIES

From [03/03]. The linked 24-technique document is not in the corpus; this list is reconstructed from the transcript. Ask the AI for a specific technique by name rather than "make it better."

Hooks and attention:
1. "How to [result] without [biggest objection]" headline.
2. Curiosity gap / open loop (keep it concrete enough that Meta still knows what you sell; "this single setting..." is fine, total vagueness is not).
3. Direct call-out ("Attention [avatar]...").
4. Paradox / counter-narrative.
5. Measurable speed or size ("30 leads in 48 hours").
6. Us vs. them contrast.
Desire and value:
7. Be specific (facts beat vague "best service" claims).
8. Future pacing (make them visualize life after).
9. Sensory language.
10. Condensing the claim by comparing to a known thing ("like having X in your pocket").
11. "Even if" claims (disarm the personal limitation).
12. Feature-to-benefit translation (never a feature without the payoff).
Proof and authority:
13. Proof stacking (several proof types in one sentence).
14. Reason-why explanation (mechanism behind the claim).
15. Borrowed authority (respected figure or institution).
16. Addressing the skeptic ("sounds too good to be true; here is how it works").
17. Position the deal as the result of an error or unexpected event (he warns it is the classic overused one).
18. Testimonial integration (weave quotes into body copy).
Persuasion mechanics:
19. Loss aversion framing.
20. Avoiding the "Hobson's choice" (offer options, not a take-it-or-leave-it).
21. Justify the failure (past failure was not their fault).
22. Genuine scarcity only. Fake scarcity destroys trust.
23. Instant gratification emphasis.
24. Risk-reversal guarantee.

## 14. Story-based copy — ADAPT

[03/04] Stories work for cold traffic because they earn attention without leading with a claim. Failure modes: story starts too far back; the founder becomes the hero; no proof; forgets to sell. Nobody cares that you started in a garage; they care when a moment makes them think "that is exactly what I'm dealing with." Make visuals relatable to the audience too (don't project wealth that doesn't match the buyer).

Three story types: founder (a problem story, not a biography: start at the conflict), customer transformation (before / tried already / what changed / now; use the customer's exact language and only what they actually said), moment of realization (a belief the prospect holds, then the specific observation that changed it; vague "then I realized everything was wrong" is useless, be specific).

Structure: setup (start inside a real moment) - conflict (obstacle, failed attempt, cost of leaving it) - realization (where the angle lives) - resolution (method/mechanism as the bridge, plus proof) - CTA (earned, the next logical step, not a bolted-on discount).

Story bank: a document with five columns (starting situation, frustration or failed attempt, turning point, proof or outcome, the person's exact language). Sources: customer interviews, reviews, sales calls, support tickets, emails, comments. Do not make ten nearly identical story ads that change only line one; test different stories (founder vs customer vs realization vs plain offer ad).

AI prompt structure: give offer, audience, usable facts, the five story fields and exact customer language; ask for three drafts (founder, customer transformation, realization), each starting inside the tension, keeping facts intact, "do not invent results or testimonials," ending with the CTA. For regulated categories (health, finance) be especially careful.

## 15. Copy diagnostics: value, cost, acceptability — APPLIES

[03/06, 03/07] Every line should do one of three jobs; if it does none, cut it.
1. Increase perceived value (appeal, exclusivity, obvious incentive).
2. Decrease perceived cost and friction (time, confusion, effort, price, "call us" being a high mental cost for a cold prospect; free short breakdown, "no opt-in required" lowers it).
3. Increase acceptability and trust (social proof, a named or explained mechanism, specifics, demonstration).
Conversion happens when perceived value exceeds perceived cost and the message is acceptable.

Teardown lessons: [03/06] "problem-aware" hook + named mechanism + low-friction CTA works. Hybrid headline (benefit + pain avoidance + exclusivity) works. Demonstration ("see the video") replaces claims. A generic "best services, call today, 10 years experience" ad fails; rewrite = specific dollar loss + niche specialization + free checklist instead of a call. Note: this rewrite's "$5,000 overpay" style claims must be real for Got Moles.

AI critique prompt: paste a draft, ask it to critique brutally on (1) perceived value and exclusivity, (2) lowered mental/time cost/friction, (3) credibility and trust, and give three specific improvements. [03/06]

Competitor research tip: search competitors in the Meta Ad Library, sort by longest running; ads running 30+ days are likely converting. Run them through the three-question diagnostic. [03/06]

## 16. Funnel stage copy (TOF / MOF / BOF) — ADAPT

[03/07, 03/08]
- One ad cannot do everything. Match the message to where the prospect is, not where you want them to be.

| Stage | Audience | Awareness | Goal | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TOF | cold | unaware / problem aware | earn the click, keep mental cost low | educational, empathetic, problem-first |
| MOF | warm (watched, clicked, visited) | solution / product aware | build trust, kill objections | authoritative, proof-heavy, differentiated |
| BOF | hot (started the form, abandoned) | most aware | convert | direct, urgent, offer-focused |

- TOF: lead with the pain, explain why the problem happens, introduce the mechanism as the logical answer. MOF/BOF: do not re-educate; show why you over competitors, stack proof (testimonials, UGC, case studies, demonstrations), then urgency/scarcity/guarantee at BOF. [03/07, 03/08]
- Two copy levers [03/07, 03/08]:
  - Simplification (TOF): turn a complex mechanism into a familiar metaphor. Example: "GPS for your supply chain." Clarity beats cleverness.
  - Escalation (BOF): amplify perceived value and the cost of not acting. Example: "You aren't disorganized, you just don't have a system."
- Diagnosis rule: 80% of failures are marketing (offer, landing page, creative), 20% media buying. High CTR but no conversions = offer or landing page problem. Low CTR = creative or hook failed to earn the click. [03/07]
- Don't run a winning cold ad word-for-word to retargeting, or a brand-heavy warm ad to cold. [03/08]
- AI Copy Scaling Prompt (verbatim structure): rewrite the core ad for three temperatures: TOF problem aware (agitate the pain, use Simplification), MOF product aware (differentiation and social proof, overcome [specific objection]), BOF most aware (short and punchy, use Escalation, offer and urgency). Tone direct, confident, practitioner-level, no emojis, short sentences. Paste the best cold ad copy at the bottom. [03/08]
- Action Dan sets: label every active ad TOF / MOF / BOF and check the match; audit retargeting for repeated cold messaging. [03/07, 03/08]

Got Moles ADAPT: audiences for retargeting are small (local, seasonal). The funnel is short: a call or form is the BOF event. Do not build a three-stage machine until audience sizes support it; one strong prospecting concept pool plus a simple warm follow-up is probably enough.

## 17. Copy testing and dynamic text — APPLIES

[03/09]
- The goal of a test is learning, not a lower CPA today. Let it run to a clear result; stopping early out of spend nerves yields bad data.
- Isolate one variable. Testing copy: keep creative constant, change only copy. Testing creative: keep copy constant, swap only the video/static. Same for landing page and form. Never test ad + page + form at once.
- Test angles (psychology), not synonyms. "Get 20% off today" vs "Claim 20% off now" is wasted spend. Example idea bank: direct/benefit-driven, indirect/curiosity, objection handling or risk reversal.
- Always duplicate the original into a testing campaign; never edit a live ad (it damages history).
- 80/20 duplication: when something wins, copy it, keep 80% identical, change 20% (e.g., same primary text, new headline; or same copy, new first 3 seconds of video).
- Headline-test prompt: for the product, with primary text already written, generate 10 distinct headline variations across angles (direct/benefit, indirect/curiosity, objection/risk reversal, urgency/scarcity), under 40 characters, no emojis.

Multi-text optimization (Meta's "add text option") [03/10]:
- A single ad can hold up to 5 primary texts and 5 headlines. Meta assembles combinations per user from a pool, so it is not 5 ads.
- Mix lengths: a couple of short punchy texts plus a couple of long story/bulleted ones. Dan says he tested this heavily and "long-form only" advice is wrong; short-form and long-form buyers differ.
- The 5 variants must be five different psychological angles that all match the creative: e.g., direct benefit, curiosity, objection/risk reversal, urgency, social proof. Synonym variants give Meta one signal.
- Use manual split tests when you need clean isolated proof; use multi-text when scaling a creative that already works. In a scaling account about 90% of active ads use multi-text.
- AI prompt: from the core brief, generate 5 distinct primary texts (short punchy, long story, bulleted, objection handling, social proof) and 5 headlines under 40 characters (direct benefit, curiosity, risk reversal, urgency, social proof). Tone direct, confident, no emojis.
- Upgrade procedure: duplicate a winning ad, add 4 more primary texts and 4 more headlines via the add-text options, publish, wait a few days.

Flexible / dynamic creative ads [03/11] — Dan does not use them: Meta mixes assets behind the scenes and you lose clean reporting on what worked. He runs one creative per ad (different sizes of the same creative are fine). Also check Meta has not auto-added extra ads into the ad when you create it, and remove them.

## Creative + copy playbook for Got Moles

1. Pull the evidence first (first-party). Mine CallRail transcripts, Google reviews and Jobber notes with Review Miner / Empathy Map prompts [02/16]. Output: a "Pain Language" and "Payoff Language" list in customers' own words, and the five or six objections that show up repeatedly (price, "do they come back," pets/kids, "how fast").
2. Write 6 to 10 Angle Opportunity Cards [02/14]. Each needs buyer evidence, market default, gap, angle statement, available proof, execution, priority. Run the five filters (true, distinct, relevant, provable, practical). Keep at most the best 5 to 6 for the first batch.
3. Spread angles across the four matrix dimensions [02/02]: at least two personas (owner, technician, real customer), at least two formats (static + short video), at least two environments (yard/lawn, truck/doorstep, plain background), at least three different benefits (safe for pets/kids, fast results, a guarantee, stops lawn damage).
4. Brief each concept using the five-section creative brief [02/09]: avatar, funnel stage, assets, modular script (3 hooks, 1 body, 1 CTA) for any video, static design direction, and ad copy block (3 short captions, 2 long, 5 headlines).
5. Produce statics first (test and translate) [02/02, 02/03]. For each of 5 angles, make one native-camouflage, one high-contrast callout, one text-heavy informational where it fits. Export 4:5 and 9:16, with headline and CTA clear of the top and bottom quarter [02/10]. Photos of real jobs beat AI where possible.
6. Write copy per framework [03/02, 03/05]. Use the master prompt (avatar, awareness stage, offer, framework). Run PAS for pain-first angles, BAB for outcome angles, story for the founder/technician angle. Awareness stage is usually problem aware (they see mounds and are searching). Apply Simplification at TOF.
7. Clean the AI draft [03/05]: remove emojis, cliche openers, "not X but Y" lines, fake empathy, weak CTAs; read aloud; then run the policy strip (no kill/trap wording, no unverified claims, no I-713, US spelling) and the claims gate. Run the copy through `tool-humanizer` per the repo rule.
8. Load multi-text variants [03/10]: 5 primary texts (mix of short and long) and 5 headlines under 40 characters, each a different psychological angle that fits the creative. One creative per ad; no flexible/dynamic ads [03/11].
9. Launch as tests in a separate campaign from workhorses [02/02, 03/09]. Never edit a live ad; duplicate. Change one variable per test. Hold the budget to what the account can read; with a modest budget start with about 5 distinct concepts, not 30 [02/02].
10. Read results as learning [03/09, 03/07]: diagnose with the three-question lens (value, cost, trust), and use CTR vs conversion to decide whether the problem is the creative or the offer/landing page. Report in the repo's required terms (cost per booked job, payback, mix effect), not only cost per lead.
11. Extend winners, rotate losers [02/02, 03/09]: apply 80/20 duplication on any winner; translate a winning static into a short video via the Format Translator prompt; keep a bucket of fresh angles in every cycle so fatigue never leaves only workhorses.
12. Weekly 20-minute swipe review [02/13]: save new patterns (including from outside pest control), tag, delete junk, promote one pattern into the next brief. Log every finished ad's angle and result in the evidence library so the next batch does not repeat a dead message.
