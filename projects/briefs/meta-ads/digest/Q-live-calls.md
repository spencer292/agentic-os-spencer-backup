# Course Q&A Calls: What the Live Calls Add

Source: five live Q&A calls from The Meta Ads Arena (Dan Edmund, Skool), dated 9/25, 10/8, 10/15,
10/22 and 10/29/2026, digested 2026-10-29. Raw captions: `projects/briefs/meta-ads/skool-corpus/07-q-a-call-recordings/`.

How to use this file: the calls are Dan answering individual members' accounts, mostly high-ticket
coaching, e-commerce, and home services. Dan states plainly that everything he says is "just my
experience" and tells members to test. Treat his rules here as strong priors, not laws. Where this
file and `got-moles-operating-rules.md` disagree, the operating rules win until Spencer approves an
edit (section 3 lists the proposed edits). Dates in brackets are the call date. Captions are
auto-generated with no speaker labels, so anything marked (unclear in captions) is a best reading.

## 1. Rules Dan states on the calls

### Account setup and safety
- Nothing substantive on restricted accounts, appeals, or Business Manager setup came up on any of
  the five calls. The account-safety checklist stays as written in the operating rules.
- Ads Manager shows an edit history per ad set (stopwatch icon, "view history", pick the date).
  Use it to see who changed what; a deletion may not appear there. A member's published ads
  vanished from view; Dan's call was a Meta glitch ("glitchy for a day or two"), wait a few minutes
  before rebuilding [10/8].
- Keep a manual change log for ad account and website changes made in the same period, "because you
  will forget" [9/25].
- Before running any ads, confirm the pixel actually fires on every page, not just the home page.
  Method: Meta Pixel Helper Chrome extension, click through the site and watch events fire. If the
  pixel fires only on the home page, fixing it comes before spend. Get site login access; the person
  who built the site can often install it [10/22].
- After every publish, open the live ad and check it. Meta silently auto-applies settings
  [10/29].

### Tracking and conversion events
- Optimize for the event that gives Meta enough volume. If the final event (booked appointment) fires
  only every couple of days, optimize for the step before it (form submit, application, answered
  phone call) "just to give some data back." Not permanent; move to the final event once volume
  exists [10/8, 10/15].
- The learning-phase "50 events per week" number "doesn't mean what it used to." Do not worry about
  it. About 20 leads a week does about as much as 50. Consistent leads matter more than hitting a
  threshold; Meta gets smarter week over week [10/15]. Under a couple of leads a week, Meta "is just
  guessing."
- Send Meta only qualified events. Example: a form asks income, anyone who selects below the
  threshold never fires the Lead event to Meta, so Meta learns only the qualified profile. Much
  easier to control on a landing page than an instant form [10/15, 10/29].
- Pixel reset: when an old pixel holds mostly unqualified or incomplete event history (instant-form
  leads that never became customers, or revenue never passed back), Dan's default is to create a new
  pixel and feed it only qualified events. "A good pixel is like gold." Triggers he named: switching
  from instant form to a qualified landing page; leads that do not book (2 of 31 booked) [10/8].
- Counterexample [10/15]: a member with roughly 50 deposits, about half qualified, was told to keep
  the dataset; it already has good data and "will fix itself." Reset when the history is roughly
  90% junk, not when it is half junk. Switching an event name mid-stream pollutes data but "it'll fix
  itself."
- Cost per lead low enough (around $4.61 to $7) means a new pixel retrains fast. At $300 per lead it
  would take far too long to be worth it [10/8].
- A new pixel starts with high CPMs; the old pixel's data does not transfer. Pixel and old campaign
  learning are separate things [10/8].
- Multiple pixels on one site are fine as long as no live campaign runs to the old one [10/8].
- Meta attributes by last click and is never fully accurate. For blended channels Dan prefers judging
  by MER (total revenue over total ad spend) rather than per-platform ROAS [10/15, 10/29].

### Campaign structure
- Do not run link-click / Traffic campaigns to generate business. "Facebook will do whatever you ask
  it to do; if you ask for clicks it finds clickers, not buyers." Use a conversion objective (form
  submit on site, or click-to-call). A Traffic campaign at $5 to $10 a day to your Instagram profile
  is acceptable only as a brand/follower top-up, never core spend [10/29, 10/8].
- Objective cannot be changed after publish; duplicate into a new campaign [10/29].
- Engagement campaigns are only worth it as a retargeting reminder to people who already took a
  step (e.g. registered). Cheap, no call to action, many short answer videos, each in its own ad set,
  each excluding viewers of the others so Meta rotates new videos to the person [10/29].
- Start broad geographically. Dan to a Florida roofing/window company: replace many small zip-code or
  per-city ad sets with one ad set covering the counties; small pools "never get out of learning"
  [9/25].
- Age: open the minimum to 25 [9/25].
- Keep Advantage+ audience off so ads do not go to 18-year-olds who do not own homes [9/25, 10/29].
- Placements: Facebook plus Instagram is fine [9/25].

### Budgets
- Test budget: 2x to 3x target CPA per test unit. At 3x CPA winners and losers usually show in 3 to
  4 days. At 1x CPA you "just have to understand it's going to take longer" [10/15, 10/29, 9/25].
- Never launch a new campaign at the full available budget "or you'll burn through it quickly."
  Start with a test slice even when the client has $300 to $400 a day [10/29].
- Meta treats a daily budget as a weekly budget: a $100/day budget can spend $50 one day and $150
  the next, up to $700 for the week. A day with 57% less traffic is usually just lower spend. Judge
  weekly, not daily, and expect weak weekends [9/25].
- Starving a campaign only prolongs results; it does not make Meta "waste" less [9/25].
- Dan's local sweet spot example: with a $50 target qualified lead, around $500 a day lets you test
  and scale fast and gives roughly 10 qualified leads a day; below that you move slowly. Max CAC
  (customer, not lead) was $300 to $350 in that case. "Outspending people is the number one way to
  win. Unfortunately it just is" [10/15].
- Dan on $30 to $100 a day home-service accounts: no magic fix; if the budget is close to the cost
  per lead he would not use instant forms either, and he offered a free structure audit [10/15].
- When budget is a fraction of 10x CPA, do not drop many winners into one scaling campaign and
  expect Meta to give each a fair share. Force spend per ad (he used to set dollar amounts, now a
  percentage per ad so it scales when campaign budget rises) [10/15]. How he sets the percentage is
  (unclear in captions).

### Testing and kill rules
- Kill decision threshold: roughly 3x target CPA of spend with no result [10/29, 10/15, 9/25].
  universal on the calls.
- Early-launch panic is the main error: new account, new pixel numbers "are rough," leave alone for a
  few days. Dan admits he is "ready to pause everything" at 48 hours on his own offers [9/25].
- A "good day" or bad day is noise: "no rhyme or reason; don't touch anything the next day" [10/22].
- Do not pause a winning ad to rest or to change other things. Trickle the budget down instead.
  A campaign paused more than a few days does not always pick up where it left off and may relearn
  [10/15].
- One major change per week, then let it sit. When many things change at once (ads, landing page,
  conversion event), "it tanked and I had no idea what went wrong" [10/15, 9/25].
- Sequence: first make the ads stable and scalable (you know what to expect day to day), then test the
  landing page or offer in a "phase two" [10/15, 10/8].
- Do not judge an ad set before the lead's event window passes. For appointment-based services, wait
  until the booked dates have happened. Lead gen is slower than e-commerce; in high-ticket agency
  work a lead takes 3 weeks to 2 months to become a client [10/22].
- Judge results at the ad set level when ads assist each other. Spend is nearly even across three
  near-identical ads at similar CPL ($43/$61/$55 per lead, about $150 each): killing the $61 ad may
  raise the $43 ad's cost, because one primes the click the other closes [10/22].
- Small-sample warning: when each ad has about $150 and one or two leads, one more lead swings CPL
  drastically. Four times as many leads at about the same spend (14 vs a handful) was enough to call a
  side the winner, but even then wait for the downstream outcome [10/22]. Dan referenced a video
  quantifying "spent X, got one lead, about 40% chance it becomes a winner" (unclear in captions).
- Angle test layout: one ad set per angle, about 3 creatives each, all ABO, around $20 a day each,
  4 to 5 days; with 3 creatives you separate "the angle works" from "that one creative worked."
  Creative test layout: one ad set, one creative per ad set [9/25]. With a small budget: one ad set
  per angle, one angle per asset, up to three formats [10/29].
- Test creatives against each other with drastic differences (see Creative). Three different ads at
  $50 a day total is a reasonable starting point for a local service until a baseline CPA exists; cut to
  two if nothing moves [10/29].

### Scaling
- Scaling means "predictably knowing you will get X back for every dollar" [9/25].
- You will usually lose money the first 30 days of any product; the faster testing ends, the faster you
  scale [9/25, 10/15].
- Fatigue is not a frequency number. The "3x frequency" rule is false. Watch frequency and CPA
  together: if frequency rises while CPA holds, keep running; if CPA rises as frequency climbs, refresh.
  One ad ran 16 to 17 months at frequency 17 and still beat every new ad [10/15].
- Some accounts run a scheduled creative refresh: weekly or biweekly, drop a handful of new ads
  (especially small audiences) [10/15].
- At a lead-per-day volume where budget is not the constraint, the job becomes "just creative
  refreshes every one to two weeks" [10/15].
- Lookalikes are not dead but cap scaling. With about 75 to 95 customers, a lookalike is "worth a test,
  wouldn't put a whole bunch of money behind it" [10/8].

### Creative
- Diversity is king. Meta now groups near-identical ads (same image, small text change) under one
  entity ID and treats them as one ad. A new person, setting, format, or scene counts as a different
  entity. Ads Manager gained a creative diversity column/rating in the last week or so (around
  10/22 to 10/29) that says whether you have enough [10/29].
- Test the format and concept first, hook variations later, once something wins [10/29].
- The viewer decides in about half a second. State what you sell in the first line and first image
  ("Hurricane impact windows" beats "We handle the paperwork"). Curiosity hooks used to work; use very
  direct ones now [9/25]. Ads should say what they are in two seconds; a member's window-graphics ad
  read as a coffee ad until the before/after buttons were made much bigger [10/8].
- Skip the logo; the page name already shows and it consumes space. Exception: branding campaigns
  [9/25].
- Copy: fill all five primary-text and five headline slots with human-written variations on the same
  angle. Mix long and short; first sentence always the hook. Do not use Meta's AI-generated copy.
  Use "Breakdown, by dynamic creative element" to read which text or headline wins. Do not use
  dynamic creative for images [9/25].
- Keep statics in almost every account; Dan cites roughly 70% of people not sitting through videos
  (his number, unverified) [9/25].
- Emojis: Dan does not use them. They helped a painting-kit brand's feel but hurt a professional
  equity-trainer account until removed. Test [9/25].
- Upload a 9:16, 4:5, and 1:1 version. Uncheck the auto aspect-ratio box (Meta groups them and makes up
  to four sizes). Under Customize placements, 9:16 on Stories/Reels only, 4:5 and 1:1 on feeds only.
  A 9:16 in a feed gets cut off. Turn off Meta's enhancements. Recheck after publish because it
  sneaks back [10/29].
- Dan's own client workflow for 40 short objection videos: AI-written scripts, filmed on a phone with a teleprompter app,
  captioned, with on-screen text naming the objection
  [10/29].

### Lead gen and lead quality
- Instant forms bring in roughly "10 to 1 lower lead quality" in Dan's accounts; landing pages cost
  about 3 times more per lead but "90% of them answer the phone." Cost per lead means nothing if you
  are not making money [10/15].
- He says this is his own accounts only. Experienced buyers in masterminds swear by instant forms. For
  the instant-form-for-home-services member he conceded "I don't want to say don't use them" and said a
  fair verdict needs 10 to 20 leads, not two [10/15].
- If you must use an instant form: use a higher-intent form with conditional logic that ejects
  unqualified people and a qualifying question. Dan is not against forms that already work: "definitely
  don't change anything" for a member whose forms were producing [10/22].
- Meta shows instant forms to people who tend to fill out instant forms [10/15].
- Add friction: 2 booked of 31 leads is too low. Friction can be a single honest qualifier, e.g. "Do
  you own your home? Yes/No" with a "No" ejecting; plus zip code and address on the page [10/8].
- Price: do not put price on the form for a sales-call offer. A qualifying question can bracket income
  or investment. A roofing estimate is sold on the appointment [10/8, 10/15].
- Ad to landing page: the page continues the ad's conversation. One landing page per angle is ideal
  but not needed; swap the headline. Priority list: get people to submit the form first; tweak page
  copy second; first thing they see should be the next step (book a call) [10/8].
- Never send ad traffic to a home page. Use a funnel: very few navigation options, a call to action
  above the fold, one path "like an IKEA store." One unnecessary click can cut conversion in half
  (Dan's claim) [9/25].
- Speed to lead, nurture, and no-answers are a separate problem; if leads are not answering the phone,
  the fix is automations and follow-up, not ads [10/8].
- Lead-quality dispute with clients: report booked and money-making volume, not just lead count.
  "We're getting fewer leads, but look how much more money you're making" [10/15].

### Local business
- Targeting income still works: Detailed Targeting, search "household income," choose top 5%, 10%, or
  25%. Dan's first move for high-ticket clients ("it quickly resolves it"). For tree removal chasing
  bigger jobs, start with the top 25% [10/29, 9/25].
- Watch audience size: a few cities combined with an income filter can shrink the audience until leads
  stop. If they stop, drop the filter and qualify on the form instead [10/29].
- Dan does not endorse interest/intent targeting; one buyer said it is "essentially worthless now."
  The creative does the targeting [10/15].
- Local audience limits: "local advertising is very easy to hit almost everybody in your market pretty
  quick," so expect a refresh every one to two weeks at higher budgets [10/15].
- Multiple angles for a home-service business (drywall, windows, bathroom, and so on) in separate ad
  sets, with different messages, because a buyer usually sees several touchpoints before they fill out a
  form (4 to 20 for e-commerce, about 8 typical) [10/15].
- If your CPL is $75 and the budget is $100 a day, you get about one lead a day; it is what it is [10/15].
- Run a follower/awareness trickle only if the budget allows; it may make you "the one people go to"
  in a local market [10/8].

### Meta platform changes
- Creative diversity rating added in Ads Manager (see Creative) [10/29].
- Ad entity IDs replace creative IDs for grouping [10/29].
- Andromeda wants many diverse creatives and a budget around 10x CPA to sort them. Below that, force
  spend [10/15].
- Learning phase matters less; weekly budget behavior (above) [10/15, 9/25].
- Q4 and election cycle: costs spiked across accounts. Examples: CPL from $6 to $10 with lead volume
  falling from about 40 estimates a week to 20; Dan thinks election plus Q4 but "we don't know for
  sure." Advice: relaunch with new creative anyway and watch it a day or two [10/29, 10/15].
- Ads Manager glitches in early October (ads missing from view) [10/8].
- Meta's auto-enhancements and aspect-ratio grouping break layouts (see Creative) [10/29].
- Instant-form and DM ("click to message") campaigns: Dan stopped running DM campaigns; members report
  glitches where clicks do not open a message (UK/EU and US coaches) [10/29].

## 2. Member cases worth learning from

1. **Fort Wayne multi-location retailer, about $10K a month, phone-first.** Click-to-call ads and link-click
   campaigns; owner refuses tracking numbers ("I only want to use my number"), so the only
   feedback was the front desk saying the phones were ringing or dead. A newer location was
   "terrible." Dan: stop running link-click objectives (clicks find clickers), switch to a
   conversion objective, form or call, track everything. A cool car photo was probably producing the
   clicks. He did not answer how to attribute calls without a tracking number [10/29]. Closest
   analogue to Got Moles: phone-first, no attribution, owner resistance.
2. **Tree removal contractor wanting bigger jobs.** Early jobs ($15K, $20K, $7K) then three weeks of
   $1K to $2K jobs; offer averages $2K. Dan: target household income top 25% first, watch audience
   size; if leads stop, qualify on the form on job size and send Meta only the qualified event. Asked to
   screenshot the setup for QA before launch [10/29]. Direct analogue for steering Got Moles toward
   commercial and 5-acre work.
3. **Home services lead-gen buyer, 50% drop in leads over two weeks.** Cost per lead moved from $6 to $10;
   estimates 40 a week to 20; B-roll plus voiceover ads in one style; frequency 2.12 over 7 days but 3.63
   over the full period; audience about 500,000. Dan: election cycle and Q4 plus creative fatigue; relaunch a
   campaign with different formats (statics, videos, different style) rather than same footage with a
   new voiceover [10/29].
4. **Florida roofing and windows company, $100 a day per ad set, audience 750K to 1.3M.** Five static
   ads, only one lead earlier; ad headline "We handle the paperwork" and "Stronger homes, brighter
   homes" did not say windows. Dan: fix the hook so a scroller knows the product in half a second, test
   one angle (hurricane) first, drop the logo, fill five headlines and five primary texts, drop the
   per-county ad sets into one county ad set, add household-income top 10%, open the age to 25, keep
   Advantage+ off [9/25, 10/8]. Landing page: "Do you own your home" yes/no with zip and address, later a
   new pixel for qualified data [10/8]. The same company's earlier funnel: $597 per booked appointment on
   $3,582; Dan said "a little high" and told them to optimize for the step before the booking.
5. **Impact-window company with 31 instant-form leads and 2 booked.** Dan: add friction, build the landing
   page, new pixel. CPL $4.61 on 394 leads from $1,815 and one purchase in August. Meta showed zero
   revenue because the class-management system sent none back; the integration is fixed now [10/8].
   Lesson: cheap form leads, no tracked outcome.
6. **Home-service media buyer ($30 to $50 a day clients, about 40 clients).** Instant forms and DMs beat
   landing pages for a remodeler near Chicago (two booked appointments in 48 hours) and fail for a remodeler in
   Austin at $75 a day, with a similar audience of 4 to 5 million. Dan: two leads proves nothing; need 10 to 20; no magic
   bullet at tiny budgets; run multiple angles; outspending wins; he runs a large window installer at
   $10,000 a day [10/15].
7. **Water filtration, $300 to $350 max CAC, lead cost historically $30 to $50.** Dan: about $500 a day
   would be a sweet spot; then CBO with one ad set holding all ads, or a few ad sets by angle and stage of
   awareness; creative refresh every one to two weeks [10/15].
8. **Appliance repair, high intent, needs messages "right then."** Member says instant forms are all that
   works. Dan: test it, but "I don't think an opt-in is required for home service" [10/15].
9. **Kids' tutoring center, new client, no pixel firing on inner pages.** Fix the pixel (find the host,
   get login, install via platform plugin if Shopify) before any ad spend [10/22].
10. **Print and window-graphics business.** Two avatars (storefront owners, marketing agencies), a landing
    page per avatar. Dan: ad looked like coffee; make the "before/after" labels bigger; one landing page
    can serve several avatars; optimize for an application or set appointment; cost per booked
    appointment was $597 on $3,582 [10/8].
11. **Lead-gen with an instant form that works.** A member's angle ("school system is broken") produced
    $40 qualified leads on $160 a day per ad set (total $320). Dan: do not change what works; leave
    running, judge after consultation dates pass [10/22].
12. **Fitness DM campaigns.** $48 to $65 cost per message, link click to message 1.93% (1,339 clicks, 22
    messages). Dan: start at 2x to 3x CPA ($150) and kill at 3x target; DMs are unreliable and quality is
    poor; test a book-a-call funnel with the same budget and creatives for a fair comparison [10/29].
13. **Salon (school community) and mindset coach.** Salon: diversity of format, $50 a day, three ads, one per
    asset. Coach: 175 registrants, 31 showed; answer: engagement-only video retargeting [10/29].
14. **Supplement and painting-kit e-commerce.** Skim only: bundles and AOV over free shipping; shipping at
    $6.95 flat; shipping and cart are the leak when add-to-cart is good and checkout is poor. Skim; not
    transferable [9/25].

## 3. Where the calls differ from or add to the course

Differences and additions, then proposed edits to `got-moles-operating-rules.md` (not applied).

### Differences and additions
- **Test spend per ad is probably too low.** Dan wants 2x to 3x target CPA per test unit and 3x as the
  kill line. The operating rules set tests at $10/day per ad set for about 5 days ($50), which reaches
  3x target CPL only when target CPL is below about $17 (inference, not a Dan statement). Either test
  fewer ads for longer or raise the target-CPL tolerance; do not call a kill at under 3x CPL spent.
- **Frequency is not a fatigue trigger.** The rules use "7-day frequency above 3.0 and CPL up 2 weeks
  running." Dan: no magic frequency; rising CPA with rising frequency is the signal.
- **Pause vs trickle.** Rules have "pause the ad." Dan says pausing a working ad can lose its footing.
  For a seasonal business this matters at the season edges.
- **Geography.** Rules say target the zips actually serviced. Dan: start broad (counties, not 50 zip
  sets), one ad set, and split ad sets only by angle, not place.
- **Household income.** Newly available as a lever for large-property work; absent from the rules.
- **Qualification at the form and "send only qualified events."** New and strong; the rules only mention
  an upstream form-submit event.
- **Appointment window.** Rules say weekly cohorts at 7 to 14 days; Dan says wait until the booked date
  has happened. For Got Moles, quote and first visit dates may be later than 14 days.
- **Judge at the ad-set level.** Ads on the same angle assist each other.
- **Click-to-call campaigns.** Dan treats a click-to-call conversion campaign as an acceptable alternative to
  link clicks. Rules forbid the Calls objective until calls are attributable. No contradiction on the
  condition, but Dan gave no attribution method.
- **Pixel hygiene.** Rules do not mention resetting a polluted pixel. Dan's rule of thumb: reset only
  when the history is mostly junk.
- **Q4 and election noise.** Costs and CPMs rose broadly in October 2026.
- **Placements and enhancements.** The rules have no creative-delivery hygiene.

### Proposed edits (for Spencer's approval, not applied)
1. **Section 2, kill/hold table.** Replace the test row with: kill only after spend of at least 3x target
   CPL with no lead; size each test unit to at least 2x target CPL over the test window (fewer ads,
   not thinner ads); replace the fatigue row with "frequency alone never triggers a refresh; refresh when
   CPL rises while frequency rises; judge weekly, not daily, because Meta flexes daily spend inside a
   weekly total"; add "do not pause a working ad to experiment; step the budget down instead."
2. **Section 5, geography and audience.** Add: one ad set across the whole service area; split ad sets
   by angle, never by city or zip; consider an optional household-income top-25% test for the
   commercial and 5-acre offer, checking audience size first; Advantage+ stays off; minimum age stays
   25 (Dan agrees).
3. **Section 1, two clocks and events.** Add: fire the Lead event only for qualified leads (homeowner,
   in the service area, correct property type) so Meta learns only the qualified profile; judge a
   cohort only after quoted or booked dates have passed, and judge angle at ad-set level; if lead volume
   is under about 20 a week, optimize for the earliest event that gives volume.
4. **Section 6, calls.** Add that Traffic/link-click campaigns are not used for acquisition; a
   click-to-call conversion objective stays gated on call attribution; ask for a pixel health check
   (events on every page) before launch.
5. **Section 4, seasonality.** Add: October through the election and into Q4, CPMs and CPL are inflated
   across accounts; do not judge creative fatigue or restructure on a spike lasting under a week.
6. **Section 9, change control.** Add: after every publish, open the live ad and verify placements, aspect
   ratios and that Meta enhancements are off; one major change a week.
7. **Section 7, offer and claims.** Add creative craft: state what the ad is for in the first half-second
   (yard and lawn first, then mole); skip logos; five human-written headlines and primary texts on the
   same angle (no Meta AI text), read the dynamic-creative breakdown; no dynamic creative for images.
8. **Section 2, creative diversity.** Add: a new concept means a different scene, person or format, not a
   text swap; check the Ads Manager diversity rating.

## 4. Open questions to ask Dan on a future call

1. **Phone-first attribution.** We are a local service where most conversions are phone calls, tracked in
   CallRail, booked days later in Jobber. What is your best method to get Meta to optimize toward
   calls it cannot see (offline conversions with call time and value, a landing-page click-to-call
   event, or a Calls objective with a tracking number), at about 10 to 20 booked jobs a month?
2. **Small budgets and cost per booked job.** If a booked job is worth about $450 to $600 and we can spend
   $X a day, what is the smallest daily budget and event choice where a single ad set exits
   "learning limited" and stays stable, and should we run one ad set until it breaks?
3. **Seasonal pause.** Mole demand drops sharply in summer and hard freezes. Should we trickle spend
   down to a low retargeting floor through the off-season, or pause and relaunch, given your warning that
   a campaign paused for days can lose its footing?
4. **Inherited pixel.** Our pixel and dataset may carry years of agency-era events (form leads that never
   booked). How do you decide between a clean new pixel and keeping the history when you cannot see how
   much of it was junk, and how long does a new pixel take to stabilize at under 20 leads a week?
5. **Small, finite geography and an ambiguous word.** With a service area of a few counties and the word
   "mole" also meaning a skin mole, what frequency is normal before a small local audience saturates, how
   often should creative rotate, and have you seen ambiguous-word ads waste spend on the wrong
   audience (how would you spot it in the placement and breakdown reports)?
