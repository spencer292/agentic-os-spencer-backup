## 1. Speed to lead and lead nurture

- **Stats Steve cites (S03/03-speed-to-lead):** 78% of customers buy from the first company that responds; conversion is 8x higher in the first 5 minutes; only 30% of companies respond within an hour. Rule: call back, do not text or email back; if they text, move them to a phone call. Qualified Christmas-light leads get a sales rep on site within 12-24h with a named rep and calendar invite, so the prospect "checks it off the list".
- **Why it matters for paid leads (C01):** a form fill tells the prospect to start shopping; no fast answer means they call the next three. A missed-call auto-text ("it's Steve with X, I saw your call") keeps them waiting. Steve's example (C06): same 100 leads, instant connect plus text versus manual calling gives roughly 3x return on the same ad spend.
- **Lead sequence Steve runs (C02):** form submit, system calls the owner ("press 2 to connect"), owner connects live (prospect says "wow, you're fast"). No answer: voicemail drop, then text. Wait 12h, text 2 ("still want to get this done?"). 24h later, text 3 ("keep me posted"). "Shocking how many respond to the third."
- **30-day last-chance text (C02):** about 50 never-reached leads sit in a "reached out, never got a hold of" column. One text at day 30: "You reached out on one of our ads. Is this something you want right now, or should I stop bothering you?" Result: about 10 replies; 5-6 "not now, back around the holidays"; about 3 genuinely interested.
- **Automation tuning (C08, C10):** the sequence can annoy people. Failures members hit: voicemail and text still fire after the owner talked to the lead; text goes out at 10 pm; every call attempt moves the lead to "engaged". Fixes: wait steps (15 min to 1 h), business-hours IF branches, exit the workflow on reply. Opposite risk: one member who held back automation for 2-4 hours lost the lead to a competitor (C08). Test a workflow with a `name+test@gmail.com` address and walk it through.
- **Pipeline discipline (C08, C09):** send only qualified leads into the field-service CRM (Jobber); set pipeline stages to match your sales process; put dollar values on open opportunities so you can see money sitting in quotes. One member found the real problem was stale quotes with no follow-up, then built a drip for them (C09).
- **Invoice reminder automation (C09):** invoice sent, reminder at day 5, up to 4 reminders, then alert the owner. Built because the owner was "bad at collecting cash".
- **Source tracking loss (C08, C09, C10):** forwarding CallRail numbers into the CRM phone system drops the source tag; the member manually keyed source and planned to port the numbers. Steve's PE-owned pool-company example (C06): every location, GBP listing and truck has its own tracking number, so a monthly pie chart shows revenue by source. Tracking numbers cost about $5/mo.

## 2. Reactivation and winback

- **Open-appointment text (C02, C07, C01):** Steve got this from a mobile dog groomer: "We'll be in your area next Wednesday May 14, arrival window 12:15-2:15, want to book this spot?" Convenience framing, a real date and window, implied scarcity. Rewrite for your trade with ChatGPT (prompt: "rewrite this text for a [X] company"). Add an expiring offer (Steve: waive a $50 trip charge "since we're already in the area").
- **Expected response (C07, C02):** 100 texts gets about 3-10 replies. 300 ghosted leads with 3 replies is "an absolute no-brainer win". If the slot is taken, offer another ("that one filled, I have Thursday 12-2"); the goal is reopening the conversation, not the exact time. Steve says the booked time is often not the pitched time (C02).
- **Overbooking control (C02):** split the list by zip or city and send each segment a different day, so responders are geographically clustered; or split 250/250 across two days.
- **Why free jobs matter (C07):** at 5x ROAS, ad-sourced jobs carry 20% acquisition cost, so a job from your own list earns about 20% more margin ($200 on a $1,000 job). Green Mango Pest Control (sold for about $120M per Steve) "kept nurturing the list" as the free-margin engine.
- **Cadence (C02):** monthly email or text to the whole database, 5 minutes, calendar reminder. Big brands do it to people who have not bought in years. Worst case is an unsubscribe.
- **Steve's mindset line (C02, C07):** "They already haven't given you money, so the worst case is they continue not giving you money." Accept automation quirks (wrong first names) as the price of reaching 400 people.
- **Mailers tied to completed jobs (C02):** Dope Marketing syncs to the CRM and mails the nearest 10-20 houses after each scheduled job ("we just did a job for your neighbor"). Steve was negotiating a member discount; cost not quoted.

## 3. Google Business Profile (the free channel Steve rates highest)

- **Results he reports (C01, S02/02):** 145 inbound calls in Q4 (Oct-Dec) for his lighting business, about $120K of sales; at $25-30 per paid conversion that equals about $4,350 of ad spend (S02). GBP alone "six figures in two months" in season (C02). A member reports $7K with no ad spend from organic work (C03). After a photo-review sprint, client GBPs went from about 1 lead/week to 4-5 per month, per Steve (C01).
- **What moves ranking (C01, C03):**
  1. Photo reviews are the strongest lever. Steve's new brand had 4 of 14 reviews with photos (about 30%) and reached top 1-3 in its radius within a month (grid scans March 31 vs April 23). A Google-maps agency owner told Steve that across their portfolio the one common factor of top-ranked listings was a service-address pin, followed by review count, products/services filled in, activity and review frequency.
  2. Target about 15 photo reviews, then fill out every service and product with keyword-specific text (ChatGPT drafts).
  3. Link the profile's site links to Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, X, LinkedIn so Google sees corroboration. Make an X account, post once, link it.
  4. Upload every Instagram post to the listing as owner.
  5. Answer the Q&A section; commercial buyers read it (C03).
  6. A brand-new GBP can dip for a week after drastic edits, then rise (C01).
- **Review capture (C01, C03, C05):**
  - Google tap cards (NFC): hold over the customer's phone, it opens the five-star flow. Steve says tap cards in person beat text automation 4-5x (about 5 reviews/mo vs 25/mo); a window cleaner got 240 reviews in 6 months (C01).
  - Text automation is "second line of defense, batting cleanup" (C01).
  - Photo asks: selfie with the customer in front of the branded vehicle, before/after photo sent for them to attach, or one text asking for a photo (C01, C03). Roughly 1 in 5-8 reviews carries a photo (C01).
  - Manual text script for past customers (C05): "Hi Tim, this is Steve with X. We've been focusing on reviews because it's the best way for a small family business to grow. Would you leave one? [paste exact review link]". Offer a $5 coffee gift card, framed as "coffee on me"; Steve warns it can read as paying for reviews (C05).
  - Incentive tactic he mentions (C01): $5 to the tech if a review names them. Conflicts with Got Moles' review-policy rules (see Got Moles fit).
- **Commercial-specific reviews (C03):** ask commercial clients to name the building or property in the review ("Adam from X Property Management, regarding service at Carter Apartments"). Screenshot those reviews and send them to prospects in the same market.
- **Verification and suspension (C01, C07, C10):** use an email on your website's domain for the GBP login (instant approval for Steve's new warehouse brand). Virtual offices and co-working addresses are a coin flip: Regus at about $250-300/mo worked for some and got others suspended; if co-working, hide that on the door sign and use a printed placard with suite number (C07). Residential addresses get flagged; a service-area-only listing is the safe option if GBP is already producing (C01). Competitors spam-report listings; Steve's junk-removal GBP was permanently banned and 30% of revenue vanished overnight (C03). Do not rely on one platform.
- **Multiple GBPs (C01):** if one performs, pour all reviews and effort into it; check each listing's analytics for calls and site visits.

## 4. Meta (Facebook/Instagram) ads, as of mid-2025

- **Budget and structure (C03, C05, C07):** $10/day cannot exit the learning phase; $30-50/day is the starting range; 100-150K audience; Steve says his Phoenix market does not plateau until $250-300/day. Three creatives minimum per campaign (his team won't answer questions without them, C05); 6-7 creatives before a winner is typical, then variations of the winner (C03). Facebook will starve some creatives within 1-2 days (normal).
- **Format (C02, C03, C05):** lead forms, not Messenger (Messenger clicks fire the pixel falsely, C05). A/B test: an address field made cost per lead 4x ($85); use first name, email, phone, city (C02). Optional "Do you own a home?" with conditional logic to kick renters (C02).
- **Creative structure (C03):** call out location, who you are, what you do, why different, an offer at the end. Show your face and uniform; a lone before/after from an unknown page gets ignored (C03, C05). Film 4 location-specific hook openers (4 seconds each) and splice onto shared B-roll (C02). Satisfying-process B-roll plus voiceover is a cheap extra variant (C02).
- **High-income targeting (C02, C03):** narrowing to rich zips doubled his cost per lead but jobs were $15-20K and intent higher; don't judge for 3-4 weeks; if no lead for $100 after 4-5 days, kill and re-evaluate (C03). The pixel learns from whatever converts, so pre-qualify in the copy ("do not book if...") or it optimizes toward junk leads.
- **Still images (C03):** in mid-2025 still-image CPMs were a third to half of video CPMs in Steve's tests and his agency-network peers' (not true in 2024). Mixed ad sets (2 still, 2 video) recommended.
- **Research (C01, C05):** Facebook Ad Library to study competitors' ads; record an ad playing and have ChatGPT transcribe and rewrite the concept for your business (copy the concept, not the words).
- **Judging performance (C01, C06, C07):** cost per lead is a vanity number; track cost per appointment, per quote, close rate. Steve's Christmas season: about $12K Facebook spend in 40 days returned about $130K (C06). A good ROAS is 5x (C07).
- **Seasonality quirk (C03):** July 4th week Meta returns worsen for 3-4 days (visitors flood the market), then normalize. Diversify so a platform ban or glitch does not zero the business.
- **Recurring-revenue frame (C01):** for monthly plans (cleaning, pest) do not judge ads on month one; judge on LTV. Cleaning companies typically become profitable on acquisition cost in month 3, provided churn is low.
- **Google paid (C01, C05, C06):** Steve says Google PPC is the one channel worth paying an agency for because negatives, targeting and bidding burn money fast. For LSA: target high-income zips only (USPS EDDM tool sorts zips by income) and bid to be first; otherwise you are the third or fourth call, effectively an "Angie lead" (C01). A $600-ticket member got poor LSA quality (C01). Add Google PPC only after Meta spend hits $70-100/day and results plateau (C05).
- **Agency math (C06, S02):** a $1,000/mo retainer on $1,500 of ad spend is 40% sunk cost. Steve says good agencies start around $4,000/mo and do landing pages and testing. He is "anti-agency up to $100-150K/mo" for lower-ticket trades because creative is the real work (S02). The exception he makes is Google PPC.

## 5. Offline and local density (neighbor effect)

- **Six-touch stack (C06):** (1) branded yard sign in the customer's yard after the job, (2) public-facing generic sign with a forwarding number, (3) branded vehicle/uniform, (4) A-frame at the job (QR code; "instant action play" whereas the truck wrap is a "branding play"; members call it their best $100), (5) photo review at completion, (6) door hangers to the nearest 10-15 neighbors. A prospect sees the brand 4-6 times in a day. Don't run door hangers alone.
- **Branded yard sign (C06):** offer $15 off to leave a sign up; 3 of 4 say yes when asked directly. Make it look good (brand colors).
- **Public signs (C06, C07, C01, S01/02):** only worth doing at volume: first test 150 in a month; 50 per week; 200 over a month at about $4 each. About half do not survive day 2. Use a Google Voice forwarding number and answer with an acronym to avoid angry callers leaving 1-star reviews. Keep text to service plus phone number only ("two to five seconds of attention"). Data points: $300 of signs returned $1,800-1,900 (S01/02); an Ohio painter reportedly ran 450 signs/mo to $40K/mo with no city complaints, while in Scottsdale Steve gets complaint calls (C01). Some Georgia cities pull signs (C06). Track coverage by coloring EDDM routes on a map, not by sign location (C06).
- **Door knocking (C05, C06):** Steve avoids it; a $1M/mo window installer saw 1-star reviews from solicitation complaints once its brand was visible. EDDM (C02): about 21c postage plus about 10c print = about 37c/piece; works for high ticket (roofing, ponds) at the 4th-5th touch; his junk-removal test of 25,000 postcards cost about $6K and returned under $5K. Verdict: skip for transactional services; use the CRM-triggered neighbor mailer instead.
- **Delight moments (C05):** train techs to find small unrequested things (drag trash bins up, wipe gutter fronts) and tell the customer they did it; cites reciprocity. Referral reward should be the customer's next service free when LTV is high (cleaning example: $100 reward against $1,200-1,500 LTV, C01).
- **Referral partners (C05):** don't ask realtors for referrals; offer them leads first.

## 6. Commercial outreach (C03, C02)

- **Drop volume (C03):** minimum 5, ideally 10 gift drops per week (donuts/cookies, about $8) to dream commercial accounts; weeks 1-2 little traction, then conversations, then a random inbound call citing a drop from 2-3 weeks earlier. At 1/week "don't bother".
- **Positioning (C03):** the "guy best friend" model: you are not closing on the spot; you are who they call when the incumbent screws up. Commercial pros are also cons: 5-10x bigger jobs, longer cycle, fewer competitors (insurance and vendor packets screen out amateurs), sticky customers.
- **Proof loop (C03):** collaborative Instagram reels with commercial clients (a gym reel got 363 likes and a few thousand views); show that content to the next prospect in the same sector. A golf-course member reports multiple recurring commercial accounts this way.
- **Cold email (C02, C03):** AI-scraped, hyper-personalized email services charge about $400-500/mo or about $80 per inbound response (Steve's contact; first week brought a mega church and a 36-store owner). Commit 2-3 weeks before judging; a $5,000+ job pays for six responses.
- **Insurance (S04/15):** general liability is a prerequisite for bigger or commercial jobs; get certificates on demand. Workers' comp is legally required with W-2 staff in most states.
- **Gatekeepers (C03):** skip cold calling; bring something and use "did you take bribes with a smile".

## 7. Pricing, offers, sales process, scaling

- **Supply-and-demand pricing (C02):** two members raise price about 20% for slots when booked out 3-4 weeks and discount to fill an empty week (labor retention).
- **Offer pre-framing (C01):** Steve states a $1,000 minimum in Christmas-light ads to filter, and asks six or seven questions on lead forms in peak season to prove intent (not for off-season).
- **Financing (C07):** claims 65% of home-improvement projects over $5K are financed; lead the pitch with the monthly payment (anchor), then offer 10% off for paying in full. One member financed about one fifth of jobs at 14%/yr.
- **Leave-behind (C07):** give a printed branded folder so the other spouse is not left to "sell" the decision.
- **Staffing (C05):** Steve has 2 people on sales (40-50 h/wk each), install crews, and an office team on fulfillment (30 employees at peak). Owners at $20-50K plateau because marketing, sales and fulfillment all degrade when one person does all three.
- **Scaling rule (C05):** if a channel works at 50 yard signs or $50/day, ask why you did the same next month. Scale until it plateaus. Avoid "D+ effort at every channel". Steve's focus list: Meta (organic + paid), Google (GBP first, then LSA/PPC), content, plus the offline stack; he says that reaches $100K/mo. Ignore magazines, directory scorecards, Yelp ads, Thumbtack, Angie. Paid share of Steve's Christmas-light work fell from 95% (year 1) to 45% (year 5) as organic and referral grew.
- **Growth stages (C05):** failure points are year 1 (effort) and year 3 (owner role not changing). At each step ($10K, 50K, 100K, 200K/mo) the owner's job changes; the "death zone" is repeating the same week for years. Typical stall: hiring the first person and "turning the thermostat back down".

## 8. Operations and admin tips usable at $1M

- **Business email (S04/09):** Google Workspace admin console (about $19/mo per Steve) so you can revoke a departing employee's access and keep their customer emails. Also helps GBP login matching (C01).
- **Stack (C06):** Steve keeps the lead CRM until the customer wants a quote, then moves to Jobber; quote via Jobber portal; footer "this number does not receive text, call X" avoids two-phone-number confusion; on-my-way texts moved to the lead-CRM number.
- **Quote-link bridge (C10):** store the Jobber portal link in a custom field and push via Zapier into the text template. Jobber sync via Zapier needs an email address or the contact will not sync (C08).
- **Cards (S04/12):** Amex Gold gives 4x points on advertising and fuel (Steve: $5K/mo ad spend is 20K points/mo). Charge cards, pay in full.
- **Banking (S04/11):** separate business account; know last month's revenue; credit union relationships help with financing.
- **Content gear (S01/05):** $20 70-inch phone tripod and a lavalier mic (under $60 together); audio matters more than video quality; consistent camera position for before/after.
- **Website (S04/07):** a website is not a lead generator until SEO or ads send traffic.
- **Review gating warning (C08, C10):** the lead-CRM review flow asks "rate us 1-10" first and sends 4-5 star responders to Google, low scorers to an internal form. That is review gating, which Google's policy prohibits. Do not copy it.

# Got Moles fit

Context: about $1.1M in 2026, five field techs, TMCP at $100-150/mo plus one-off Quick Fix, 2027 plan about $2.05M (residential recurring +25%, winter/spring selling push, Quick Fix to recurring winback, 1.5 commercial accounts/month, price increases Jan/Mar), marketing coming in-house, Meta being rebuilt, LinkedIn for commercial.

## High fit: do or test

1. **Reactivation open-appointment text tied to the route grid (C07, C02, C01).** Maps straight onto Quick Fix winback and ghosted quotes. Got Moles has what Steve's members lack: each zip has a known service day, so "our tech is in your area Tuesday between X and Y" is true, not invented. Expect 1-3% reply rate (3-10 per 100). Segment by service day so responders cluster. Use the TMCP offer as the expiring incentive. Needs consent and opt-out handling before any send, and Spencer approval per the mkt-cmo rules.
2. **Quote follow-up sequence and day-30 "stop bothering you" text (C02, C09).** Same data, lowest cost. Add the stale-quote drip and the invoice-reminder automation (day 5, up to 4 reminders, then flag the owner).
3. **Speed-to-lead discipline (S03/03, C02, C08, C10).** Already part of phone training; use the stats and the 12h/24h text cadence for missed-call and form leads. Copy the tuning lessons: stop on reply or answer, quiet hours, wait steps. Check the CallRail source-tag loss pitfall (C08-C10) against the current setup.
4. **LTV framing for ads (C01, C07).** Judge recurring plans on month-3 payback, not first-month ROAS, and cost per appointment/quote/close over cost per lead (C01, C05, C06). Matches the mkt-cmo CPBJ / payback / mix standard.
5. **Meta mechanics for the rebuild (C02, C03, C05).** 3+ creatives, $30-50/day minimum, lead form without an address field (4x CPL jump), a homeowner filter if apartments are common, location-callout hooks, show the tech and truck, pre-qualify in copy, wait 3-4 weeks before judging high-income zips, $100-no-lead kill rule (re-base to Got Moles' own CPBJ). The Ad Library research trick is free. Posture A applies to picture and words.
6. **GBP photo reviews and Q&A across all three listings (C01, C03).** Highest free leverage. Add: photo ask on every completed first visit, Q&A seeded, products/services filled, socials linked, tap cards (the 4-5x claim is Steve's). Respect Got Moles' review-policy P0 (no quotas, no technician-naming, disclosed incentives): skip Steve's "$5 to the tech for a review naming them" and don't offer undisclosed gift cards. Never copy the rate-first review gating (C08, C10).
7. **Commercial outreach cadence (C03, C02).** The 1.5 accounts/month target needs volume: Steve's number is 5-10 touches per week to named accounts and 2-3 weeks to the first inbound. Fits LinkedIn nurture and whale hunt as the in-person complement (gift drops). Reviews that name the managed property and collab content with HOA or property-manager clients are cheap proof loops. Keep the insurance certificate and vendor packet ready (S04/15). Cory owns bids; route drop-offs and follow-up accordingly.
8. **Neighbor effect: A-frame, branded yard sign, door hangers (C06, C01).** Moles are a neighborhood problem and techs already work weekly clusters. Branded sign at TMCP properties, A-frame at the job, door hangers to the nearest 10-15 doors on a first visit ("sorry for the noise" courtesy framing). Wording follows Posture A and the chemical-free lead, no trap language. Cheap to trial in one territory.
9. **CRM-triggered neighbor mailer (C02).** Mails the nearest 10-20 houses after each job. Cost unknown. The only mailer format Steve endorses for transactional services.
10. **Referral reward sized to LTV (C01).** "Refer a friend who signs, get a free month" costs $100-150 against a TMCP lifetime value Steve would call a no-brainer. Run it past acc-cfo before launch.

## Medium fit: useful, needs adaptation

- **Scale-what-works audit (C05).** For every channel that pays (GBP, Google, any Meta test), ask why spend and volume stayed flat. Got Moles' ceiling is territory density and tech capacity, not audience size.
- **Booked-out pricing (C02).** Raise price when booked out 3-4 weeks. Jan/Mar increases are already set by plan; this is a spring Quick Fix intake signal. Decisions go through acc-cfo and Spencer.
- **Leave-behind folder for bids (C07).** Cheap addition to Cory's commercial bids so a second decision-maker has material.
- **Financing (C07).** Only relevant for commercial bids above $5K or annual prepay; TMCP is already monthly.
- **Separate owners for sales, marketing, fulfillment (C05).** Supports bringing marketing in-house.
- **Agency math (C06, S02).** Confirms the in-house move; his one exception is Google PPC, already scripted for Got Moles.
- **Diversification (C03).** A GBP ban cost Steve 30% of revenue overnight. Worth a documented fallback if any listing is suspended.
- **Tracking numbers per source (C06).** About $5/mo each; extends CallRail source tracking to mailers, signs and A-frame.
- **Workspace admin for every seat (S04/09).** Confirm departing-employee mail can be reclaimed.
