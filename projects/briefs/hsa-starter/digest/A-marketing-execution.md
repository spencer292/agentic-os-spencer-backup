Source: HSA Starter Program (Steve, home-service coach), Skool corpus at `projects/briefs/hsa-starter/skool-corpus/`. Folder prefixes below: 08 cycle, 09 yard signs, 11 A-frame, 12 door hangers, 13 gift cards, 06 Facebook/IG, 07 Google Business, 04 business setup.

## Part 1: Tactics as taught

### 1. The HSA cycle (framing for everything else) - 08/02, 08/03, 08/04
Five phases ("building a fire"):
1. Fire pit: look credible everywhere (Google Maps, website, Facebook/IG, Nextdoor). Free.
2. Logs: organic neighborhood tactics plus Google reviews. Signs, A-frame, door hangers, branded truck and uniform, photo review, gift cards, referral ask. Treat every job as the marketing event; goal is to turn 1 job into 2 or 3 via neighbors.
3. Gasoline: Facebook ads, optionally LSA.
4. Measure: speed-to-lead and close rate. Per 10 quotes over 30 days, how many said yes. If the close rate is healthy, raise all prices 5%, re-measure a month, repeat until close rate drops.
5. Nurture: email to everyone who got a quote (yes, no, ghosted); Google review automation to catch stragglers.

Touch-point stack per job (08/02, 11/03): branded vehicle (1), visible work (2), A-frame (3), door hangers (4), location-tied Google reviews (5), neighborhood Facebook-group post by a champion customer (6), gift cards (7). Steve: do all of them every job; picking and choosing fails.

Claimed rates: reviews from about 4 of 5 customers, photo reviews from about 1 of 5 (08/02).

Speed-to-lead (08/04): form leads and missed calls leak to competitors. Tool shown: SnipeyLead (phones the owner the instant a form is submitted, press 2 to connect; if no answer, drops a pre-recorded voicemail plus auto-text; follow-up texts at about 12 hours and a third; business hours only). Claimed lifts: close rate +15-20%, second text about 25% reply, third about 10-15%. Cites an unnamed "Harvard Business Review" study (5 min vs 1 min contact). Treat those numbers as unverified.

### 2. Yard signs - 09/01 to 09/05
Two separate sign programs.

A. Unbranded "public" signs (09/01, 09/02, 09/03)
- Copy: service name on top, phone number at bottom, one color. No brand or logo, so complaints, one-star reviews and city code reports cannot attach to the company (Steve: about 1 in 10 calls is an angry neighbor).
- Phone: a free Google Voice number (personal Gmail account, "personal use") forwarding to the owner's phone, so the business number cannot be Googled. Settings: caller ID shows the caller's number, call screening off, spam filter off on the phone, vague voicemail greeting, call back from the Voice app. Test by calling it from another phone.
- Vendor: Dope Marketing, HSA-exclusive page `shop.dopemarketing.com/products/hsa-yardsigns` (not the main site, which has different pricing). 100 single-sided one-color 18x24 signs with stakes $315, up to 500 for $1,575, free shipping. Flow: add to cart, check out, email with order number, submit the design form (service + phone, new design, no QR, no subscription). Proof comes back by text/email quickly.
- Placement (09/04): where cars are stopped, not where they pass at 45 mph. Neighborhood exits and entrances, stop signs, intersections, shopping center and home-improvement store entrances, gas station entrances. Stakes in grass, or a 2-foot zip tie through the sign around a stop sign pole. Steve concedes they get pulled; buy many and "drip" them out over time.
- Target areas: highest-income USPS EDDM routes (tactic 9). Navigate by a landmark in the route.

B. Branded customer-yard signs (09/03)
- Copy: service on top, brand, phone plus QR. Must look good. 2-color, 3-color or full-color 18x24; the transcript quotes roughly $4.34 to $7.45 each depending on colors (speech-to-text of the prices is garbled). 24-inch stakes. Design in the vendor editor, Canva, or Fiverr ($10, $20, $55 examples, 2-day turnaround; give the designer the vendor's color limit and 18x24 size).
- Script (for short jobs): "Want $10 or $15 off your invoice? I'll give you that if I can put this sign in your yard until the next time you mow, a week or two." Steve claims about 90% say yes. Leverages social proof: the neighbors just saw a service at the Smiths'.

QR code (09/05, 11/01): qr-code-generator.com, point at the booking/quote page (not the homepage). Free basic; paid adds logo and frame text. Download the image once and reuse on signs, A-frames, hangers, shirts.

Tracking as taught: the forwarding number identifies sign calls; the QR goes to a booking page. Nothing more formal.

### 3. A-frame sidewalk signs - 11/01, 11/02, 11/03
- Vendor: UZ Marketing (link `lddy.no/1jpqw`), category Blanks and Hardware, Sidewalk Sign A-Frame, largest size 24x36, same design both sides, buy 2 if possible (one by the truck, one at the neighborhood entrance). Price not stated in the transcript.
- Design: solid brand-color background (skip their patterns), logo centered, "[service] in progress", bottom half is the quote call-out: phone, website, QR to the quote page, text like "Ask us for a free quote". Optionally "5-star by Google".
- Placement (11/02): when parked at the curb at the job, A-frame about 2 feet in front of the truck's nose and about 2 feet out, roughly in line with the driver-side wheel, half into the roadway "so traffic slows". Second one the same way behind the truck. Not on a 45 mph road. Intent: slow residential traffic so people photograph it or scan the QR.
- Rationale (11/03): looks like a "men at work" safety sign but invites neighbors to talk to the crew; people will not bother a contractor unless explicitly invited. Steve claims HSA Pro members get 3 to 5+ jobs per week from A-frames and rates it above a vehicle wrap. Do it at every job.
- Tracking: QR scans only.

### 4. Door hangers - 12/01 to 12/05
- Rule (12/01): do not start unless it is built into SOP at every job. 15 hangers to the nearest 15 neighbors per job (10 to 20 is fine; door-knock first is optional, Steve never did). His math: 18 jobs/week x 15 = 270/week, about 1,080/month. Claimed return 2 to 3%. Compounding: 5 months at 1,000/month = 5,000 homes holding your piece; many call weeks later. Bonus: handwritten note on the back (sticky note, or leave blank space).
- Required content (12/02): brand, contact info, an invitation to get a free quote or estimate, and an offer that expires. Print "offer expires by ____" and hand-write the date with a Sharpie (one print run serves any period). Example back-side line: "Take an additional 5% off when you present this door hanger by ____." Leave room for a personal note: "This is Steve with Blake's Roofing, I was just doing the Smiths' roof down the road and wanted to introduce myself."
- Vendor and cost: UZ Marketing, 1,000 for $210 ($0.21 each); order 1,000 to 2,500; use their templates plus your logo ("browse designs" in the editor); keep the die-cut hole area clear. Alternatives: Canva (about $9 per month), download PNG/JPG; or Fiverr (insist on the vector/editable file, both sides, pay a little more for speed).
- Evidence (12/05): a Pro member closed a $2,576 quote from handwritten hangers placed a month earlier.
- Tracking as taught: the expiring date; calls attributed ad hoc. No dedicated numbers.

### 5. Gift card referral campaign - 13/01 to 13/04
- Mechanic (13/01): at job completion, hand the customer 3 branded "gift cards" (a $100-off card to the business, valid through a stated end date) and write the customer's name in Sharpie on the back ("gifted by ____"). Each time a card is redeemed on a booked job, the original customer gets a real reward. His examples: $50 or $100 steakhouse card (his average ticket is $3,300; his customers are $5M homes). Reward must be worth redeeming and fit the demographic: Chick-fil-A, Starbucks, Red Robin, ice cream, or a local small-business collaboration for a lower-price trade. A hardware-store card is too weak.
- Rules (13/02, 13/03): the named person cannot use their own card; redemptions are matched by the written name; give more only after one of the first three is redeemed (scarcity: "if you give out 100 the perceived value disappears", never hand out ten); process rewards once a week. Cost is incurred only on a booked job, so it is a pure CAC line.
- Assets: pre-built Canva templates for the card (front/back with "gifted by") and a one-sided glossy FAQ sheet ("What are these cards? What's in it for me? How do you track redemptions? Can I have more?"). Premium glossy thick stock so it feels like a gift card. Order 250 to 500 (about $0.37 each at 100; at 50 jobs/month x 3 cards you use 150/month). Canva premium about $15/month for logo edits, or other card templates (13/04).
- Tracking: the name written on the card; weekly redemption processing.

### 6. Facebook / Instagram ads (starter, basic setup only) - 06/01, 06/02
Steve withholds his real strategy (creative, scripts, bidding) for the paid PRO tier; the starter lesson is mechanics only.
Steps (06/02): Business Suite > Ads Manager > Create > objective Leads > Manual leads campaign. One ad set, no Advantage campaign budget, no A/B test, no special category. Conversion location: Instant forms (keeps leads on Facebook, easy to attribute; a dedicated landing page used only by the ad also works; do not send ads to the main homepage because you cannot attribute). Daily budget set by you, no end date. Location: city plus about 10 miles, not into areas you will not serve. Switch from Advantage+ audience to "original" audience (Advantage+ drifts outside the area). Minimum age 30 for home services. No interests or placements needed. Creative: video or image; he uses ChatGPT (about $19/month) to draft primary text, headline and description, and turns off Meta's AI text variations; make several variants. Duplicate the ad and change one thing at a time. Form fields: full name, email, phone (more questions = more friction; name + phone alone attracts junk). Add a "call now" button after submit. Publish; review can take about 2 hours.
Missing from the starter: creative strategy, scripts, bidding, budgets, results.

### 7. Google Business Profile (GBP) - 07/01, 07/03, 07/04, 07/05 (duplicate in 04/14)
- Why (07/01, 07/03): the Map Pack sits above organic results; ads (LSA green badge pays per call, search ads pay per click) sit above that. A listing with a pin (address shown) ranks better than service-area-only; proximity matters. Steve claims the free listing produced 145 calls and 970 site visits in two months for a seasonal business, about $91.8K of revenue at a 1-in-6 call close and $3,800 ticket (07/04).
- Setup (07/01): business.google.com > add business; name exactly as on state and IRS documents; he says to answer "No" to customers visiting if you work from home (but then there is no pin; he rents a virtual mailbox at Regus to get one); list service areas; hours 5am to 10pm, 7 days, only if you will actually answer (not 24 hours); add photos; skip ad upsells and the Admin console/custom domain pitch. Verification by postcard, video or other; plan on 5 to 10 business days or weeks.
- Ranking signals he emphasizes (07/03, 07/05): review volume and recency, respond to every review, photo reviews, keywords in reviews/Q&A/products and services (add each service as a product/service). He says review location affects where you rank locally (08/02).
- Rank measurement (07/05): SEMrush Local > Map Rank Tracker, add campaign (business + address), enter keywords (ask ChatGPT for the highest-searched variants), grid 9x9 or 11x11, 10 to 15 mile radius, monthly schedule; about $10 per month per campaign. Each dot is a geo-located search showing your rank there; red to yellow to green shows progress. Fix gaps by adding the missing keyword in listing content and reviews.

### 8. Local Services Ads (LSA) and Yelp - 07/02 (04/16 identical), 04/13
- LSA (07/02): pay per call (or per message; he turns messaging off because you pay for ghosts). Requirements: Google Business Profile, proof of insurance, personal background check, customer reviews, budget/billing, job types that match your services, service area by ZIP. Settings he uses: manual bidding to start (options shown $60 to $105 per call), weekly budget set to what you can stomach (math: $50 per call and $500 per week = about 10 calls), direct business search on (so a competitor is not first when someone searches your name). Verdict: works or fails by trade. For junk removal in Phoenix he paid $60 to $90 per call at a roughly $300 ticket, closing 1 of 2, so $120 to $180 per job, and shut it off. Test it yourself if the economics can work.
- Yelp (04/13): claim or add the business via Yelp for Business; get 5 to 10 reviews "just to say you have them"; do not advertise on Yelp, there are better places to spend.

### 9. Target the wealthiest areas (EDDM) - 04/08
USPS EDDM route tool (`eddm.usps.com/eddm/select-routes.htm`): enter ZIPs, open the Table view, sort routes by average household income, tick routes at $100K+ (his example $122K to $165K), check residence counts (apartments distort), then locate the route on the map, pick a landmark, navigate there for yard signs, door knocking and hangers, and use the same ZIPs for Google and Facebook geo targeting.

## Part 2: Got Moles fit

Assumptions from the brief: about $90K/month, TMCP $100/$125/$150 per month by acreage, Quick Fix $450/$500/$600, 2027 target needs residential TMCP +25%, winter/spring selling, Jan-Mar Quick Fix winback, 1.5 commercial accounts per month. Mole activity peaks in fall and spring rain; hills cluster by neighborhood and moles cross lot lines, so neighbors of an active job are the best prospect list this business has. Today is 2026-10-29, inside the fall peak.

Biggest structural difference from the course: Steve's trades do one job per customer, a visible 2-4 hour event. Got Moles has many short recurring visits and a smaller number of new starts. Neighborhood tactics should fire on new starts (first visit, Quick Fix start, TMCP enrollment, first catch), not on every recurring stop, or crews will over-saturate the same streets and labor cost becomes real.

### Tactic by tactic

**Door hangers (do first).**
- Trigger: first visit of each new Quick Fix or TMCP start, and the first visit where a mole is caught (the neighbors' best proof). Not every recurring visit. Tech walks the block and hangs on the nearest 10 to 15 homes; door handle only, never in a mailbox (federal postal rule).
- Copy (Spencer approves; passes the claims gate): headline "Chemical-free mole control, now serving your street", subhead "Safe for pets and children", generic line "We're working in your neighborhood this week". Never name or point at a neighbor's house or say they have moles (privacy). Include phone (CallRail tracking number), QR to the quote page, "283+ five-star Google reviews" (claims-gate figure; re-measure before print), "Founded 2017" if age is mentioned. Offer line with a hand-written expiry date (course technique), e.g. waived setup fee or a dollar-off on first-month TMCP, set by Spencer. Handwritten line on the back from the tech: first name plus "I was just treating a lawn down the road." Nothing about how moles are caught.
- Cost, ESTIMATE: 1,000 hangers at about $0.21 (course price) = $210, plus design free in Canva or $10 to $55 on Fiverr. Labor about 8 to 10 minutes per 15 hangers, about $3 to $5 of tech time per start. At 40 qualifying starts per month (assumed), about 600 hangers: about $130 print plus about $150 labor per month.
- Expected result, ESTIMATE: course claims 2 to 3% return. Plan on 0.5 to 1% calls for a service people do not need yet: 3 to 6 calls per month at 600 hangers, 1 to 3 booked jobs, plus some late calls over the following 1 to 3 months. One booked TMCP covers months of print cost. Judge after 90 days and about 2,000 hangers.
- Tracking (course gap, so add): unique CallRail number and QR path for hangers; Jobber "how did you hear" value "Door hanger"; log the start address for each drop so a call can be tied to a street.
- Seasonality: heavy in fall (now) and March to May; in winter lean on the winback/TMCP offer.
- Neighbor targeting: Jobber start dates are the work list. A weekly batch for the previous week's new starts is the fallback when a tech forgot.

**Gift card referral (do second).**
- Adapt: give each new TMCP enrollee, and each Quick Fix customer at the visit with the first catch, 2 cards (not 3). Card text: "A gift from [name]: [dollar-off] first mole control service", expiry date, usable only by someone other than the named giver, residential only. Reward to the giver on the booked job: $25 to $50 (local coffee/restaurant card or a TMCP bill credit). Reward never depends on a review.
- Reuse: the Canva FAQ sheet idea (what is this, what is in it for me, how do we track it, can I have more) and the scarcity rule (replacement cards only after a first redemption).
- Cost, ESTIMATE: 250 cards at about $0.40 = $100; reward $25 to $50 only per booked job. Redemption for a service customers already pay monthly: assume 10 to 20% of givers produce one booked neighbor within 90 days.
- Tracking: Jobber referral source plus the giver's name; process weekly.
- Timing: hand over after the first catch (customer is happiest), and in Jan to Mar winback calls (Quick Fix customers become referrers while being offered TMCP).

**Google Business Profile and reviews (do third).**
- Already 3 listings, 289 total / 283 five-star. Course adds: reply to every review, ask for photo reviews, keep the services list current with specific terms, measure map position by grid.
- Review requests must follow Google policy: ask everyone, never reward or discount for a review, never screen for happy customers only. A tech-with-customer selfie is fine as a request, not as a condition for anything.
- Map-rank grid (SEMrush Local Map Rank Tracker or equivalent): 11x11 grid, 10 to 15 miles around each of the 3 locations, monthly. Keywords such as "mole control near me", "mole removal [city]", "get rid of moles". Cost, ESTIMATE: about $10 per month per campaign, about $30/month.
- Hours: list only hours the phone is answered.
- Owner: this seat (reviews and reputation). Any change to got-moles.com pages or schema goes to Roy.
- Expected result, ESTIMATE: incremental and slow. The course's "145 calls in 2 months" is a seasonal consumer business with a $3,800 ticket and does not transfer. Treat GBP as defense and compounding, not a quick win.

**Customer-yard branded sign (small pilot).**
- 50 signs, 18x24, 24-inch stakes, 3 color: "Chemical-free mole control / Got Moles / phone + QR". ESTIMATE $4.50 to $6 each = $225 to $300 plus design. Ask the customer ("could we leave this on your lawn for a week or two?"), no discount or a small one.
- Only where visible from the street and the lawn is already being treated. Pick up at the next visit (recurring visits make retrieval free), better than the course's "until you mow".
- Expected result, ESTIMATE: a few calls per 50 signs; mostly brand support. Track with a separate CallRail number.

**LSA (conditional).**
- Check status in `ops-got-moles-ads` first. If not running: unit economics look better than the course's junk removal case. ESTIMATE: at $60 to $90 per call and a 1-in-2 close, $120 to $180 per new customer, against TMCP at $100 to $150 per month or Quick Fix at $450 to $600. Start with a weekly cap Spencer sets, manual bidding at the lowest option, messaging off, direct business search on, ZIP-based service area.
- Constraint: per the claims gate, moles must be classed under rodent in LSA (re-verify against the live setup); wildlife wording can take LSA dark silently. Insurance proof and background check required. Spencer approves any spend; no cron may spend or bid.

**Yelp.** Claim and complete the listing (tier-1 local asset per GOT-MOLES.md). The course says get 5 to 10 reviews; do not solicit through a filter or incentive (Yelp itself discourages asking for reviews). No ad spend.

**EDDM targeting.** Mole damage tracks lawn size, irrigation and soil, not income. Use EDDM to find residential routes with high single-family counts and larger lots inside the four territories, cross-checked against Jobber customer density. Income is a secondary sort at most (larger lots do pay the $125 to $150 TMCP tiers).

**Facebook/IG.** Steps are mechanical and match `ops-meta-ads`; defer there. Course choices worth keeping: instant form with name, email, phone, plus property type and approximate acreage (needed at intake; commercial and over 5 acres never get a price); radius limited to served territories; 35+ rather than 30+ (homeowners); one change per test; duplicate-and-swap workflow; turn off Meta's automatic text variations. Do not copy the ChatGPT prompt "next 10 people" urgency (false scarcity).

### Skip and why
- Unbranded roadside signs with a Google Voice number: right-of-way sign rules in most Washington cities and counties (ESTIMATE, verify locally; assume unpermitted signs are removable and can be fined); the course admits they get pulled and draw complaints; the anonymous number hides who is calling the customer; one angry post would hit a 5.0 rating across 3 listings.
- A-frame as taught: a sign placed in the roadway dressed up as a "men at work" safety sign is misleading and a traffic/liability risk, and mole visits run 15 to 30 minutes, so there is no 3-4 hour event to exploit. Low-risk substitute: the lawn sign pilot above, honest wording, no street placement.
- Google Voice (personal account): use CallRail tracking numbers for per-source attribution.
- SnipeyLead: replace with CallRail missed-call text-back, Jobber request alerts and a 5-minute callback rule; confirm what exists before buying. Verify the "Harvard" figures before quoting them.
- 5% price-raise loop: belongs to `acc-cfo`.
- Door knocking, HSA PRO: not needed.

### Conflicts with Got Moles rules
1. Posture A: no trap, kill, bait, body-gripping, lethal, spike or harpoon wording on signs, hangers, QR landing pages or ad copy. Lead with chemical-free and safe, not trapping.
2. I-713: never imply legal compliance or approval on any piece.
3. Claims: 283+ five-star reviews (re-measure before print), "Founded 2017", Spencer's 15+ years only with clarification; no "#1"/"best"; no client count; US English.
4. Reviews: gift card rewards go to referrers for booked jobs only, never conditioned on a review; no discounts for reviews; no gating. The $10-15 yard-sign discount is a separate offer; keep it distinct from any review ask. Skip false-urgency prompts in ad copy.
5. Pricing: hangers, cards and signs must not state commercial or over-5-acre pricing; residential offers stay inside published tiers and the $150 setup fee / no-catch guarantee wording (not "money back"); every discount needs Spencer's approval before print. Bid requests route to Cory (Tavis when back), not Spencer.
6. Disposal: dead moles are double-bagged into the customer's garbage can; nothing says "hauled away".
7. Privacy: do not tell neighbors that a specific house has moles.
8. Entity: platform verifications (Meta, LSA, registrations) use Rainier Power Wash LLC; customers read "Got Moles".
9. Lanes: website, landing pages and SEO go through Roy; this seat briefs him. Nothing here spends, bids, posts or sends without Spencer's approval, and no cron may do those things.
10. Course statistics (HBR, 2 to 3% return, 3 to 5 jobs per week from A-frames, $91K from GBP) are single-source claims from a coach selling a paid tier; do not use them as targets.
