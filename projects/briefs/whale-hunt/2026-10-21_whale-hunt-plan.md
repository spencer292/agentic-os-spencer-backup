## What a whale is

One yes has to open many properties or a large recurring contract. A single HOA or a single homeowner never qualifies, however friendly.

A whale meets all four:

1. **Portfolio or scale.** Controls or influences 10+ properties, or 50+ acres of maintained turf, in King, Pierce or Thurston.
2. **Pain is plausible.** Turf they're judged on: greens, fields, HOA common areas, campus lawns, cemeteries.
3. **Budget authority, or one step from it.** The person signs, or they're the person the signer listens to (a superintendent, a branch manager, a portfolio manager).
4. **Reachable.** There's a warm path: a mutual connection, an existing client in the same firm, a shared association, or a Got Moles customer next to their property.

Score each candidate 1–5 on **Size, Pain, Authority, Access**. Top 10 by total go in. Aim for a mix so one bad season in one segment doesn't empty the board: roughly 3 property-management firms, 2 golf operators, 2 landscape firms, 2 parks/sports venues, 1 commercial real estate.

**Landscape companies are partners, not buyers** (existing rule). The ask is "send me your mole callbacks", never a pitch to buy.

## The dossier (one per whale)

Built by me before week 1 and kept current. Public and professional information only: role, career, posts, associations, causes they post about. No home addresses, no family digging. It reads as creepy if they ever notice, and they'd notice at lunch.

- **The person:** role, tenure, previous jobs, school, associations and boards, what they post about, anything you share (Army, local sports, a hometown, fishing).
- **The organization:** properties in our area, acreage, who maintains their turf now, current pest vendor if findable, how they buy (board approval, RFP, public bid cycle, fiscal year).
- **Our footprint:** Jobber customers on or next to their properties. This is the street anchor that turned Patrick Oien in under a minute.
- **Warm paths:** mutual connections, colleagues who are already clients, shared associations.
- **Gives:** three things we could offer that cost them nothing and aren't free work: an intro, local information, a mention, an invite.
- **Touch log:** every touch, channel, date, response.

## How we talk to them: give, give, give, lunch

The goal is a lunch, then a friendship. Not a sale. Every touch has to be worth receiving on its own, with no pitch attached.

**Weekly touch menu (rotate channels; never the same channel two weeks running):**

| Channel | Examples |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn engagement | A real comment on their post or their company's post |
| LinkedIn DM | Short, casual, in your voice. Something you saw that made you think of them |
| Email | Something useful: a local mole-pressure note for their area, a relevant article |
| Introduction | Connect them to someone who helps *them* (a striper, an irrigation guy, a client in their market) |
| Mail | Handwritten card. Holiday card in December |
| In person | Drop by their office or clubhouse with coffee; say hi at an association event |
| The ask | Lunch, your treat, no agenda |

**The 10-week arc:**

| Weeks | Aim |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Get noticed. Engagement, a useful note, a connect if we're not already |
| 3 | First lunch ask, casual: "I'd like to buy you lunch, no pitch, just like knowing the people in this industry" |
| 4–6 | Keep giving. One intro, one in-person touch if possible |
| 7 | Second lunch ask, different framing (coffee near their office, or a meetup at an event) |
| 8–10 | Light touches, holiday card. Week 10 is a clean, friendly close: "Door's open anytime" |

**Graduation:** lunch held → they move to a monthly "friend" track and their slot opens for the next whale.
**Fall-off:** no meeting after 10 weeks → back into normal quarterly nurture, slot refilled from the bench. No hard feelings, no burned bridge.

**Rules that carry over from the LinkedIn campaign:**
- Nothing goes out without your approval.
- No commitments of staff, time or free work inside a message unless you've approved that commitment as a separate decision (rule from 2026-09-10).
- Messages are drafted in your own DM voice (casual, warm), not the brand voice.
- Claims stay inside the guardrails (283+ five-star reviews; no "WA's #1"; no I-713 claims).
