# LinkedIn Campaign — Game Plan

**Date:** 2026-08-21 (Friday)
**Skill:** `mkt-linkedin-nurture`
**Cadence:** two supervised sessions per week — Monday and Friday
**Account:** Sales Navigator Core free trial, decision point at day ~25

---

## The one-line strategy

Work the warm network before working strangers. Four of Spencer's own connections are the shared
link into most of the target market, and using them costs nothing against any LinkedIn limit.
Cold outreach is what we do *after* that route is exhausted, not instead of it.

## Session 1 — Monday 2026-08-24 (~75 min, longer than normal)

The only heavy session. Three things happen once and never again.

**Setup (my work, ~30 min):**
- Build `pipeline.json` and seed it from the July research — 20 researched prospects, 14 drafted
  message pairs, none contacted. Re-verify each person is still in the same role before anything
  goes out; the research is six weeks old.
- Read the account's real limits: invite allowance, note allowance, and whether Sales Nav's
  interface changes the extraction technique.
- Decide the search split: standard people search for finding people, Sales Nav for lead lists
  and job-change alerts. I'll confirm after seeing the interface.

**The warm-hub play (this session's actual output):**

Four people keep appearing as the mutual connection across the whole target market — **Patrick
LaCroix** (the link to roughly eight of the HOA managers found), **Nick Granberg**, **Brandon
Sechrist**, **Mark Pyrah**. They're already 1st-degree connections, so messaging them spends no
invite, no note, and no search against the monthly limit.

I draft four individual messages — not a pitch, an ask: who in your network deals with mole
damage, and would you mind an introduction. You approve, we send. One warm introduction into a
management company is worth more than a dozen cold requests, and it's how the referral engine is
supposed to work anyway.

**Then the first cold batch:** 8–10 HOA / community association managers, warm-routed where
possible. Comment on their recent posts where they have any; invites go out Wednesday, two days
behind the engagement.

**Session 1 output:** 4 hub asks sent · 8–10 prospects approved and queued · pipeline live.

## Session 2 — Friday 2026-08-28 (~60 min, and this is the normal shape)

- Work the follow-up queue first: who accepted, who replied, what the hubs said
- Send follow-ups to anyone who accepted (approval gate as always)
- New batch: 10–12 prospects, HOA managers again — go deep on one segment before adding another
- Engagement pass: comment on 5–8 prospect posts
- Update pipeline, log the session

**Every session after this follows the same shape.** Follow-ups first, new batch second,
engagement throughout.

## Four-week ramp

| Week | Sessions | Segment focus | New prospects | Cumulative |
|------|----------|---------------|---------------|------------|
| 1 (Aug 24, 28) | 2 | HOA / community association managers | ~20 | 20 |
| 2 (Aug 31, Sep 4) | 2 | HOA + commercial property management | ~24 | 44 |
| 3 (Sep 7, 11) | 2 | Golf superintendents (cold — different message shape) | ~24 | 68 |
| 4 (Sep 14, 18) | 2 | Whichever segment is replying best | ~24 | 92 |

Roughly 24 invites a week against a ~100/week cap — a quarter of the ceiling, deliberately. The
constraint on this campaign is how many messages can be written properly, not how many LinkedIn
allows.

Segment order is not arbitrary. HOA managers first because they're the best fit *and* the warmest
— highest chance of an early win, which is what tells us the campaign works. Golf superintendents
are held to week 3 because they're cold with no mutual connections, so they test the messaging
without a warm route propping it up.

## What we measure

Tracked in `pipeline.json`, reviewed every session, and compared against the 2026 benchmarks in
the skill's `references/current-benchmarks.md`:

| Metric | Benchmark | Our target |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Connection acceptance | 28–30% average | 40%+ (we're warm-routed and hand-researched) |
| Reply rate once connected | ~10.4% average | 20%+ |
| Replies → call | — | the number that actually matters |

Also running as a live experiment: **message length.** The 2026 guides say keep everything under
300 characters; our July drafts run 600–900 and are genuinely good. We run both and let the reply
data settle it within a few sessions.

**Our own numbers replace every published benchmark once we have thirty-odd outcomes** — roughly
week 3.

## Decision points

**Day ~25 (around Sep 15) — keep Sales Navigator or drop to free.**
Keep it if job-change alerts have produced real prospects, or if the warm-route pipeline needs
more than five noted invites a month. Drop it if the campaign is working fine on standard search
— nothing in the method depends on the paid tier. One signed HOA management contract covers a
year of it several times over, so this is a "does the pipeline work" question, not a cost one.

**End of week 2 — is the message landing?**
If acceptance is under 25% the message or the targeting is wrong, and we change it rather than
sending more of it. If nobody replies after accepting, the follow-up is the problem, not the
invite.

**End of week 4 — does this deserve to continue?**
By then there should be a call booked or a clear reason there isn't.

## What could stall this, and how it gets caught

| Risk | How it shows up | Response |
|------|-----------------|----------|
| Commercial use limit (~300 searches/mo on free) | Results throttle to 3 per query, no warning | Session logs track search count; Sales Nav trial removes it during the trial anyway |
| Account restriction | Warning banner or captcha | Stop everything immediately, tell Spencer, no clicking through |
| Prospects go stale | July research is 6 weeks old | Re-verify role before contacting, every time |
| The campaign quietly does nothing | Weeks pass, no replies | The week-2 gate exists precisely for this |
| Sessions don't happen | Life | It's twice a week for an hour. If Monday slips, one session a week still works — the pipeline holds state |

---

## What this campaign is not

It doesn't run in the background. Nothing sends without approval. It doesn't produce residential
leads — those come through CallRail and Jobber. And it isn't a volume play: 24 invites a week is
a deliberate choice, because the entire advantage here is that each message was written for the
person receiving it.
